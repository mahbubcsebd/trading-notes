# Notes Library: Notes, Cheat Sheets and Logs That Really Help

When you finish reading, the book goes on the shelf, the video goes into history, and what you learned sinks deep in the mind. A few months later you cannot find anything. This chapter is a way to keep what you learned so that you can **find it and use it**.

## What is a notes library

**A notes library is a collection of small, testable notes you make yourself: claims from books and videos, useful formulas, a log of your own mistakes and a weekly review.**

This chapter answers one question: **"How do I use what I learned, without forgetting it, and after checking it?"**

## Why learn this now

- **Most of what you learn is lost.** If you do not write it down, little is remembered a week later.
- **A claim and proof are different.** You test the claims of books and videos yourself, and your [Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/00-overview) skill is useful here.
- **If you know the cost of mistakes, improvement is faster.** The results of the [Review](/en/learn/17-case-studies-trade-reviews/00-overview) are collected here.
- **This is the last chapter of the course.** From here you walk your own path.

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Write the claims of books and videos** in a testable template.
- **Recognize an educator's warning signs.**
- **Build a one-page cheat sheet.**
- **Keep a mistakes log** and work out the cost of mistakes in R.
- **Read the pattern** from a **monthly collection**.

## How the lessons join together

A cycle of one month:

1. **Learn.** One claim from a book or video (lessons 1, 2).
2. **Test.** A small backtest on your own data.
3. **At hand.** The useful formulas on a cheat sheet (lesson 3).
4. **Correct.** The most expensive one from the mistakes log (lesson 4).
5. **At the end of the month.** Four weeks side by side, one monthly decision (lesson 5).

- After reading lessons 1 and 2, make one note from each; use it at once.
- Print the cheat sheet of lesson 3 only once, but with your own numbers.
- Lessons 4 and 5 are a regular habit: once a week, once a month.
