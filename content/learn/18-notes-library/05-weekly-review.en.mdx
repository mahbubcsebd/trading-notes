# Weekly Review Collection: Reading Four Weeks Together

One week's review is a picture. Four weeks side by side make a **film**: pattern, change, progress. This lesson is the method of collecting the reviews and reading them at the end of the month.

## What you will be able to do

- **Keep the reviews together in one place.**
- **Write a monthly summary.**
- **Put a bad week in context.**

## Why it matters

- **A pattern cannot be seen in one week.** In four weeks the same mistake or the same strength catches the eye.
- **Checking whether the goal worked.** Whether each week's goal gives a result the next week.
- **The right time to change the plan.** [Plan v2](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/05-building-a-plan) rests on monthly data.

Prerequisites: [Weekly Review Template](/en/learn/17-case-studies-trade-reviews/03-weekly-review-template) and [Mistakes Log](/en/learn/18-notes-library/04-mistakes-log).

## What is the collection

**A weekly review collection is keeping the one-page review of each week in one place by date, so that they can be read side by side at the end of the month.**

| Week | Net R | Rules followed % | Most expensive tag | Goal                 | Goal met? |
| :--- | :---- | :--------------- | :----------------- | :------------------- | :-------- |
| 1    | +3    | 83%              | Moved stop         | Do not move the stop | Partly    |
| 2    | −1    | 90%              | FOMO               | Wait for the retest  | Yes       |
| 3    | +2    | 92%              | Revenge            | Stop after 2 losses  | Yes       |
| 4    | +4    | 100%             | None               | The full checklist   | Yes       |

(Imaginary numbers.) Six columns are enough. With more, it won't get read at the end of the month.

## Step by step: the monthly summary

<Steps>

### Step 1: Put the four reviews side by side

One row per week, like the table above.

### Step 2: Look for the trend

Is the rules-followed % rising? Is the expensive tag changing (a good sign: one problem solved and the next one coming up)?

### Step 3: The result of the goals

How many were met, partly met, not met. If not met, was the goal too big?

### Step 4: One monthly decision

Either the plan stays unchanged, or one change is proposed (with a checking condition).

### Step 5: One goal for next month

Bigger than a weekly goal, in one sentence.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Data:** the table above.
- **Lesson read:** the rules-followed % went from 83 to 100, and net R rose too. The expensive tag changed every week, which means old mistakes are being fixed.
- **Decision:** the plan unchanged; goal: hold 100% rule-following next month.
- **Lesson:** the trend showed up in numbers; there was no need to panic over one red week.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** seeing the -1R of the second week, the trader changed the whole plan and took up another strategy.
- **Result:** in the next three weeks no pattern could be caught, because the rules were different every week.
- **Why it failed:** a decision from one week, with no monthly collection. Yet the net was positive that month.
- **Lesson:** the decision to change the plan belongs in the monthly summary, not in the emotion of one week.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens           | What to do                                         |
| :--------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| Reviews scattered                  | Separate notebooks/files | One spreadsheet, one row per week                  |
| Changing the plan after a bad week | Disappointment           | Wait until the end of the month                    |
| A goal that is too big             | Ambition                 | The weekly goal small, just one                    |
| Skipping the monthly summary       | No time                  | Put the last Saturday of the month in the calendar |
| Not matching goal and result       | Stopping once written    | A "met?" column the next week                      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Once every three months, read the 12 weekly rows side by side. The pattern of
  the year, such as which month has more mistakes, is caught then.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Progress is not a straight line. If the rules-followed % ever falls, it is not
  a failure; look for the reason (sleep, work pressure).
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the weekly reviews are incomplete:** the base of the monthly summary is weak.
- **If the number of trades is very small:** even in four weeks the pattern is not clear.
- **If you can't find time because of other work:** if even half an hour is not there, make the review template smaller still.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Four weeks side by side show the pattern.
- Six columns: net R, rules followed %, expensive tag, goal, goal met.
- See one bad week in context in the month.
- Change the plan in the monthly summary.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Build a six-column table for the next four weeks, and fill in one row every Sunday. At the end of the month, write the trend analysis.
