# Video Notes: Taking Notes While Checking the Educator

Watching a video is easy: a lovely chart, a confident voice, "see how 120 pips!" But what is not shown: the losing trades, the hundreds of setups that did not work, and who is making this video and for what purpose. In this lesson you will learn to separate learning from a video and being fooled by an advertisement.

## What you will be able to do

- **Pull claims out and take notes** from a video.
- **Check an educator** with a **warning-sign list**.
- **Recognize the trap** of "picked examples".

## Why it matters

- **Nice examples can be picked.** With picked winning charts, any setup can be made to look good.
- **Your money is at risk.** Following wrong teaching means a direct loss.
- **An educator who does not admit mistakes is dangerous.** You cannot check their results.

Prerequisites: [Book Notes](/en/learn/18-notes-library/01-book-notes) and [How to Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest).

## What is a video note

**A video note is writing together the video's claim, the proof shown, the proof not shown and your way of testing it.**

| Column      | What to write                                     |
| :---------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| Video, time | the title, at which minute the claim is           |
| Claim       | what was said, in your own words                  |
| Proof shown | how many examples, from what period, how picked?  |
| Not shown   | losing trades? the total number of trades? costs? |
| My test     | how will I check it on my own data?               |

## The warning-sign list (checking the educator)

The more "yes" answers to the questions below, the more doubt:

| Sign                                                     | Why to be careful                |
| :------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Are "sure profit", "risk-free", "works 100%" being said? | There is no certainty in trading |
| Only winning examples, no losing ones?                   | Selection bias                   |
| Is the total number of trades and the period missing?    | The result cannot be checked     |
| A profit screenshot, but no costs or drawdown?           | Half the picture                 |
| Pressure to buy a signal/robot with money?               | Conflict of interest             |
| Hurry ("join today, the offer ends!")                    | A sales tactic                   |
| No talk about their own risk and stop?                   | Teaching without risk management |

One sign does not mean bad; be careful when several match together.

## Step by step: notes from a video

<Steps>

### Step 1: A question before watching

"Which problem am I looking for an answer to?" With no specific question you just wander.

### Step 2: Pull out claims

Pause while watching and write in the template. Write the time.

### Step 3: Judge the proof

How many examples? How picked? Are there losing trades? How many trades in total?

### Step 4: Match the warning-sign list

Count the signs. If there are many, step away without even a small test.

### Step 5: Your own backtest

Write the teacher's rules and run them on 20 old setups. Work out the [Metrics](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/02-performance-metrics-forward-testing) yourself.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Video:** an educator is showing a breakout setup. The claim: "entry on the retest is safer."
- **Checking:** they showed two winning and two losing examples, and gave a summary of 30 trades in total, with costs.
- **My test:** log 20 old breakouts; compare with and without the retest.
- **Verdict:** partly supported on my data. Added to the plan as a test.
- **Lesson:** believable because of the losing examples and the summary; still, my own test is not skipped.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** the trader saw "9 of 10 signals on this indicator succeed." They took risk straight live, without matching any rules or data.
- **Result:** in the first month the result was far below the claim.
- **Why it failed:** the 10 examples in the video were picked, and there was no count of total trades or costs. No warning-sign list was matched.
- **Lesson:** a video is a claim, not proof. Proof is your own test.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Why it happens                  | What to do                                 |
| :------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------- |
| Believing a nice chart                      | It looks good                   | Ask for losing trades and the total number |
| Not writing down the claim                  | Thinking you'll remember        | Write it in the template with the time     |
| Following many educators in one week        | Not able to know which is right | One educator, one test                     |
| Buying a signal/course first, testing later | FOMO and offer pressure         | Test it yourself first, decide after       |
| Treating a result screenshot as proof       | It seems like easy proof        | Ask for a record that can be checked       |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  While watching a video, always keep one question in mind: "what could have
  been proof against this, and why was it not shown?"
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The words "sure profit", "risk-free" or "guarantee" are always a reason to
  doubt. This is educational writing, not investment advice. Check independently
  before paying any money.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the video is only inspiration:** there is nothing to test; just take the attitude.
- **If you doubt everything:** learning stalls too. The balance: test, do not discard.
- **In a live session:** under the pressure of the time, your own checking gets dropped; test after the session.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A video = a claim; proof = your own test.
- Warning signs: certainty, only winning examples, no numbers, sales pressure.
- One educator, one claim, one test.
- Do not buy; test first.

</KeyTakeaways>
