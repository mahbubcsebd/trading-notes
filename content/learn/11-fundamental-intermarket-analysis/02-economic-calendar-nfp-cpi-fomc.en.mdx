# Economic Calendar: How to Read NFP, CPI and FOMC

On a Friday afternoon there is a nice setup, and suddenly one candle moves 40 pips and the stop is gone. The calendar had told you in advance. This lesson is about reading that calendar.

## What you will be able to do

- **Read** the calendar's **impact, forecast, previous and actual**.
- **Say briefly what NFP, CPI and FOMC are.**
- **Understand how price behaves before and after** a release.

## Why it matters

- **The biggest risk is known in advance.** The time is written on the calendar.
- **Spread and slippage rise.** [Spread widening](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening) is largest at the exact moment of the release.
- **Entering at the wrong time brings a pointless loss.** The calendar protects your plan.

Prerequisites: [Central Bank, Interest Rates and Inflation](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/01-central-bank-interest-rate-inflation).

## The columns of the calendar

**An economic calendar is a schedule of upcoming economic announcements, with the possible impact and the expectation written for each.**

| Column   | Meaning                                                | How to use it                              |
| :------- | :----------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| Time     | The exact time of the announcement (in your time zone) | Be alert in advance                        |
| Currency | The country the news is from                           | Match it to your pair                      |
| Impact   | Low / medium / high (shown by color)                   | "High" means a risk of a big shock         |
| Forecast | What analysts expect                                   | This is what the market is already holding |
| Previous | The earlier value                                      | The base for comparison                    |
| Actual   | The announced value                                    | Look at the gap from the forecast          |

Match the time zone. Check whether the calendar shows your broker's time zone or your own, or you will be alert at the wrong time.

## The three big announcements

| Name | Full form                     | What it measures                   | How often                                          |
| :--- | :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| NFP  | Non-Farm Payrolls             | New jobs outside farming in the US | Once a month                                       |
| CPI  | Consumer Price Index          | Consumer prices (inflation)        | Once a month                                       |
| FOMC | Federal Open Market Committee | The US interest rate decision      | A few times a year (schedule published in advance) |

Because these affect the USD, EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY and XAUUSD all move. Always check the exact date and time from your own calendar.

## Step by step: checking the calendar in the morning

<Steps>

### Step 1: Find today's "high impact" news

Look at all the news for both currencies of your pair.

### Step 2: Write down the times

Mark 30 minutes before and 30 minutes after the release. Decide the rules for this window in advance.

### Step 3: Decide what to do if a trade is open

Decide beforehand whether you will bring the stop closer, close half, or close everything.

### Step 4: Compare the forecast and the actual

If the gap is big, a big move is more likely.

### Step 5: Look at the structure that follows

Not the first spike. When the dust settles, look at the [structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** NFP marked on the calendar in the morning, no new trades from 30 minutes before the release.
- **Result:** on the release candle, price went about 40 pips down and reversed up. You were out, and no stop was hit.
- **Lesson:** following the calendar does not mean losing a chance. It means avoiding risk.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the range breaks down, and a sell on seeing the first spike.
- **Result:** after the spike, price ran fast the other way and the stop was hit. You had to leave before the direction changed.
- **Why it failed:** the first reaction can be a trap. Fast shocks came in both directions (the chart above).
- **Lesson:** do not enter on direction within a few minutes of a release.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Why it happens       | What to do                                  |
| :------------------------------------- | :------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| Trading without checking the calendar  | No habit             | Look for 2 minutes at the start of the day  |
| Taking the time in the wrong time zone | Settings not matched | Check the calendar's time zone              |
| Looking only at "high impact"          | Ignoring medium news | Look at all news for your pair              |
| Entering at the moment of release      | Fear of missing out  | Wait until the structure that follows comes |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write a small rule: "No new trade 30 minutes before and 15 minutes after
  high-impact news." Change the numbers to suit your style, but write them in
  advance.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  A forecast is not a sure result. Analysts get it wrong too, and the market
  moves on that gap.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When unexpected news comes:** sudden political or crisis news is not on the calendar.
- **If the calendar is in the wrong time zone:** the warning comes at the wrong time.
- **When several news items come one after another:** the market stays unsettled for a long time.
- **In a long-term trade:** a trend of years does not change on one piece of news alone.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Learn to read the impact, forecast and actual of the calendar.
- NFP, CPI and FOMC bring the biggest shocks to the USD.
- Quiet before the release, a spike and whipsaw at the moment, and a calm move after.
- Write the rules in advance: at which times there is no new trade.

</KeyTakeaways>
