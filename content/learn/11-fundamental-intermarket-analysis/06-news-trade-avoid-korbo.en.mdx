# News Trade: Trade It or Avoid It

At the moment of news the market is at its fastest, most expensive and most uncertain. Many new traders enter right then, because that is when the most "opportunity" is visible. This lesson says when it is better to stay out, and how to enter safely after the news.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain why news trading and catching the spike directly are risky.**
- **Write the before-and-after rules.**
- **Learn to enter** by **waiting for a retest** after the spike.

## Why it matters

- **Spread and slippage are at their highest.** The risk you calculated actually becomes bigger.
- **Wherever you place the stop, it may not survive.** The spike runs both ways.
- **Emotion is at its strongest.** Rules written earlier bring you back.

Prerequisites: [Economic Calendar](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/02-economic-calendar-nfp-cpi-fomc) and [Slippage and Spread](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening).

## Three paths

**News trading means deliberately taking or managing trades around high-impact news. There are three common paths.**

| Path               | What you do                                               | Risk                             |
| :----------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Avoid              | No new trades before or after the release                 | Missing a chance (the safest)    |
| Manage open trades | Stop closer, close half or close everything               | The stop can be hit in the spike |
| Enter later        | Once structure forms after the spike, enter on the retest | Medium, if you follow the rules  |

For beginners the first path is best. With experience, the third path can be practiced slowly.

## Step by step: rules for before and after news

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark the times on the calendar

Write down the times of high-impact news at the start of the day.

### Step 2: Set the window before

For example, no new trades from 30 minutes before the release. The number is up to your style, but written in advance.

### Step 3: Plan for open trades

If in profit, think about closing half or moving the stop to break-even ([Take profit and partial close](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/02-take-profit-partial-close)). The rule is set in advance.

### Step 4: Wait after the spike

Let a few candles pass. See whether price holds above or below some level.

### Step 5: Enter on the retest

When the [retest](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/01-breakout-retest-pullback-entry) comes, enter by the rules with the stop a little beyond the structure.

### Step 6: Skip it if the spread is not normal

If the spread is still wide, do not enter.

</Steps>

- **Set in advance:** not at the release, but entry when the retest comes. Risk 1%, stop 16 pips, target 32 pips.
- **Result:** after the spike, price held at the new level and retested, and the buy hit the target (1:2).
- **Lesson:** if you wait for the structure instead of chasing the spike, the risk is clear.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** a nice range like the earlier chart (news-skip), and a buy just before the release hoping for a breakout.
- **Result:** the release candle first goes up (showing profit), then quickly falls and goes past the stop. With slippage the real loss is bigger than planned.
- **Why it failed:** the problem was not the quality of the setup. It was the timing. No stop place would have survived this spike.
- **Lesson:** even a nice setup can be skipped before news. "The same setup will come again in a few minutes."

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Why it happens           | What to do                                   |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| Entering a breakout before the release          | Fear of missing a chance | Write the rule: out during the window before |
| Running right after seeing a spike              | FOMO                     | Wait a few candles                           |
| No plan for open trades                         | Forgetting the news      | Check the calendar in the morning            |
| Entering after news without checking the spread | Hurry                    | Check that the spread is normal              |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Make "is there news today" the first line of your checklist. Once it is a
  habit, it will be your cheapest risk control.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Execution around news differs by broker. Some fill late, and some widen the
  spread a lot. Look at the behavior on your own account with a small size.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When several news items come one after another:** it takes a long time to "let the dust settle".
- **If no clear structure forms after the spike:** you may not get an entry even after waiting, and that is right.
- **If the spread does not return to normal:** even the retest carries more risk.
- **With open trades on a small account:** one spike on news can cause a big loss, so close them beforehand.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- At the moment of news, spread, slippage and risk are at their highest.
- The safest path is to take no new trades before or after.
- If you want to enter, skip the spike and wait for the retest.
- Write the rules in advance. Do not decide in the moment.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Pick one high-impact news event for next week. In three lines, write the rule for the window before it, the plan if a trade is open, and the condition for entering on the retest. At the time of the news, watch the chart (without taking a trade) and check whether your rules held.

3. What is the benefit of entering on the retest?
4. What do you do if the spread is still wide after the news?

**1.** The spike runs fast in both directions, and there can be slippage.

**2.** Taking no new trades before or after the news.
