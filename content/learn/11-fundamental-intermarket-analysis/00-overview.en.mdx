# Fundamental and Intermarket Analysis: News and How Markets Relate

The chart tells you where price has gone. News and other markets tell you **why** it went, and where the next risk is. This chapter is not meant to make you a fortune teller. It is to give the chart a context.

## What are fundamental and intermarket analysis

**Fundamental analysis means understanding what drives a currency's value by looking at the economy, interest rates and news. Intermarket analysis means understanding the environment of one market by the moves of another (such as DXY, shares, gold).**

This chapter answers one question: **"What is happening outside my chart right now that could help or hurt my trade?"**

## Why learn this now

- **A technical setup breaks on a news shock.** Even if your [stop loss](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) is in the right place, the spike of a release can go past it.
- **Context helps set the direction.** The same buy setup has different odds in a strong and a weak environment.
- **Two trades can really be one bet.** [Correlation risk](/en/learn/08-risk-management/06-correlation-risk) gets a new dimension here.
- **Avoiding news is also a skill.** Knowing when to stay out is part of the plan.

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Explain how central bank decisions and inflation move a currency.**
- **Read the economic calendar** and mark high-risk times.
- **Get the idea of currency strength** from DXY.
- **Recognize a risk-on/risk-off environment.**
- **Use the gold-DXY relationship, and understand its limits.**
- **Write rules for trading or avoiding news.**

## The order of decisions at a glance

1. **Is there any big news today?** Check the calendar (lesson 2).
2. **What is the dollar and fear environment?** Look at DXY and shares (lessons 3, 4).
3. **How does this relate to my pair or gold?** (lesson 5).
4. **Take the trade, skip it, or wait?** (lesson 6).

- All charts in this chapter are imaginary (synthetic). In reality, relationships and reactions can differ.
- Check each relationship you learn on your own broker's chart.
