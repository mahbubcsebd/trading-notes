# Risk-on / Risk-off: Is the Market Feeling Bold or Afraid

On some days everything rises together, and on others everything runs away in fear together. If you understand this mood, the behavior of separate charts fits into one story.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say what risk-on and risk-off mean.**
- **Separate safe havens** from **risky assets**.
- **Estimate the environment** by watching share indices and gold together.

## Why it matters

- **Many pairs move in the same mood.** Several trades at the same time can become one bet.
- **It helps set the direction.** You know which currencies usually rise on a day of fear.
- **You get an early hint of a sudden shock.** When shares fall fast, other markets move too.

Prerequisites: [Currency Strength and DXY](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/03-currency-strength-dxy).

## The main idea

**Risk-on is an environment where investors feel bold and buy risky assets (shares, high-yield currencies). Risk-off is an environment of fear, where they run to safe havens.**

Think of a storm forecast on the street. Some people leave the festival and run indoors, and some buy umbrellas. Everyone goes one way for the same reason.

| Environment | Usually rises               | Usually falls               |
| :---------- | :-------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| Risk-on     | Shares, AUD, NZD (tendency) | JPY, CHF, gold (tendency)   |
| Risk-off    | JPY, CHF, gold, USD (often) | Shares, AUD, NZD (tendency) |

These are tendencies, not certainties. Every crisis is different, and the USD sometimes rises as a haven and sometimes moves for another reason.

## Step by step: reading the environment

<Steps>

### Step 1: Look at the share index

Is US500 or another big index falling or rising? This is the main gauge of the mood.

### Step 2: Look at the safe havens

Are gold, JPY and CHF showing a tendency to rise?

### Step 3: Match the two

If shares fall and the havens rise, risk-off is quite strong. If they do not match, the picture is unclear.

### Step 4: Adjust your risk

In risk-off, an AUD or NZD buy and a gold buy may be opposing bets. Check [Correlation](/en/learn/08-risk-management/06-correlation-risk) again.

### Step 5: The setup is always your own

The environment is only context. The entry comes from the structure of the chart.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** if the share index is weak, I will look for a careful buy setup in gold, following the earlier rules.
- **Result:** shares keep falling, and gold gives its own pullback setup. The risk-off context strengthens that setup, and it is easier to hold in a steady move.
- **Lesson:** when context and setup say the same thing, confidence rises, but the stop still has to be placed.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** shares are falling, so, thinking "gold will surely rise", a buy at a large size.
- **Result:** on some days, as shares fall the dollar also gets stronger, and gold stays under pressure. The buy hits the stop.
- **Why it failed:** risk-off does not mean every haven rises. Putting certainty on one of them is a mistake.
- **Lesson:** a tendency means a probability, not a guarantee. Size and stop are the same as before.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Why it happens                    | What to do                         |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Assuming risk-off means gold rises              | Liking an easy rule               | Check it on the real chart         |
| Several trades in the same mood                 | Thinking they are different pairs | See whether they are all one bet   |
| Staying with an old idea after the mood changes | Habit                             | Watch shares and the DXY regularly |
| Fearing small noise in shares                   | Looking at a small timeframe      | See the bigger picture on H1 or H4 |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write a rule: "If shares fall abnormally in one day, I will wait before
  entering a new risky pair." When the environment is unsettled, even a small
  mistake feels big.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  The idea of a safe haven changes with time. Always check on your own chart how
  each asset is behaving.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a calm market with no news:** there is no clear mood.
- **When a currency's own cause is heavy:** for example a sudden change in interest rates.
- **When the crisis is the dollar's own:** then the usual pattern of havens breaks.
- **On a very short timeframe:** the mark of the mood is hidden in noise.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Risk-on means boldness (shares rise), risk-off means fear (running to havens).
- Gold, JPY and CHF are usually safe havens, but there is no certainty.
- Estimate the environment by watching shares and the havens together.
- The mood gives context, not an entry signal.

</KeyTakeaways>
