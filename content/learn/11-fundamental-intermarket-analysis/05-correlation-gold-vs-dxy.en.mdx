# Gold vs DXY: The Inverse Relationship and Its Limits

"When the dollar gets stronger, gold falls." Almost everyone has heard this. It is often true, but not always. This lesson teaches both how to use the rule and where to stop.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say the reason** for the inverse relationship between gold and the DXY.
- **Use the relationship as context** in a trade.
- **Recognize the signs** that the relationship is breaking.

## Why it matters

- **Gold is priced directly in dollars.** In XAUUSD, the USD is one side.
- **One chart can show the possible pressure on another.** You get context fast.
- **Blind belief brings losses.** If you do not know the limits, you make a big bet in the wrong direction.

Prerequisites: [Risk-on / Risk-off](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/04-risk-on-risk-off) and [DXY](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/03-currency-strength-dxy).

## The main idea

**Correlation is the tendency of two markets to move together or in opposite directions. For gold and the DXY, it is usually opposite (negative).**

The reasons, simply:

- Gold is priced in dollars. When the dollar gets stronger, gold is more expensive for buyers using other currencies, and demand can fall.
- When interest rates rise, dollar assets become attractive, and gold, which pays no interest, becomes less attractive. (Details: [Gold and US yields](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/03-dxy-us-yields))

| DXY  | Gold usually | Reason (simple)                     |
| :--- | :----------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Up   | Down         | Gold priced in dollars is expensive |
| Down | Up           | Gold is cheaper, demand rises       |

## Step by step: using the relationship

<Steps>

### Step 1: Put the two charts side by side

Open XAUUSD and the DXY on the same timeframe.

### Step 2: Check the recent relationship

Over the last few days, did they really move opposite? If not, drop the rule.

### Step 3: Mark the important levels of the DXY

When the DXY reaches [support/resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones), be ready for gold's possible reaction.

### Step 4: Look for gold's own setup

The relationship is only context, for or against. The entry comes from gold's structure.

### Step 5: Drop it when the relationship breaks

If the two move the same way together, step away from this rule.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** the DXY is going up, I will look for a sell setup in gold, and place the stop by the rules.
- **Result:** the DXY keeps rising, and gold forms a lower high and a lean downward. On the sell setup gold falls about 50 USD. Both charts tell the same story.
- **Lesson:** when context and structure agree, it is easier to hold.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** thinking "the DXY is rising, so gold will fall", a sell.
- **Result:** gold and the DXY rise together and the sell hits the stop.
- **Why it failed:** in an environment of fear, both the dollar and gold can be bought as havens. The relationship flips for a while.
- **Lesson:** correlation is not law. Check again after a big event.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                                | Why it happens               | What to do                    |
| :----------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| Taking a big size, treating the relationship as a rule | Belief in "everyone says so" | Check it on the recent move   |
| Trading gold by looking only at the DXY                | Looking for the easy path    | Look at gold's own structure  |
| The same rule on big news days                         | The relationship can break   | Lower your trust on news days |
| Not matching the time range                            | One chart M5, the other H4   | Compare on the same timeframe |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  A quick check on whether the relationship is reliable: out of the last 20 big
  moves, count in how many gold and the DXY went opposite. Do the count from
  your own chart.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  In gold's moves, interest rates, geopolitics, central bank buying and ETF
  flows also play a part. The DXY is only one factor.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a crisis of fear:** both the dollar and gold can be bought.
- **On gold-specific news:** for example big central bank buying.
- **When another currency in the dollar basket moves:** the cause of the DXY is not the cause of gold.
- **On a very short timeframe:** noise hides the relationship.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Gold and the DXY usually move opposite, because gold is priced in dollars.
- The relationship is context, not an entry signal.
- It can break in a crisis or on special news.
- Check it on the recent move before using it.

</KeyTakeaways>
