# What Is Forex: Currency Pairs, the Market and Its Risks

Before a trip abroad, you swap your local money for dollars. Millions of people and institutions make that same kind of exchange every day. That is Forex. In this lesson you will learn what Forex is, how prices are quoted, and what a trader actually does there.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** the Forex market in plain words.
- **Tell apart** the base and quote currency.
- **Understand** what buy and sell mean.
- **Recognise** the types of pairs (major, minor, exotic).
- **Be aware** of the risks of retail trading.

## Why it matters

- **It is the base for everything.** Every later lesson builds on these terms.
- **It protects you from false ideas.** Before you believe an "easy money" ad, you need to know what the market really looks like.
- **You start with risk in mind.** You will study [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/00-overview) later, but caution starts now.

Prerequisites: none. This is the first lesson of the course.

## What Forex is

**Forex (foreign exchange) is the global market where one currency is bought or sold in exchange for another.**

- It is **not a single exchange.** Trading happens over the counter (OTC), across a network of banks, brokers and institutions.
- It is usually open **24 hours a day, Monday to Friday.**
- Daily turnover is among the largest of any market in the world. For the latest figure, see the BIS Triennial Survey (check the number yourself).

Who trades here: importers and exporters, banks, central banks, funds, and retail traders like you. More on this in the next lesson: [Market Participants](/en/learn/01-foundations/02-market-participants).

## Pairs, base and quote

Currencies always trade in **pairs**, because you can only state the value of one currency by comparing it with another.

**EURUSD = 1.0900** means **1 euro = 1.0900 US dollars**.

| Part            | Name           | Here |
| :-------------- | :------------- | :--- |
| First currency  | Base currency  | EUR  |
| Second currency | Quote currency | USD  |

- **Buy EURUSD:** you buy euros (with dollars). You expect the euro to rise.
- **Sell EURUSD:** you sell euros (and receive dollars). You expect the euro to fall.

If the price **rises**, a buy makes money. If it **falls**, a sell makes money. You can trade in either direction.

## Types of pairs

| Type          | What it is                                                  | Examples                                               |
| :------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| Major         | The most traded pairs, all including USD                    | EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, USDCHF, USDCAD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD |
| Minor (cross) | Pairs of major currencies without USD                       | EURGBP, EURJPY, GBPJPY                                 |
| Exotic        | One major currency and one from a small or emerging economy | USDTRY, USDZAR                                         |

Exotic pairs usually have wider spreads and more risk. Learn on the major pairs first.

## Step by step: reading a pair

<Steps>

### Step 1: Look at the pair's name

EURUSD: base = EUR, quote = USD.

### Step 2: Read the price

1.0900 means 1 EUR = 1.0900 USD.

### Step 3: Decide on direction

Your view: will the euro rise (buy) or fall (sell)?

### Step 4: Set your risk

First decide where the stop goes and how much you are willing to lose. Never trade without a [stop](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement).

### Step 5: Practise on demo

Not real money. Use a [demo account](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/02-demo-vs-live-account) first.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **View:** EURUSD will rise.
- **Action:** Buy at 1.0850, stop 1.0820, target 1.0900.
- **Result:** Target hit, +50 pips.
- **Lesson:** Buying means buying the base currency; you profit when the price rises. The stop was in place beforehand.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **View:** A new trader thinks "the euro will weaken", meaning EURUSD will fall.
- **Action:** Mixing up the pair's name, they think "I'm buying dollars" and press Buy. So they bought euros.
- **Result:** Price fell, and they lost. With no stop, the loss kept growing.
- **Lesson:** Before placing an order, be sure what you are buying and what you are selling. Never trade without a stop.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                    | Why it happens         | What to do                            |
| :------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| Mixing up base and quote   | The pair names are new | Keep saying "1 base = how much quote" |
| Believing "easy money" ads | Greed                  | Risk first, income later              |
| Starting with exotic pairs | They seem to move more | Start with the majors                 |
| Trading without a stop     | "It will work out"     | A stop on every trade                 |
| Going live without demo    | Impatience             | A few weeks on demo                   |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  In your first month, work with one pair only, such as EURUSD. Once you know
  how one pair behaves, the others become easier.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  With leveraged Forex trading you can lose more than you put in, and many
  retail traders lose money. This is educational material, not investment
  advice. Do not trade with money you cannot afford to lose.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **Expecting to get rich quick:** Forex takes skill, patience and risk discipline.
- **Legal limits:** Not every kind of retail Forex trading is allowed in every country. There is a separate lesson [on this](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/06-bangladesh-e-retail-forex-niyom-ar-tax).
- **Unknown platforms and brokers:** Unregulated firms are risky.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Forex = the global market for exchanging currencies, usually open 24 hours a day, 5 days a week.
- Currencies trade in pairs: base and quote.
- Buy = buy the base; sell = sell the base.
- Retail trading is risky; a stop and small risk are essential.

</KeyTakeaways>
