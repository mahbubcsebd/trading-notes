# Trading Styles, Platforms and Tools: Fitting Trading to Your Life

"Which strategy is best?" is the wrong question. The right one is: **which one fits my time, my temperament and my money?** The best style for someone on night shifts cannot be the same as for a student who is free all day.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** the differences between the four trading styles.
- **Understand** the role of platforms and tools.
- **Choose** a style for yourself.

## Why it matters

- **A style that doesn't fit leads to mistakes.** Scalping under time pressure, or swing trading without patience, both do damage.
- **Costs and mental strain differ by style.** The [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) is a scalper's biggest enemy.
- **It helps you use the next chapters properly.** Indicators and setups have to be chosen to suit your style.

Prerequisites: [Reading a Chart](/en/learn/01-foundations/06-reading-a-chart).

## The four styles

**A trading style is the combination of how long you hold a trade and how often you trade.**

| Style       | Holding time          | Typical timeframe | Attention needed  | Cost pressure       |
| :---------- | :-------------------- | :---------------- | :---------------- | :------------------ |
| Scalping    | Seconds to minutes    | M1–M5             | Very high         | High (spread)       |
| Day trading | Closed within the day | M5–H1             | High              | Medium              |
| Swing       | A few days to weeks   | H4–D1             | Medium            | Low; watch the swap |
| Position    | Weeks to months       | D1–W1             | Low, but patience | Low; swap matters   |

The boundaries are not strict. One person can run different styles at different times, but not all at once.

## Platforms and tools

| Type                    | Role                                 | Examples                |
| :---------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :---------------------- |
| Trading platform        | Placing orders and viewing charts    | MetaTrader 4/5, cTrader |
| Charting platform       | Advanced charts and analysis, alerts | TradingView             |
| Broker's web/mobile app | Orders and account overview          | Varies by broker        |

Platform setup: [MT5 and TradingView](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/03-mt5-ar-tradingview-setup).

**The four classes of indicators** (more detail: [Technical Indicators](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/00-overview)):

| Class      | What it measures  | Examples             |
| :--------- | :---------------- | :------------------- |
| Trend      | Direction         | Moving average       |
| Momentum   | Speed             | RSI, MACD            |
| Volatility | Range of movement | ATR, Bollinger Bands |
| Volume     | Participation     | Volume, VWAP         |

## Step by step: choosing a style

<Steps>

### Step 1: Count your time

How long can you be at the screen each day, after sleep, work and family?

### Step 2: Look at your temperament

Do you like fast decisions, or waiting? How unsettled do you get when you lose?

### Step 3: Costs and capital

On a small account, scalping costs are a large fraction. (Cost calculations are in the previous lessons.)

### Step 4: One style, one pair

For the first three months. No frequent switching.

### Step 5: Test on demo

For at least a few weeks. Does it feel sustainable?

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Situation:** 8 hours a day at the office, 30 minutes free in the evening.
- **Choice:** Swing trading, H4/D1 charts, one check a day, using alerts.
- **Result:** Less stress, rules are easier to follow, and costs are a small share of each trade.
- **Lesson:** When your style fits the structure of your life, discipline gets easier.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Situation:** The same person chose scalping after watching videos, and traded the M1 chart during office hours.
- **Result:** Scattered attention, rushed entries, high spread costs, moved stops. The account wore down within weeks.
- **Why it failed:** The style did not fit their life; it was chosen because the videos made it look appealing.
- **Lesson:** Choose a style based on your own time and temperament, not on other people's videos.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Why it happens                | What to do                          |
| :------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Picking the most "exciting" style      | Hoping for profit             | Match it to your life               |
| Many indicators at once                | It feels safer                | One from each class                 |
| Switching styles often                 | Impatience after bad results  | At least three months               |
| Spending too much time on the platform | The pull of features          | Learn on a simple setup             |
| Ignoring costs                         | Watching only profit and loss | Check the cost share for each style |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  For new learners, swing trading or slower day trading is usually easier to
  learn: lower costs, less rush, more time to think.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  No style gives easy or certain profits. This is educational material, not a
  recommendation of any style. Decide according to your skill, time and
  tolerance for risk.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If your life is irregular:** Keeping a fixed window is hard; position or swing trading suits better.
- **With very small capital:** Scalping costs are a large fraction.
- **Dependence on platform and broker:** Slow execution is a big problem for scalpers.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Four styles: scalping, day, swing, position; they differ in holding time.
- Platforms for orders and charts; tools fall into four classes.
- Choose a style based on time, temperament and costs.
- One style, one pair, three months.

</KeyTakeaways>
