# Pips, Lots, Leverage and Margin: Measuring Distance, Size and Borrowing

On the road you measure distance in kilometres, but the fare depends on the size of the vehicle. In trading, the **pip** is the distance, the **lot** is the size of the vehicle, and **leverage** is borrowed power. Unless you understand all three together, your money maths will be wrong.

## What you will be able to do

- **Measure** pips.
- **Work out** the money value of a pip from the lot size.
- **Calculate** margin.
- **Understand** the risk of leverage.

## Why it matters

- **The wrong lot size causes the biggest losses.** Even with the right direction, the wrong size can wipe out an account.
- **It is the base of [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/00-overview).** Position size rests on these calculations.
- **You need to understand your broker's terms (margin, free margin).**

Prerequisites: [Market Participants](/en/learn/01-foundations/02-market-participants).

## Pip

**A pip is the smallest standard step in price:** the fourth decimal place on most pairs (0.0001), and the second on JPY pairs (0.01).

- EURUSD from 1.0850 to 1.0900 = **50 pips** (0.0050).
- USDJPY from 150.00 to 150.50 = **50 pips** (0.50).
- Many brokers also show a fifth decimal place (a **pipette**, or fractional pip).

## Lot

**A lot is the unit of trade size.**

| Name                | Units of base currency | Value per pip on EURUSD (USD account) |
| :------------------ | :--------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| Standard lot (1.00) | 100,000                | About 10 USD                          |
| Mini lot (0.10)     | 10,000                 | About 1 USD                           |
| Micro lot (0.01)    | 1,000                  | About 0.10 USD                        |

Pip value varies a little with the pair, the account currency and the price. Check it with your platform's calculator. (More detail: [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing))

## Leverage and margin

**Leverage is the broker's facility that lets you open a position larger than your own capital.** **Margin** is the deposit locked up to keep that position open.

**Margin = total value of the position ÷ leverage**

Example (synthetic): 0.10 lot of EURUSD at 1.0900. Position value = 10,000 × 1.0900 = 10,900 USD.

| Leverage | Margin required |
| :------- | :-------------- |
| 1:30     | About 363 USD   |
| 1:100    | 109 USD         |
| 1:500    | About 21.8 USD  |

**Margin is not a cost.** It is a deposit, returned when the trade closes. But **losses are real.** More leverage makes it easy to open bigger positions, and losses come faster.

## Step by step: sizing a trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Decide how many pips the stop is

Based on the setup. For example, 30 pips.

### Step 2: How much are you willing to lose?

1% of the account = 100 USD on 10,000. ([Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade))

### Step 3: Work out the lot size

Lot = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × value per pip). 100 ÷ (30 × 10) = 0.33 lot.

### Step 4: Check the margin

Use the platform's figure. Is there enough free margin?

### Step 5: Size by risk, not by leverage

Leverage is a limit, not a target.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Account:** 10,000 USD. Risk 1% = 100.
- **Trade:** EURUSD, stop 30 pips. Lot = 100 ÷ (30 × 10) ≈ 0.33.
- **Result:** If the stop is hit, the loss is about 100 USD = 1%; with a 60-pip target, about +200 USD.
- **Lesson:** Stop first, then lot size. The loss stays within the planned limit.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Account:** 1,000 USD. The trader opened 1.00 lot (possible because leverage was 1:500).
- **Trade:** Stop 30 pips.
- **Result:** If the stop is hit, the loss = 30 × 10 = 300 USD = 30% of the account. Two such trades in a row take about 60% of the account.
- **Why it failed:** Leverage made it easy to open a big lot, but there was no risk limit.
- **Lesson:** The question is not how much leverage you have. The question is what % you will lose per trade.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Why it happens           | What to do                              |
| :--------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| Thinking more leverage = more profit     | Greed                    | Think in risk %, not leverage           |
| Assuming a pip is worth 10 on every pair | Oversimplifying          | Check with a calculator                 |
| Treating margin as a cost                | Confusing terms          | It is a deposit; losses are separate    |
| Picking a lot size "by feel"             | Rushing                  | Use the formula                         |
| Counting JPY pips wrongly                | Different decimal places | On USDJPY, the second decimal = one pip |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  On a demo account, try the same 20-pip stop with different lot sizes and see
  how much money you lose. Experience sticks better than a formula.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  With leverage, in some regions and with some brokers, you can lose more than
  you deposited. Know your broker's negative balance policy. This is educational
  material, not investment advice.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On gold, indices and crypto:** The contract specs differ, so these numbers don't apply directly. [Gold's maths](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/02-gold-contract-spec-pip-value) is different.
- **In an account in another currency:** Pip value has to be converted.
- **Lot definitions differ by broker:** Some brokers offer "cent accounts", where a lot is smaller.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Pip = a small step; lot = size; leverage = borrowed power; margin = deposit.
- Margin = position value ÷ leverage.
- Set the lot size from the stop and your risk %.
- More leverage raises risk; it does not guarantee profit.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

With a 2,000 USD account, 1% risk, a 25-pip stop and a pip value of 10 (per standard lot), work out the lot size. Then calculate the approximate margin at 1:100 leverage (EURUSD 1.0900).

## Quiz

1. If USDJPY moves from 150.00 to 150.30, how many pips is that?
2. On 0.10 lot of EURUSD, about how many USD is one pip worth?
3. Is margin a cost?
4. At 1:100 leverage, what is the margin on a 10,900 USD position?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** 30 pips (0.30).

**2.** About 1 USD.

**3.** No, it is a deposit; it is returned when the trade closes.

**4.** 109 USD.

</details>

**Practice answer:** Risk = 20 USD; lot = 20 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.08 lot. Position value = 8,000 × 1.09 = 8,720; margin (1:100) = 87.2 USD.
