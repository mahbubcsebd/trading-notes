# Trading Sessions: When Each Market Is Active

City roads are packed at rush hour and empty at midday. Forex is the same: activity is highest when the big financial centres are open. Once you know when that is, you can trade your pair at its most active time.

## What you will be able to do

- **State** the approximate hours of the four sessions.
- **Understand** why overlaps matter.
- **Match** the sessions to your own time zone.
- **Recognise** the risk of trading at the wrong time.

## Why it matters

- **Volatility and spread change with the time of day.** In quiet hours spreads are wider and moves are slower.
- **Your strategy has to fit the time.** Breakouts need momentum; range trading needs a calm market.
- **Your time zone is different.** Bangladesh is UTC+6, so you need to work out when each session falls.

Prerequisites: [Spread, Commission and Swap](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap).

## The four sessions

**A trading session is the active business hours of a major financial centre, when its banks and institutions trade.**

| Session  | Approx. hours (UTC, winter) | Bangladesh time (UTC+6)  |
| :------- | :-------------------------- | :----------------------- |
| Sydney   | 22:00 – 07:00               | 04:00 – 13:00            |
| Tokyo    | 00:00 – 09:00               | 06:00 – 15:00            |
| London   | 08:00 – 17:00               | 14:00 – 23:00            |
| New York | 13:00 – 22:00               | 19:00 – 04:00 (next day) |

**Caution:** These hours are approximate. London and New York observe daylight saving time (summer time), when their hours move one hour earlier in UTC, and the change dates differ by region. Check your broker's server time and the actual dates.

**Overlaps:**

- **Tokyo-London:** short.
- **London-New York** (about 13:00–17:00 UTC): usually the busiest time of the day; activity on EURUSD and GBPUSD is high.

## Which pairs are active when

| Type of pair         | Usually active                          |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| JPY, AUD, NZD pairs  | Tokyo/Sydney, and when London opens     |
| EUR, GBP pairs       | London, and the London-New York overlap |
| USD-based, CAD pairs | New York                                |

This is a general tendency. On any given day, news can change everything.

## Step by step: choosing your session

<Steps>

### Step 1: Pick your pair

For example, EURUSD.

### Step 2: Look up when it is active

London, and the London-New York overlap.

### Step 3: Convert to your own time

In Bangladesh, roughly 14:00–23:00. It shifts by one hour during daylight saving.

### Step 4: Set a realistic window

2 to 3 hours that fit around your work and sleep. ([Routine](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/06-routine))

### Step 5: No trades outside the window

Write the rule down.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Strategy:** Breakout, which needs momentum.
- **Action:** The trader chose a two-hour window on EURUSD after the London open.
- **Result:** The breakout had momentum, the spread was normal, and the trade went to plan.
- **Lesson:** Match the time to the strategy.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Strategy:** The same breakout.
- **Action:** Because it suited them, the trader looked for EURUSD breakouts at 3 a.m. (BD time), when New York was winding down and London was closed.
- **Result:** The market was slow and the spread was wide; the breakout failed several times and hit the stop.
- **Why it failed:** In low-activity hours, breakouts don't get momentum.
- **Lesson:** The time has to fit the strategy, not just your convenience.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Why it happens              | What to do                        |
| :-------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Ignoring daylight saving                      | Memorising fixed hours      | Recheck the hours twice a year    |
| Not understanding broker server time          | Assuming it is UTC          | Check the platform clock's offset |
| Trading every pair at every hour              | Fear of missing out         | A window per pair                 |
| Trading Friday's last hour and Sunday's open  | "The market is always open" | Avoid low-liquidity hours         |
| Treating the overlap as a time of sure profit | Wrong assumption            | More activity, but more risk too  |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Add session markers (vertical lines) to your platform. Seeing on the chart
  every day what happens when helps the idea stick faster.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Bigger moves during the overlap do not automatically mean bigger profits; set
  your [stop](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement)
  and size accordingly.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On big news days:** Session rules give way to the news.
- **On holidays:** If a centre is closed, the schedule changes.
- **On other pairs or products:** Gold's active hours can differ, see [Gold sessions](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/05-sessions-volatility).

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Four sessions: Sydney, Tokyo, London, New York; the hours are approximate and shift with daylight saving.
- The London-New York overlap is usually the busiest.
- Match the time to your pair and strategy.
- Convert to Bangladesh time and set your window.

</KeyTakeaways>
