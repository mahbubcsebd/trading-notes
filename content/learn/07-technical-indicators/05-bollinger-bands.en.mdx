# Bollinger Bands: Reading the Squeeze and the Band Walk Correctly

Price touched the upper band, and you thought "it should fall here". You sold. Price kept climbing along the band. A band is not a boundary; it is a measure.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain **what Bollinger Bands measure**.
- Spot a **squeeze** and be ready for the breakout.
- Spot a **band walk** and avoid wrong counter-trades.
- Place **entry, stop and target** on a squeeze breakout.

## Why it matters

- **You can see volatility.** Wide bands mean a restless market; narrow bands mean a calm one.
- **A move after the calm.** Narrow bands often come before a big move, without telling you the direction.
- **The most common counter-trade trap.** Selling because price is at the upper band.

Prerequisites: [ATR](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/04-atr-stop-size) (the idea of volatility) and [Range Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout).

## What are Bollinger Bands

**Bollinger Bands are three lines: a 20-period SMA in the middle, and two bands two standard deviations above and below it.**

Think of a rubber band wrapped around price. When price is restless the band stretches; when it is calm the band shrinks.

Key facts:

- **Middle line:** 20 SMA, the average price.
- **Upper and lower band:** average ± 2 × standard deviation.
- **Wide bands:** high volatility. **Narrow bands:** low volatility.
- Price usually stays inside the bands, but in a trend it can move along or outside a band.

**A band is not a reversal limit.** It shows "how far price is from the average".

## What it looks like on a chart: a squeeze breakout

  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example) — at candle 42 the band width is only 9.5 pips (it was 45.2 pips at candle 22). Candle 43 closes above the upper band: buy 1.0915."
/>

Three stages:

1. **Squeeze:** band width fell from 45.2 pips to 9.5 pips. The market is tired and calm.
2. **Burst:** a strong candle closed above the upper band.
3. **Follow-through:** price climbed hugging the band.

Important: a squeeze tells you a move **is coming**, not which way. The direction comes from the breakout close.

## Step by step: the squeeze breakout trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Spot the squeeze

Band width is clearly smaller than in the previous few weeks.

### Step 2: Plan for both directions

Do not pick a side in advance. Write down what you will do on a breakout either way.

### Step 3: Wait for a close outside the band

A close, not a touch. In the example, candle 43, buy 1.0915.

### Step 4: Place the stop below the recent low

In the example, 1.0901, a risk of 14 pips. You can check it against ATR if you like.

### Step 5: Set the target

At least twice the risk. In the example, 1.0943, hit at candle 49.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **What you could see:** a long squeeze, then a strong close above the upper band.
- **Entry:** buy 1.0915.
- **Stop:** 1.0901. Risk 14 pips.
- **Target:** 1.0943, 28 pips, 1:2.
- **Lesson:** the squeeze gave the warning; the close gave the time to enter.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Now selling at the upper band in a strong uptrend:

- **What you could see:** price touched the upper band. It seems "it has risen too much".
- **Decision:** sell 1.0888, stop 1.0918 (30 pips), target the middle line at 1.0837.
- **Result:** the stop is hit at candle 30. Later price rose to 1.1150, 262 pips against the entry.
- **Why it was wrong:** in a trend, price moves along the band (band walk). A touch alone is not a reversal.
- **Lesson:** in a trend, the upper band means strength, not weakness.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Why it happens                       | What to do                           |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Selling at the upper band, buying at the lower | Treating the band as a boundary      | First check whether there is a trend |
| Picking a direction from the squeeze           | Rush to get in early                 | Wait for the close                   |
| Entering on a touch only                       | It seems lower risk                  | Look for the close                   |
| Placing the stop outside the band              | Treating the indicator as everything | Place it beyond the swing            |
| Changing settings                              | Looking for better results           | Start with 20 and 2                  |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  In a range, the bands can hint at reversals; in a trend, they do not. First
  decide which state the market is in.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  On a squeeze breakout, look for confirmation from

## When this does not work

- **In a strong trend:** counter-trades at the upper or lower band keep failing.
- **When a range changes edges:** the bands react late.
- **Before big news:** there is a squeeze, but the direction depends on the news.
- **On small timeframes:** noise creates countless small squeezes.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Bollinger Bands = 20 SMA ± 2 standard deviations; they measure volatility.
- A squeeze warns that a move is coming, not its direction.
- A band walk is normal in a trend; selling at the upper band is a trap.
- After the breakout close, place the stop beyond structure.

</KeyTakeaways>
