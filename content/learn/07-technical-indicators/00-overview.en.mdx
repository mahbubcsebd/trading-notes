# Technical Indicators: Helper Tools Built From Price

A car dashboard has a speedometer, but you watch the road yourself. The speedometer tells you how fast you are going, not where to go. An indicator is the same.

## What is a technical indicator

**A technical indicator is a number or line calculated from price (and sometimes volume). It helps you read one side of price behaviour on the chart more easily.**

This chapter answers one question: **"Which indicator answers which question, and when does it mislead?"**

Every indicator comes after price, not before it. So it supports a decision; it does not make one.

## Why learn this now

- **You can now read structure.** After [Market Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/00-overview), [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/00-overview) and [Chart Patterns](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/00-overview), it is easier to see where indicators help and where they do not.
- **Beginners learn in the wrong order.** Indicators first, price later. The result is 10 lines on the chart and no decision.
- **ATR directly sets stop and size.** You need it for the next chapter, [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).
- **Wrong use is costly.** For example, selling on RSI in a trend, or buying on a crossover in a range.

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- Use moving averages to **spot trend direction and where pullbacks may stop**.
- Use RSI to **read momentum and spot divergence**.
- **Understand the three parts of MACD separately and use them in the right situation**.
- Use ATR to **set stop and size based on volatility**.
- Use Bollinger Bands and volume to **read squeezes, breakouts and their confirmation**.
- **Trim unnecessary indicators and set up a clean chart**.

## How these lessons work together

When you open a chart, the questions come in this order:

1. **What is the direction?** A moving average (lesson 1) or structure tells you whether there is a trend.
2. **How is momentum?** RSI and MACD (lessons 2-3) show whether speed is rising or falling.
3. **How much volatility?** ATR and Bollinger Bands (lessons 4-5) point to stop distance and a coming move.
4. **Is there participation?** Volume (lesson 6) checks the strength of a breakout.
5. **How many tools do you need?** The last lesson teaches you to keep one tool per question.

## How to read this chapter

- **Add one indicator at a time.** Clean the chart after each lesson.
- **Practise on a demo account.** Not with real money.
- **Do not change the settings.** Start with the common values (for example RSI 14, MACD 12-26-9).
- **Also look at each indicator's failure charts.** That is where most of the learning is.

<Callout type="note">
  The charts in this chapter use synthetic educational data, and each one is
  labelled. No success rate is given for any indicator, because results depend
  on the market, timeframe and definition. In forex, volume means tick volume,
  not actual traded volume.
</Callout>
