# RSI: Reading Momentum and Using Divergence the Right Way

RSI went above 70, so you sold. Price rose another 400 pips. There is no counting how many traders the belief "overbought means it will fall" has dragged down.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what RSI measures and what it does not.
- Understand **why** an overbought reading in a trend misleads.
- **Spot** bearish divergence on a chart.
- After divergence, place **entry, stop and target with a structure break**.

## Why it matters

- **The most misunderstood indicator.** Wrong ideas about overbought/oversold get many stops hit.
- **Momentum changes first.** Even when price makes a new high, RSI shows early if the speed is weakening.
- **It pairs well with structure.** [Break of Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure) and RSI together avoid many traps.

Prerequisites: [Moving Averages](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/01-moving-averages-crossover) and [Swing High and Swing Low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low).

## What is RSI

**RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a number between 0 and 100. It compares how strongly price rose versus fell over the last n candles.**

Think of a runner's breathing. Running fast makes breathing heavy, but a fit runner can keep that pace for a long time. RSI measures speed; it does not announce a stop.

Key facts:

- The common setting is **14 periods**.
- The usual limits are **above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold**.
- Formula: RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS), where RS = average gain ÷ average loss.
- The 50 line is roughly balance: above it, buyer pressure; below it, seller pressure.

**Overbought ≠ sell.** It means "price rose fast". RSI does not tell you why it rose.

## What is divergence

**Divergence is when price and RSI move in different directions.** Bearish divergence means price makes a higher high, but RSI makes a lower high. The message: the speed of the rise is slowing.

Important: **divergence is a warning, not a trigger.** Price often keeps rising despite divergence. The trigger is a break in price structure.

## Step by step: the bearish divergence trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark two highs

The second high is above the first. The two should be several candles apart and be clear swing highs.

### Step 2: Match the two RSI highs

If the second RSI high is lower than the first, there is divergence.

### Step 3: Draw the swing low between the two highs

This is your trigger level.

### Step 4: Wait for the close

Sell only when a candle closes below that swing low. Not before.

### Step 5: Place the stop above the second high

If price goes back there, the divergence idea is wrong.

### Step 6: Set the target

At least twice the risk. See [Risk Reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

<Chart
  id="rsi-divergence-01"
  height={460}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example) — high at candle 26 is 1.1001 (RSI 75.4), high at candle 55 is 1.1008 (RSI 60.6). Candle 59 broke the swing low at 1.0936 and closed at 1.0933."
/>

- **What you could see:** price made a higher high from 1.1001 to 1.1008, but RSI fell from 75.4 to 60.6.
- **Trigger:** swing low 1.0936 (candle 33). Candle 59 closed below it.
- **Entry:** sell 1.0933.
- **Stop:** 1.1013, above the second high. Risk 80 pips.
- **Target:** 1.0773, 160 pips away, so 1:2.
- **Result:** price fell and hit the target.
- **Lesson:** the divergence was there from candle 26, but the right time to enter was candle 59, when structure broke.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade, and no result is guaranteed.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Look again at the trend chart from the start of this lesson: selling at candle 17 because it is "overbought".

- **What you could see:** RSI above 70; it seems "price has risen too much".
- **Decision:** sell 1.0849, with no structure, only RSI.
- **Result:** price rose another 411 pips. Any reasonable stop would have been hit.
- **Why it was wrong:** in a trend, RSI easily stays above 70. There was no divergence, and structure did not break.
- **Lesson:** you cannot trade on the RSI value alone.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Why it happens                            | What to do                           |
| :------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Selling at 70, buying at 30            | The name itself sounds like a simple rule | First check whether there is a trend |
| Entering as soon as you see divergence | Rush to get in early                      | Wait for the structure break         |
| Divergence on small timeframes         | Noise creates many "divergences"          | Use H4 or a higher timeframe         |
| Treating one divergence as certain     | A neat picture builds confidence          | Always place a stop                  |
| Changing settings to move the limits   | Looking for "better" results              | Start with 14 and 30/70              |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  In an uptrend, RSI usually moves in the 40-80 zone; in a downtrend, 20-60.
  Once you know the trend zone, the "overbought" mistake becomes obvious.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  When divergence lines up with nearby resistance or a supply zone, the warning
  is stronger. See [Supply and
  Demand](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/03-supply-demand).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The stronger the trend, the longer divergence can keep failing. Never trade it
  without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a strong trend:** RSI often stays overbought or oversold.
- **On very small timeframes:** noise creates many fake divergences.
- **In a range:** RSI moves between 30 and 70, which means nothing special.
- **During big news:** RSI jumps in a single candle, so comparisons are meaningless.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- RSI measures price speed; above 70 means it rose fast, not that it will fall.
- In a trend, RSI can stay above 70 for a long time.
- Divergence is a warning; the trigger is a structure break.
- The stop goes above the second high.

</KeyTakeaways>
