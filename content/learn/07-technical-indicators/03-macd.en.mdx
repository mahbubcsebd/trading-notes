# MACD: A Simple Guide to Reading Changes in Momentum

Two lines on the chart and a pile of small and large bars. Beginners say "buy when it crosses up, sell when it crosses down". Sometimes this rule works; sometimes it is wrong five times in a row. The reason is hidden in how MACD is built.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain the **three parts** of MACD separately.
- Read from the histogram **whether momentum is falling or rising**.
- Place **entry, stop and target** with a crossover at the start of a trend.
- Spot **the signs to avoid MACD** in a range.

## Why it matters

- **It catches changes in momentum direction.** Price is still up, but speed is falling, and MACD shows this early.
- **One indicator, three pieces of information.** Direction, speed and its change.
- **Wrong use costs money.** In a range, crossovers lose again and again.

Prerequisites: [Moving Averages](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/01-moving-averages-crossover) (EMA), because MACD is the difference between two EMAs.

## What is MACD

**MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) is the difference between two EMAs. It shows how short-term speed compares with long-term speed.**

Think of two friends walking. One is fast (EMA 12), the other slow (EMA 26). When the gap between them grows, speed is rising; when it shrinks, speed is falling.

Three parts:

| Part            | Calculation             | What it tells you                                                                  |
| :-------------- | :---------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MACD line**   | EMA 12 − EMA 26         | Direction and strength of speed. Above zero means the short-term average is higher |
| **Signal line** | EMA 9 of the MACD line  | A smoothed version of the MACD line                                                |
| **Histogram**   | MACD line − Signal line | Whether speed is rising or falling. Crossing zero means a crossover                |

The common setting is **12, 26, 9**.

Notice:

1. The histogram went from negative, through zero, to positive.
2. The crossover happened **below** the zero line (MACD −0.00042, signal −0.00059). This means the downtrend's speed slowed and is now turning the other way.
3. Price already shows a higher low and a break upward. MACD confirms it; it does not decide alone.

## Step by step: the MACD crossover trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Look for signs of change in price

A higher low, a structure break or a level holding. MACD alone is not enough.

### Step 2: Look at the histogram

If the negative bars keep getting smaller, selling speed is falling. When it turns positive, the crossover is confirmed.

### Step 3: Wait for the crossover candle to close

The histogram changes while the candle is open. Wait until the close.

### Step 4: Place the stop below the recent swing low

In the example, 1.0844, a risk of 51 pips.

### Step 5: Set the target

At least twice the risk. In the example, 1.0997, 102 pips, 1:2.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **What you could see:** price broke out of the base to 1.0895; MACD and signal both below zero but rising.
- **Entry:** buy 1.0895 (candle 44).
- **Stop:** 1.0844, below the swing low. Risk 51 pips.
- **Target:** 1.0997, hit by candle 59.
- **Lesson:** the MACD crossover worked because price itself was turning.

- **What you could see:** price in a 1.0830-1.0890 range; the histogram is small and flips quickly on both sides of the zero line.
- **Signals:** sell 1.0847 at 37, buy 1.0861 at 47, sell 1.0854 at 56, buy 1.0857 at 66.
- **Result:** closing at the next cross gives −14, −7 and −3 pips, a total of −24 pips, before spread and commission.
- **Why it failed:** each time the signal came, the swing was almost over. The losses are small, but add spread and every one loses.
- **Lesson:** a flat histogram and frequent crosses mean there is no trend.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | Why it happens                       | What to do                               |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| Trading every crossover                            | Simple rule                          | First look for signs of a trend in price |
| Entering on the histogram while the candle is open | Rush                                 | Wait for the close                       |
| Setting the stop based on MACD                     | Treating the indicator as everything | Place it below the swing low             |
| Changing settings                                  | Looking for better results           | Stick with 12-26-9                       |
| Ignoring the zero line                             | Looking only at the cross            | Understand the relation to the zero line |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Shrinking histogram bars mean speed is falling. This warns you even before the
  crossover.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  MACD also shows
  [divergence](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/02-rsi-divergence): price makes
  a new high, MACD a lower high. Same rule, same caution.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  MACD lags, because it is built from EMAs. After a fast spike, the signal comes
  late.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a range:** the histogram is flat; crossovers are frequent and meaningless.
- **During big news:** MACD jumps in a single candle.
- **On very small timeframes:** noise creates countless crosses.
- **Late in a strong trend:** the crossover comes when the move is almost over.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- MACD = EMA 12 − EMA 26; signal = its EMA 9; histogram = the difference between the two.
- The histogram shows whether speed is rising or falling.
- A crossover works when price is also turning.
- A flat histogram and frequent crosses mean stay out.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. What does the MACD histogram show?
2. Why is a MACD crossover alone not enough?
3. Which MACD sign in a range tells you to stay away?
4. Where do you place the stop in a MACD trade?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** The difference between the MACD line and the signal line, that is, whether speed is rising or falling.

**2.** MACD lags and gives frequent false crosses in a range, so you need confirmation from price structure.

**3.** A flat histogram near zero, with frequent crosses.

**4.** Beyond the recent swing low (buy) or swing high (sell).

</details>
