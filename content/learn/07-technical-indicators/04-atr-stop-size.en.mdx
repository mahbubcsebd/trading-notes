# ATR: Set Stop and Position Size by Volatility

Your idea was right. Price went your way. But before that, one quick jolt hit your stop. The idea was not wrong; the stop distance was.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what ATR measures.
- Use ATR to **place the stop outside the noise**.
- When the stop is further away, **cut lot size to keep risk fixed**.
- **Understand from the chart** why a very close stop loses even when the idea is right.

## Why it matters

- **Stop distance is not a guess.** A fixed 15 pips does not mean the same thing in every market.
- **Volatility changes.** On a quiet day 15 pips is a lot; on a wild day it is small.
- **Size and stop are set together.** This is the base for the next chapter, [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).

Prerequisites: [Swing High and Swing Low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low) and [Pips, Lots, Leverage](/en/learn/01-foundations/03-pips-lots-leverage-margin).

## What is ATR

**ATR (Average True Range) measures how far each candle moves on average. It does not give direction, only the size of the move.**

Think of a river current. On a calm river even a small boat is fine; in a fast current big waves come. ATR tells you how restless the river is today.

The calculation in short:

- **True Range** = the largest of these three: high − low; |high − previous close|; |low − previous close|.
- **ATR** = the average True Range of the last 14 candles (with Wilder's smoothing).
- The result is in price units. On EURUSD, 0.0019 means 19 pips.

Here ATR is 19.4 pips. That means each H4 candle moves about this much on average. If you place the stop at **half that distance** (about 10 pips), one normal candle can hit it.

## Step by step: ATR stop and size

<Steps>

### Step 1: Set the entry

The entry comes from price structure (pullback, breakout and so on). ATR does not give the entry.

### Step 2: Read the ATR

What is ATR on the entry candle? In the example, 19.4 pips.

### Step 3: Pick a multiplier and place the stop

Usually 1.5 to 3 times ATR. In the example, 2 times: 38.8 pips. If possible, the stop should also fall below the swing low.

### Step 4: Set the size

Lots = risk amount ÷ (stop pips × value per pip). On EURUSD, the pip value for 1 standard lot is about $10.

(Example, synthetic) With a risk of $10: 10 ÷ (38.8 × 10) = 0.026, so round down to 0.02 lots.

### Step 5: Set the target

At least twice the risk. In the example, 1.1030, about 78 pips away.

</Steps>

Notice: if the stop is twice as far, the lot size is roughly halved to keep risk fixed. **An ATR stop does not mean more risk; it means smaller size.** Details in [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **Entry:** buy 1.0952.
- **Stop:** 1.0913 (2 x ATR). Risk 38.8 pips.
- **Target:** 1.1030 (about 1:2).
- **Result:** price pulled back for a while but did not touch the stop. Candle 65 hit the target.
- **Lesson:** the stop was outside the noise, so the idea had a chance to work.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade, and no result is guaranteed.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Same candle, same entry. Only the stop changed:

- **What you could see:** the same setup. ATR 19.4 pips, yet the stop is 10 pips away.
- **Result:** the very next candle (53) hits the stop. Then price goes up and reaches the target from the previous example.
- **Why it was wrong:** the stop was inside normal noise. The idea was right, but it still lost.
- **Lesson:** pulling the stop closer to show "less risk" is a mistake. You reduce risk by making the size smaller.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Why it happens         | What to do                 |
| :----------------------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------- |
| Fixed pip stop all the time                | It seems simple        | Adjust it by ATR           |
| Pulling the stop close to show "less risk" | Wanting smaller losses | Cut the size, not the stop |
| Looking for entries with ATR               | Misunderstanding ATR   | Entry comes from structure |
| Stop further away but same size            | Not doing the math     | Recalculate the lots       |
| Treating ATR as a forecast                 | ATR is a past average  | Be careful before big news |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Match the stop to both the ATR multiple and structure. If 2 x ATR falls inside
  the swing low, put the stop below the swing low.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Rising ATR means the market is heating up; falling ATR means it is calming
  down. A move comes after a quiet period; you will see another form of this in
  [Bollinger Bands](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/05-bollinger-bands).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  ATR does not see a big news spike in advance. Before the event, it is safer to
  cut size or stay out.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **A sudden jump in volatility:** ATR lags, so your stop is too small for the first spike.
- **Assets with very little data:** the average is unstable.
- **When structure is unclear:** ATR gives the stop distance, but not where to measure it from.
- **On small timeframes:** the spread can eat a large part of the ATR.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- ATR is the average size of each candle's move, not its direction.
- Place the stop 1.5-3 x ATR away, outside the noise.
- If the stop is further away, cut the lots and keep risk fixed.
- You can lose even with the right idea if the stop is inside the noise.

</KeyTakeaways>
