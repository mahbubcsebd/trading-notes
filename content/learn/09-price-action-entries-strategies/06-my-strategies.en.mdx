# Writing Your Own Strategy: Your Rules on One Page

Saying "I trade price action" says nothing. Where do you enter? When do you exit? How much risk? If you cannot write it down, it is not a strategy. It is a habit.

## What you will be able to do

- **Name the necessary parts** of a strategy.
- **Write your rules** on a one-page card.
- **Explain** why judging a strategy by one win or one loss is wrong.

## Why it matters

- **Without rules there is nothing to test.** A backtest needs written rules.
- **Emotion changes the rules.** A written card brings you back under pressure.
- **You can catch the same mistake.** When a rule is broken, you can see it.

Prerequisites: [Entry Model Checklist](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/05-entry-model-checklist) and [Expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy).

## What is a strategy

**A strategy is a set of written rules that says when you take a trade, how you handle risk, when you exit and when you do not trade.**

Think of a cooking recipe. "I cook well" is not a recipe. With ingredients, amounts, times and steps, someone else can make it again. Your strategy must be repeatable too.

| Part            | Question                                       |
| :-------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| Market and time | Which pair, which timeframe, which session?    |
| Direction       | When to buy, when to sell, when to do nothing? |
| Entry           | What are the setup, confirmation and trigger?  |
| Stop            | Where, and based on what?                      |
| Target          | Where, and what is the minimum ratio?          |
| Risk            | How much per trade and per day?                |
| Exclusions      | When do you not trade (news, range)?           |

## Step by step: build a strategy card

<Steps>

### Step 1: Pick one pair and one timeframe

Start with one pair. With more, the sample gets scattered.

### Step 2: Write the entry rules

Separate [setup, confirmation and trigger](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/04-confirmation-vs-trigger). "If it feels right" cannot be written.

### Step 3: Write the stop and target rules

The stop beyond the structure, the target before the next barrier, and write the minimum [ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward).

### Step 4: Write the risk rules

Write the [risk per trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade) and the [daily limit](/en/learn/08-risk-management/05-daily-weekly-limits).

### Step 5: Write "when not"

During news, in a range market, when the limit is passed. This part is the one most often forgotten.

### Step 6: Build up a sample

Do not call a strategy good or bad until at least many trades (demo or backtest) have piled up. The method for testing: [How to Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 30 pips, target 90 pips (1:3).
- **Result:** the trade followed every rule, and price touched the target.
- **Lesson:** one win is not proof. It is just one sample.

- **Lesson:** losing while following the rules is not a mistake. Winning while breaking the rules is.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Why it happens            | What to do                               |
| :------------------------------------- | :------------------------ | :--------------------------------------- |
| Vague rules ("if it feels right")      | Laziness in writing       | Write rules that can be answered yes/no  |
| Changing the strategy after two losses | Emotion over the result   | Keep it until you have many samples      |
| Many strategies at once                | Wanting to try everything | One strategy, one pair                   |
| Not writing "when not"                 | Thinking only about entry | Make the exclusion rules a separate part |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If you cannot fit the rules of your strategy on one page, it is too complex.
  If it does not fit on one page, you will not remember it under pressure.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Copying someone else's strategy does not copy their situation. Test it on your
  own time, risk tolerance and sample.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the sample is small:** in ten trades the result can be luck too.
- **When the market's character changes:** going from trend to range, the old rules can weaken.
- **When the rules are not followed:** a written strategy stays on paper.
- **When costs are left out:** spread and commission can ruin the result.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A strategy means written rules: market, entry, stop, target, risk and "when not".
- One win or one loss does not prove a strategy.
- Losing while following the rules is normal. Winning while breaking them is dangerous.
- A one-page card brings you back under pressure.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write a one-page card for your strategy: pair, timeframe, entry, stop, target, risk, "when not". Then apply it to the last month of charts and count how many trades there would have been and what the result was.
