# Trend Continuation vs Reversal: Whose Side Do You Trade On

Price has risen a lot, and you think "now it must fall". You sell. Price rises more. When is the thought "it will fall" right, and when is it expensive?

## What you will be able to do

- **Tell the difference** between continuation and reversal setups.
- **Place an entry in the direction of the trend** on a pullback.
- **Say what proof you need first** if you want to take a reversal.

## Why it matters

- **Trading against the trend carries more risk.** You are swimming against the current.
- **"Catching the top" is attractive but hard.** Assuming a reversal from one wick is the most common mistake.
- **In continuation, the stop is natural.** The structure itself gives the place for the stop.

Prerequisites: [Trend and Range](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) and [Break of Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure).

## What are continuation and reversal

**Continuation means entering again in the direction of the running trend, usually after a pullback. Reversal means betting on a change in the direction of the trend.**

Think of a train running. Continuation is staying on the train at the next station. Reversal is jumping off to board the train going the other way. The first is normal. The second is risky without proof.

| Side       | Continuation                         | Reversal                          |
| :--------- | :----------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Direction  | with the trend                       | against the trend                 |
| Proof      | higher low / lower high still intact | structure broken (BOS/CHoCH type) |
| Stop       | beyond the last swing                | beyond the extreme point          |
| Usual risk | comparatively lower                  | comparatively higher              |

The last low of the pullback did not go below the earlier higher low. That is the proof that "the trend is intact".

## Step by step: the continuation entry

<Steps>

### Step 1: Confirm the trend

At least two higher highs and higher lows. With fewer, it is not a trend.

### Step 2: Wait for the pullback

Let price come down from the last swing high. Do not enter high up.

### Step 3: Spot the end of the pullback

See whether price is stopping near the earlier higher low, a [support zone](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) or a [trendline](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/04-trendlines-channels).

### Step 4: Enter on the signal

Buy when a candle turns back upward.

### Step 5: Stop below the higher low

If the low breaks, the idea of a trend was wrong. This is the natural stop.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 30 pips, target 90 pips (1:3).
- **Result:** the higher low held, and in the next wave price touched the target.
- **Lesson:** because the structure was intact, the stop sat in a meaningful place.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

<Chart
  id="reversal-attempt-fail-01"

/>

- **What was visible:** a wick like a shooting star, which looked like a "top".
- **Result:** after falling a little, price rose again and the stop was hit.
- **Why it failed:** the structure had not broken. One candle does not reverse a trend.
- **Lesson:** before a reversal, ask for proof of the BOS or CHoCH type.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Why it happens                       | What to do                            |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| Calling a reversal from one wick               | The attraction of "catching the top" | Wait until the structure breaks       |
| Entering without a pullback                    | Hurry                                | Wait for the pullback and a signal    |
| Not setting a stop, assuming the trend is over | Overconfidence                       | Stop always first                     |
| Continuation on a small trend                  | Misreading the direction             | Check the trend on a bigger timeframe |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If you do want to take a reversal, halve the risk. Less proof means less
  certainty, so the size should be smaller too.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The longer the trend, the more attractive the thought "it will turn this
  time". But a long trend can get longer.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a range market:** there is no trend, so there is no continuation.
- **Near the end of a trend:** the last pullback can be the reversal.
- **During sharp news:** the structure breaks in a jolt.
- **If the pullback is very deep:** check whether the trend is actually changing.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Continuation is entering with the trend, on a pullback. Reversal without proof is risky.
- Proof that the trend is intact: the higher low or lower high has not broken.
- One wick or one candle does not prove a reversal.
- If you take a reversal, keep the size small.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Pick one clear trend on an H4 chart. Mark three pullbacks. For each, write whether the higher low (or lower high) stayed intact, and what the result would have been with an entry, stop and target.

4. What do you do about size if you take a reversal?
