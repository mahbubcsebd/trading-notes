# Entry Model Checklist: The Filter Before Every Trade

A pilot goes through a checklist before every flight, however experienced they are. That is because mistakes usually come not from ignorance but from hurry. It is the same in trading.

## What you will be able to do

- **Match six questions** before an entry.
- **Spot the difference** between the ratio on paper and the real ratio.
- **Skip the trade** if one box on the checklist is empty.

## Why it matters

- **Emotion comes while you watch the chart.** The checklist was built earlier, so it is a calm-headed decision.
- **A small mistake becomes a big loss.** Ignoring one box is often the reason for a failed trade.
- **Results can be compared.** Trades taken with the same checklist can be analyzed later.

Prerequisites: [Risk:Reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) and [Confirmation vs Trigger](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/04-confirmation-vs-trigger).

## What is an entry checklist

**An entry checklist is a list of yes-or-no questions answered before taking a trade. If all are "yes", enter. If one is "no", do not.**

Think of airport security. Passing one door is not enough. You must pass every step.

| #   | Question  | Main point                                          |
| :-- | :-------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Direction | Does it match the bigger timeframe's trend/barrier? |
| 2   | Place     | Is price at an important zone or level?             |
| 3   | Trigger   | Has the specific condition to enter been met?       |
| 4   | Stop      | Is it beyond the structure, in a meaningful place?  |
| 5   | Ratio     | Is there the minimum ratio (for example 1:2)?       |
| 6   | Path      | Is there no big barrier on the way to the target?   |

## Step by step: using the checklist

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write it in advance

Build the checklist once. Do not think it out afresh for each trade.

### Step 2: When you see a setup, match every box

Write "yes" or "no" for the six questions. "Almost" is not allowed.

### Step 3: Measure the ratio and the path separately

See whether there is a [resistance or support](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) coming up before the target. If there is, that is the real target.

### Step 4: If one is "no", drop it

An empty box means no trade. Wait for the next setup.

### Step 5: After the trade, write the result

Which boxes were green, and what was the result. Keep it in your [journal](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/05-journaling-habit).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** all six boxes "yes", risk 1%, ratio 1:2.5.
- **Result:** the path to the target was clear, and price reached it.
- **Lesson:** a checklist is not a guarantee of winning, but it holds the quality of the setup.

- **Lesson:** the ratio on paper and the real ratio are not the same.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Why it happens             | What to do                           |
| :------------------------------------ | :------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Entering on "almost matched"          | Fear of missing the trade  | One "no" means skip                  |
| Not looking at the path to the target | Dazzled by the ratio       | Mark the levels in between           |
| Filling the checklist after entering  | Defending yourself         | Fill it before entry                 |
| Changing the checklist constantly     | Not satisfied with results | Look at many samples before changing |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Print the checklist and keep it beside the screen, or pin it. If it is in
  front of your eyes, a box is not skipped in a hurry.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Add new boxes from your own mistakes. For example, a "is there news?" box can
  be born after the same mistake again and again.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the checklist is too long:** nobody follows it. Six to eight boxes are enough.
- **If you fill it in with emotion:** it is easy to assume "yes". Ask for specific proof.
- **In a fast market:** the steps have to be matched quickly, so it takes practice.
- **If you rely only on the checklist:** when the market changes, test the rules too.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Six questions: direction, place, trigger, stop, ratio, path.
- One "no" means no trade.
- The ratio on paper is not real if there is a barrier on the path.
- Fill the checklist before entry, not after.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write a checklist of six to eight boxes for yourself. Apply it to your last five demo trades (or to old charts). Which would have passed, and which would have been dropped?

## Quiz

1. What do you do if there is one "no" on the checklist?
2. Why does the ratio on paper differ from the real ratio?
3. When do you fill in the checklist?
4. Why is it not right to make the checklist very long?

**2.** If there is a barrier (support/resistance) on the way to the target, price can turn back earlier.

**3.** Before entry.

**4.** If it is too long, it is hard to follow and decisions get slow.
