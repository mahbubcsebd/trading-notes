# Price Action Entries and Strategy: When to Take a Trade

In the earlier chapters you learned the tools: structure, candles, levels, patterns, indicators, risk. Now the question is: when do you put them together and press "buy" or "sell"? Having the tools does not cook the meal. You need a recipe.

## What is a price action entry

**A price action entry means reading the price's own behavior on the chart (structure, levels, candles) and picking a place and a moment where the stop is small and the target is big.**

This chapter answers one question: **"At which place, on which signal, by which rules do I take a trade?"**

## Why learn this now

- **Risk first, entry second.** If you know [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/00-overview), it is easier to write entry rules: where the stop goes, what the ratio is.
- **Entry mistakes are the easiest to spot.** Entering late, entering early, entering with no signal: all three take money from the account.
- **The later chapters stand on this one.** [Trade Management](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) assumes you have an entry.
- **A strategy means rules.** If you do not write the rules, you cannot backtest.

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Recognize and take** an entry on the retest of a breakout.
- **Tell the difference** between trend continuation and reversal setups.
- **Find the timing**, taking the direction from the bigger timeframe and the timing from the smaller one.
- **Separate** confirmation from trigger.
- **Match a checklist** before every trade.
- **Write your own strategy** as rules.

## How these lessons work together

Before a trade, the questions come in this order:

1. **What is the big picture?** Multi-timeframe analysis sets the direction (lesson 3).
2. **With the trend or against it?** This decides continuation or reversal (lesson 2).
3. **What kind of place is it?** Recognize a breakout retest or a pullback (lesson 1).
4. **Has the signal come?** Measure confirmation and trigger separately (lesson 4).
5. **Does everything line up?** The checklist is the last filter (lesson 5).
6. **Is this my strategy?** Keep the rules written on one page (lesson 6).

## How to read this chapter

- Read the lessons in order. Each assumes the ideas of the one before.
- After each lesson, find three examples yourself on a demo chart.
- At the start, practice with one pair of timeframes (for example H4 and H1).
- All charts in this chapter are imaginary (synthetic) and drawn for teaching. Results in a real market can differ.
