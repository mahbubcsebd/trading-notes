# Top-Down Entry: Direction From the Big Timeframe, Timing From the Small One

You saw a nice bounce on the H1 chart and bought, but on the H4 chart price had just run into a big barrier. A small chart alone does not tell the truth.

## What you will be able to do

- **Set the direction and zone** from the bigger timeframe.
- **Place the entry** in that zone on the smaller timeframe.
- **Skip the trade** when the timeframes conflict.

## Why it matters

- **Smaller timeframes have more noise.** The bigger timeframe shows which direction really matters.
- **Entering at a big zone makes the stop small.** The small chart gives precise timing.
- **Spotting a conflict saves time.** You do not have to take every bounce.

Prerequisites: [Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis) and [Supply & Demand](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/03-supply-demand).

## What is top-down

**Top-down means first setting the direction and the important zones on the bigger timeframe, then going down to the smaller timeframe to look for the exact moment.**

Think of a map app. First the whole city (big picture), then you zoom in on the lane (small picture). If you start from the lane, you get lost.

| Step | Timeframe (example) | Job                                 |
| :--- | :------------------ | :---------------------------------- |
| 1    | H4                  | direction and zone                  |
| 2    | H1                  | behavior in the zone, signal, entry |
| 3    | (optional) M15      | a more precise trigger              |

Pick your own pair of timeframes, but the ratio is usually kept close to 4 times.

## Step by step: the top-down entry

<Steps>

### Step 1: Read the direction on the bigger timeframe

On H4, is the [trend](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) up or down? If it is a range, wait.

### Step 2: Draw the important zones

Mark the demand or supply zones on H4. These are your "areas of interest".

### Step 3: Go down to the smaller timeframe

On the H1 chart, wait for price to reach the same zone. No trades outside it.

### Step 4: Look for a signal in the zone

Is price turning after entering the zone? Look for a rejection or a [candle pattern](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context) on the smaller timeframe.

### Step 5: Stop beyond the zone

If the zone breaks, the idea is over. Put the stop there and measure the ratio first.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** H4 uptrend, demand zone, risk 1%, ratio about 1:1.8.
- **Result:** on H1, price touched the zone and turned, and went to the target.
- **Lesson:** direction and place come from the big chart, timing from the small chart. Both lined up.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                   | Why it happens                   | What to do                                |
| :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| Looking only at the small chart           | Wanting more signals             | Read the direction on the big chart first |
| Matching too many timeframes              | Looking for certainty            | Two or three are enough                   |
| Entering the opposite way near a big zone | A small bounce is tempting       | Skip the trade when they conflict         |
| Changing timeframe on each trade          | Looking for the picture you like | Fix one pair and stick to it              |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you see a conflict, saying "no trade today" is a decision. There is no
  harm in not trading every day. There is harm in taking a wrong trade.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Scalpers use a small pair of timeframes (for example M15 and M5), swing
  traders a big pair (D1 and H4). The rule is the same: the bigger one gives the
  direction.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If both timeframes are in a range:** there is no direction, and without a zone it means nothing.
- **During news:** even a zone on the big chart breaks in a jolt.
- **If the zone is old and has been touched many times:** it is weak.
- **If you add too many timeframes:** analysis paralysis.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The bigger timeframe gives the direction and zone, the smaller gives the timing and entry.
- When they conflict, skipping the trade is the decision.
- A stop beyond the zone gives a small stop and a good ratio.
- Fix your pair of timeframes and stick to it.

</KeyTakeaways>
