# Breakout Retest Entry: Wait for the Return Instead of Chasing

Price broke resistance, you jumped in and bought, and on the very next candle price was lower. Many people have had this experience. The fix is often "wait a little longer".

## What you will be able to do

- **Tell the difference** between a breakout, a retest and a pullback entry.
- **Place the entry and stop** on a retest.
- **Show why the loss stays limited** when a retest fails.

## Why it matters

- **Chasing a breakout puts the stop far away.** The higher the entry, the bigger the distance to the stop.
- **On a retest the risk is small.** The entry is near the broken level, so the stop sits close.
- **You can catch a fakeout.** If the retest does not hold, you do not enter at all, or you exit with a small loss.

Prerequisites: [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal) and [Fake Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/04-fake-breakout).

## What are breakout, retest and pullback

**A breakout is price moving through a level. A retest is price coming back to the broken level to test it. A pullback is a small opposite move inside a bigger wave.**

Think of an embankment by a river. Water spills over the embankment (breakout). After a while the water comes back and presses against the embankment to see whether it is still strong (retest). If it holds, the water moves on again.

When an old resistance becomes a new support, that is role reversal.

## Step by step: the retest entry

<Steps>

### Step 1: Find a clear level

A [resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) touched at least twice, or the top of a range. Avoid unclear levels.

### Step 2: Wait for the break

A candle must close above the level. A wick alone is not enough.

### Step 3: Do not buy, watch

If you enter on the first jolt, the stop lands far away. Watch to see whether price comes back.

### Step 4: Look for a signal on the retest

Is price touching the level and going back up? Is there a rejection candle or a small [bullish engulfing](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/04-bullish-engulfing)? Enter only if there is a signal.

### Step 5: Place the stop and target

The stop goes a little below the broken level (here 1.0875). The target goes before the next barrier. Measure the [ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) first, then work out the lot.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 30 pips, target 90 pips (1:3).
- **Result:** on the retest, price held above the level, then went past the target.
- **Lesson:** because the entry was near the broken level, the stop was small.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** the level broke, and on the retest there was a rejection-like candle.
- **Result:** price slipped back into the range and the stop was hit. The loss is 1% (as planned).
- **Why it failed:** the breakout was a fakeout. There were not enough buyers above.
- **Lesson:** even a good setup fails. Because the stop was set in advance, the loss was limited.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Why it happens             | What to do                                          |
| :------------------------------------ | :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| Entering on the first jolt            | Fear of missing out (FOMO) | Wait for the retest. If it does not come, let it go |
| Entering on the retest with no signal | Automatic thinking         | Enter after seeing a rejection candle               |
| Stop right against the level          | Greed to keep it close     | Leave a little room for noise                       |
| Expecting a retest on every breakout  | A wrong belief             | Many breakouts do not come back. Accept it          |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If the retest does not come, you missed a trade, not lost money. If the office
  bus leaves, wait for the next bus. Do not run after it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  If volume or candles shrink during the retest (momentum is fading), that is
  usually a good sign. Be careful if a big opposite candle appears.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A retest before and after news is unreliable. Follow the rules for [avoiding
  news](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo).
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If price goes straight on without a retest:** you miss the trade.
- **If the level is unclear or old:** the retest has no meaning.
- **If the range is very small:** the stop and target are close together, and the ratio is bad.
- **If the spread is high:** a close stop is easily hit.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- If you wait for the retest after a breakout, the entry is near the level and the stop stays small.
- Enter on the retest after seeing a signal (rejection), not just because price came back.
- A retest can fail, so always set the stop first.
- If the retest does not come, missing the trade is right. Chasing is not.

</KeyTakeaways>
