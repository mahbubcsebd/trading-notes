# How to Backtest: Testing the Rules on Past Charts

You do not test a new recipe by feeding the whole family. You let one person taste it first. A backtest is that tasting: running your written rules on past charts without real money.

## What you will be able to do

- **Run a manual backtest** step by step.
- **Recognize and avoid the four common traps.**
- **Write the result of each setup** in a **log**.

## Why it matters

- **Do not believe the rules work without proof.** "I think it works" and "I saw it in 40 setups" are different.
- **You can catch mistakes at no cost.** If you learn a mistake on demo or live, money goes. In a backtest only time goes.
- **The next step stands on it.** [Performance metrics](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/02-performance-metrics-forward-testing) can be measured only when you have data.

Prerequisites: [Entry, Exit and Risk Rules](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/02-entry-exit-risk-rules-ek-page-e).

## What is a backtest

**A backtest is applying fixed rules to past prices one setup at a time and writing down every result, losses included.**

The word "fixed" is the key here. If you change the rules as you go, it is not a backtest. It is a story built by looking at the past.

| Type           | What                                                            | When it is good                   |
| :------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Manual         | Moving the chart back and forward one candle at a time yourself | At the start; you learn           |
| Semi-automated | Finding signals with an indicator and counting results yourself | If the signal is measurable       |
| Automated      | Code runs it (briefly in a later chapter)                       | If the rules are fully measurable |

This lesson covers manual.

## Step by step: a manual backtest

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write the rules, then start

Entry, stop, target, risk: all fixed first. Keep a version number for the rules (v1).

### Step 2: Pick the data, before seeing results

For example the last 12 months of H1. Cover the right side of the chart (with replay or paper) so the future is not seen.

### Step 3: Move forward one candle at a time

When each candle closes, ask: "Have all the conditions of the rules matched?" If yes, mark the entry, stop and target.

### Step 4: Read the result

Did it touch the stop or the target first? If both are touched in one candle, **assume the stop first** (conservative).

### Step 5: Write it in the log

Date, direction, entry, stop, target, result (R), comment. Every setup, losses too.

### Step 6: Run until the sample is enough

No decision from a few trades. At least a few dozen trades, across different market conditions (trend, range).

</Steps>

## The four traps

| Trap           | What happens                                                                                                 | How to avoid it                                |
| :------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| Lookahead bias | Deciding by looking at candles on the right                                                                  | Cover the chart, use replay                    |
| Cherry-picking | Keeping the nice setups and dropping the ugly ones                                                           | Log it whenever the rules match, no exceptions |
| Small sample   | Confidence from five or seven trades                                                                         | A few dozen trades, at different times         |
| Ignoring costs | Leaving out [spread](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening)/commission | Subtract the cost on every trade               |

One more: **overfitting**. Changing the rules again and again so the result in the past looks good. Such rules usually lose in the future.

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Rules (v1):** buy on a pullback in an uptrend, stop 30 pips, target 60 pips.
- **While running:** the trader covered the chart and marked the setups one by one. Of five, three were +2R and two were -1R. Each was logged.
- **Result:** a net of +4R in these five. The number is small, so this is just the start; more setups are needed.
- **Lesson:** the rules were fixed before the result was known, and losses were logged too. This is an honest backtest.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** the trader picked 10 "nice" setups with the whole chart open. 9 won.
- **Result:** thinking "90% win rate", went live. In the first month 3 of 10 won.
- **Why it failed:** the setups were picked by seeing the future (lookahead), and the ugly setups were left out. That is not a backtest. It is a selected collection.
- **Lesson:** if the result looks too good, doubt it first. Do not celebrate.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens              | What to do                           |
| :--------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Changing the rules while running   | Seeing a bad result         | A new version, start again           |
| Only one market condition          | Picking a convenient period | Keep both trend and range            |
| Not keeping a log                  | Thinking you'll remember    | Write right after each setup         |
| Leaving out costs                  | It seems complicated        | Subtract a fixed cost on every trade |
| Stop and target both in one candle | Ambiguity                   | Assume the stop first                |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Whether the first backtest gives a good or a bad result, run the same rules on
  another period. If the two periods agree, confidence rises.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  A backtest is no guarantee of the future. The past market and spread can be
  different in the future. That is why the forward test in the next lesson is
  essential.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the rules are unclear:** rules of the "if it feels good" kind cannot be tested.
- **If the structure of the market changes:** the result of an earlier period may not apply in the new situation.
- **In discretionary trading:** if every trade needs judgment, the result depends on the person, so comparison is hard.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Backtest = fixed rules, past prices, every result in the log.
- Four traps: lookahead, cherry-picking, small sample, ignoring costs.
- If one candle touches two levels, assume the stop first.
- Doubt a result that looks very good.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

With your plan's rules (v1), log at least 20 setups on the chart of the last six months. Run it with the chart covered, and subtract costs.
