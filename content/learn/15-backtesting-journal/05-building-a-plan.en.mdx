# Building a Plan: Making Plan v2 From Data

Your [plan v1](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/01-trading-plan-template) is written, backtested, the journal is running, and a review has been done. Now the question: how do you put what the data says into the plan, without fooling yourself?

## What you will be able to do

- **Pick one change** from a review and write plan v2.
- **Tell safe changes from risky ones.**
- **Set the forward test rules** after a change.

## Why it matters

- **The plan is not fixed, but you need rules for changing it.** If you change it at any time, the data gets mixed.
- **A change built by looking at the past risks overfitting.** The [traps of backtesting](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest) apply here too.
- **Slow improvement lasts.** When many changes happen at once, you cannot tell which one worked.

Prerequisites: [Reviewing Trades](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/04-reviewing-trades).

## What is a plan version

**A plan version (v1, v2, …) is a fixed form of the plan written with a date, in which the reason for each change and the condition for checking it are written.**

| Kind of change                | Risk   | Example                | Check                               |
| :---------------------------- | :----- | :--------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Removing (setup, time)        | Low    | Close the weak setup B | forward test, same rules            |
| Tightening (cutting risk)     | Low    | Risk from 1% to 0.5%   | Start at a small risk               |
| Adding new (a new setup)      | Medium | A new pattern          | A separate backtest + forward       |
| Tuning numbers (stop, target) | High   | Target from 2R to 2.7R | Big risk of overfitting, be careful |

Usually **removing and tightening** are safe, because you do not have to guess anything new. Tuning numbers is the most dangerous.

In the past, v2 looks good. But this is a **guess**, not proof, because we chose by looking at the result.

## Step by step: plan v2

<Steps>

### Step 1: Pick one change

One, from the result of the review. For example: "close setup B."

### Step 2: Write the reason and the expectation

Why the change, and what result you expect. For example: "B averages -0.4R, A +0.8R."

### Step 3: Write the condition for checking first

How many trades you will run forward, and what result keeps v2 and what sends you back. For example: 20 trades, average R positive.

### Step 4: Run at a small risk

In the forward test, half the risk. Match the numbers following the [Metrics](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/02-performance-metrics-forward-testing).

### Step 5: Save with the version and date

Do not throw v1 away. You need it if you have to go back.

### Step 6: If the result matches, return the risk to normal

As part of the regular review. If it does not match, go back to v1 or investigate anew.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **v1:** two setups, combined +4R (20 trades).
- **Review:** A +8R, B -4R.
- **v2:** only A. 20 forward trades at half risk, condition: average R positive.
- **Result:** forward average +0.3R. The condition was met, and the risk went back to normal.
- **Lesson:** write the condition before the change, then check on unseen data.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** in the review the trader saw that a target of 2.7R instead of 2R gave a better result in the past. At the same time they tuned the stop and the time too.
- **Result:** live, the result was many times worse than the backtest. It was not clear which change caused it.
- **Why it failed:** many changes at once and number tuning are both doors to overfitting.
- **Lesson:** one change at a time, and number tuning last, with care.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                           | Why it happens                  | What to do                    |
| :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------ | :---------------------------- |
| Starting v2 without checking      | Confidence from the past result | A forward test is a must      |
| Setting the check condition later | Choosing to suit the result     | Write it first                |
| Many changes at once              | The urge to fix fast            | One at a time                 |
| Deleting v1                       | Attention only on the new       | Save the versions             |
| Frequent changes                  | Impatience                      | At the set time of the review |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Keep a "change log" at the top of the plan: date, what changed, why, the
  result. At the end of the year this is your most valuable lesson.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Some changes only cut risk or tighten the rules. These need less checking, but
  still write them in the log.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If there is little data:** the change stands on luck. Collect more trades.
- **If the type of market changes:** a review of old data does not apply to the new situation.
- **Driven by emotion:** changing the plan after breaking the [stopping rules](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/05-kokhon-trading-bondho-korbo) is risky.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The plan has versions, with dates and reasons.
- Safe changes: removing and tightening.
- Tuning numbers is the riskiest.
- Write the checking condition first, and run a forward test.

</KeyTakeaways>
