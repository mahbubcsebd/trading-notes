# Backtesting and Journal: Building the Plan on Evidence

A new medicine is tested before it reaches the market, the results are written down, and it is checked again on different patients. Your trading rules deserve the same path: first test, then calculate, then check on data you have not seen.

## What are backtesting and a journal

**Backtesting means running the rules on past prices and measuring the result. A journal means writing down the result and reason of your real trades. Together the two make the plan evidence-based.**

This chapter answers one question: **"How will I know whether my rules really work, and how do I improve them step by step?"**

## Why learn this now

- **You have a written plan, but no evidence.** The [Trading Plan](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/00-overview) gives the rules, and this chapter tests them.
- **Guesses and data are different.** Taking risk on "it seems to work" is expensive.
- **The only way to separate luck from skill** is enough data.
- **Before learning advanced techniques, you need the skill of checking.**

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Run a manual backtest** while avoiding the traps.
- **Work out win rate, expectancy, profit factor and drawdown.**
- **Check a backtest** with a **forward test**.
- **Build a journal template** and fill it in every day.
- **Review** and recognize weak setups.
- **Write plan v2** safely.

## How the lessons join together

A cycle:

1. **Backtest.** Run the rules on the past and log the result (lesson 1).
2. **Measure.** Expectancy, drawdown; if it looks good, a forward test (lesson 2).
3. **Journal on live or demo.** Every trade in the same format (lesson 3).
4. **Review.** Split the data and find one weakness (lesson 4).
5. **Plan v2.** One change, with a condition for checking it. Then back to 1 (lesson 5).
