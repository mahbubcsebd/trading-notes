# Performance Metrics and Forward Testing: Measuring and Checking the Result

If an exam sheet says only "pass", you learn nothing. You need to see the marks, the result by subject, and the comparison with the earlier exam. It is the same for a backtest result: just "it made a profit" is not enough.

## What you will be able to do

- **Work out win rate, expectancy, profit factor and max drawdown.**
- **Understand what forward testing is and why.**
- **Read the gap** between the backtest and forward results.

## Why it matters

- **Win rate alone misleads.** A profit is possible with 45% wins, and a loss with 70%.
- **Without knowing the drawdown, patience runs out.** If you know it beforehand, you will not drop the plan in a bad period.
- **Confidence in a backtest comes from a forward test.**

Prerequisites: [How to Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest) and [Expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy).

## The main measures

**Performance metrics are a few numbers worked out from the list of trades that say how profitable and how risky the rules are.**

| Measure               | Calculation                                           | Example with 40 trades (imaginary) |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Win rate              | winning trades ÷ total trades                         | 18 ÷ 40 = 45%                      |
| Average win / loss    | in R                                                  | +2R / -1R                          |
| Expectancy            | (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss) | (0.45 × 2) − (0.55 × 1) = +0.35R   |
| Profit factor         | total profit ÷ total loss                             | 36R ÷ 22R ≈ 1.64                   |
| Max drawdown          | the biggest fall from a peak (%)                      | about 2.0% (in this sequence)      |
| Longest losing streak | the longest run of losses                             | 2                                  |

Expectancy is how many R you can expect per trade on average. If it is positive, the rules have a chance of surviving, but with a small sample the number is unstable.

## Step by step: working out the metrics

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write all trades in R

Not money, but R. For example: +2, -1, -1, +2. Comparison gets easy.

### Step 2: Count the wins and losses

Win rate = wins ÷ total. It says nothing alone; look at it with the next step.

### Step 3: Average win and average loss

In R. Then put them into the expectancy formula.

### Step 4: Draw the equity curve

The account after each trade. The biggest fall from a peak is the max drawdown.

### Step 5: Run a forward test

After the backtest, new trades by the same rules in a new, unseen market (demo or live at a small risk). Put the results side by side and compare.

</Steps>

## What is forward testing

**Forward testing means running the rules chosen in the backtest in a new, unseen market and looking at the result.** It catches the mistake of arranging rules by looking at the past (overfitting).

- **Demo account:** no risk, but the execution is not real.
- **Small live:** minimal risk, but with the real spread and slippage.

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Backtest:** 40 trades, net +14R, max drawdown about 2%.
- **Forward:** by the same rules, 15 trades in the next few weeks, net +4R.
- **Result:** the pace is lower than the backtest, but in the same direction.
- **Lesson:** when the two results point the same way, confidence rises. Do not expect an exact match.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What happened:** the backtest was +14R, but in forward, 15 trades gave a net of 0R.
- **Why it may have failed:** the rules were overfit to the past, the market changed, or the sample is still small (15).
- **What to do:** cut the risk, collect more trades, and look for a hidden bias in the backtest. Do not hurry to change the rules.
- **Lesson:** a gap is a signal for investigation, not for panic.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                       | Why it happens             | What to do                                  |
| :---------------------------- | :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| Looking only at win rate      | It is easy and attractive  | Look at expectancy and profit factor too    |
| Ignoring drawdown             | Eyes only on profit        | Know the max drawdown and the losing streak |
| A decision on a small sample  | Hurry                      | More trades, at different times             |
| Skipping the forward test     | Confidence in the backtest | Check at least at a small risk              |
| Changing the rules in forward | Seeing a bad result        | A new version, forward again                |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If you know the backtest's max drawdown, prepare yourself mentally for one and
  a half to two times that live. The real drawdown is often deeper than the
  backtest's (a general guide, not a certainty).
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">

## When this does not work

- **If trade sizes are unequal:** if it can't be measured in R, comparison may be wrong. Keep the risk fixed each time.
- **If the sample is very small:** the numbers cannot be separated from luck.
- **If the type of market changes:** the old metrics do not apply to the new market.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Win rate alone is not enough; look at expectancy, profit factor and max drawdown.
- Write all trades in R.
- A forward test checks the extra confidence from a backtest.
- If there is a gap, investigate, do not hurry.

</KeyTakeaways>
