# Trading Plan and System: Written Rules Instead of Emotion

In the earlier chapters you learned separate parts: setups, risk, stops, mindset. The engine, wheels and steering are lying apart. This chapter joins them into a car that runs: your own **trading system**.

## What is a trading plan

**A trading plan is one page of rules written in advance, and a system is the habit of running by those rules every day.**

This chapter answers one question: **"How do I write down in advance what, when and how much I trade, and when I stop?"**

## Why learn this now

- **Separate pieces of knowledge are no use until they are joined.** [Risk](/en/learn/08-risk-management/00-overview), [Entry](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/00-overview) and [Psychology](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/00-overview) come together here.
- **Without written rules, testing is impossible.** The next chapter, [Backtesting](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest), runs on the rules.
- **Emotion is stronger than rules, if you have to remember the rules.** If they are on paper, you do not have to remember.
- **Before arranging advanced techniques, you need a base.**

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Fill in a one-page plan template.**
- **Write entry, exit and risk rules** so that they can be measured.
- **Build a three-level pre-trade checklist.**
- **Arrange a daily and weekly routine.**
- **Write the rules for stopping** in advance.

## How the lessons join together

The flow of decisions in a day:

1. **What does the plan say?** The six-part page in front of you (lesson 1).
2. **Has the setup matched?** Match it against the rules for entry, stop and target (lesson 2).
3. **Pass at three levels?** The market, the calculation, your own state (lesson 3).
4. **Inside the window?** Trade by the routine (lesson 4).
5. **Have I reached the limit?** Then stop (lesson 5).
6. **At the end of the week?** Review, and whether the plan is still right (lesson 4).

## How to read this chapter

- Read in order, and put the practice of each lesson into your own plan. At the end you will have one page ready.
- Test whether someone else can understand the plan by looking at it.
- Do not try to make the first version perfect. Write v1 and run it, and v2 comes from the review.
- All charts in this chapter are imaginary (synthetic), to explain the idea. They are not real results.
