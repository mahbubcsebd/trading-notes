# When to Stop Trading: Write the Stopping Rules in Advance

Everyone learns when to enter. When to **stop** is learned after a loss, when the head is no longer cool. So the stopping rule has to be written beforehand, with a cool head.

## What you will be able to do

- **Write three kinds of stopping rule:** by **day, week and mental state**.
- **Decide what to do** after stopping.
- **Understand the difference** in results between having the rule and not having it.

## Why it matters

- **Judgment blurs after a loss.** [Revenge trading](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/04-fomo-revenge-trading) starts right here.
- **A small loss becomes big by your own hand.** Often the cause is not the market's fault, but not being able to stop.
- **Saving the account is the first job.** When the [drawdown](/en/learn/08-risk-management/04-drawdown-rules) is deep, coming back is hard.

Prerequisites: [Daily and Weekly Limits](/en/learn/08-risk-management/05-daily-weekly-limits) and [Accepting Losses](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/03-accepting-losses).

## What is a stopping rule

**A stopping rule is a condition written in advance: when it is met, you will not trade again that day or that week, whatever the result.**

Think of a red card in football. When the referee decides, the player does not argue. He leaves the field. Your rule is your referee.

| Type          | Condition (example)                    | What after                                              |
| :------------ | :------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| Daily loss    | 2 losses a day, or -2% in total        | Close the platform, write the journal, done for the day |
| Weekly loss   | -5% in a week                          | No trading the rest of the week, a review sitting       |
| Rule breaking | One trade outside the plan in a day    | Closed for that day                                     |
| Mental state  | Anger, tiredness, the urge for revenge | Closed for that day, a break                            |
| Outside cause | Big news or an uncertain market        | No trade in the window                                  |

The numbers are examples. Set them by your own [risk limit](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).

## Step by step: writing the stopping rules

<Steps>

### Step 1: The daily limit

A multiple of the risk (such as 2%) or a number of trades (such as 2 losses). Whichever happens first.

### Step 2: The weekly limit

2 to 3 times the daily limit. When it is touched, the rest of the week is review.

### Step 3: Signals of your own state

Write three signals, such as: my hand is shaking, I want to get even, I cannot take my eyes off the screen. If you get even one, stop.

### Step 4: The way to stop

Close the platform, log out of the mobile app, leave the room. If the way is set in advance, stopping is easy.

### Step 5: The condition for returning

When to start again: the next day, after completing the journal, after passing the [checklist](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/03-pre-trade-checklist).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Rule:** stop after two losses in a day.
- **While running:** the first two trades are both -1%. The trader closes the platform and writes the journal.
- **Result:** the day ends at -1.99% (9,801 from 10,000).
- **Lesson:** the bad day stayed small and planned. The next day it was possible to return with full attention.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

<Chart
  id="plan-stop-ignored-01"
  height={380}
  caption="ACCOUNT, per trade (educational, imaginary example): there is no stopping rule. After two losses the trader did not stop and raised the risk too: -1, -1, -2, -3, -4. The day ends at about -10.6%."
/>

- **What happened:** after the first two losses, the thought "I'll win it back today". The risk doubles on the next trade, then goes higher.
- **Result:** -1, -1, -2, -3, -4 (%). With compounding, from 10,000 to about 8,944, which is about -10.6%.
- **Why it failed:** the stopping condition was not written, and each loss made the next trade bigger. This is a mark of revenge trading.
- **Lesson:** two days that began with the same two losses ended so differently, only because of the stopping rule.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Why it happens                | What to do                              |
| :----------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| Going on, saying "one more trade"          | The wish to win the loss back | At the limit, close the platform        |
| Moving the limit once you pass it          | Emotion                       | Change the limit only in the review     |
| Extra trades on a winning day              | Overconfidence                | Keep a limit on profit too (such as 3R) |
| Keeping only a money limit                 | Ignoring the state of mind    | Write the mental signals too            |
| Closing and then opening the chart to look | Curiosity                     | Close the chart after the window        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you reach the limit, do one thing decided in advance: go for a walk or
  write the journal. If there is free time, the pull to trade returns.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  This is educational writing. If trading is putting you in deep trouble with
  sleep, relationships or financial security, take a break and talk to someone
  you trust, or get help from a suitable professional.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the limit is so close that it hits every day:** then the problem is not the limit, but the strategy or the size of the risk. See [Position sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).
- **If the limit is so far that it never hits:** then it is not a rule. It is decoration.
- **If there is an open position:** the stopping rule blocks new trades. The stop of a running trade is already there, under a separate rule.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Write the stopping rules in advance: daily, weekly, mental signals.
- Set the way to stop in advance too.
- A day that begins with the same loss can be small or big because of the rule.
- Change the limit only in the review.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

In your plan, write three stopping conditions: one daily, one weekly, one mental. Then add the way to stop and the condition for returning.
