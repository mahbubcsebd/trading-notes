# Pre-Trade Checklist: Market, Calculation and Your Own State

The [Entry Model Checklist](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/05-entry-model-checklist) checks only the chart. But even a good setup can lose if today's limit is used up or you are tired. In this lesson you will learn to extend the checklist to three levels.

## What you will be able to do

- **Divide the checklist into three levels:** **market, calculation and mind**.
- **Write yes/no questions** at each level.
- **Drop the trade** if you get one "no".

## Why it matters

- **A chart signal alone is not enough.** The risk state of the day and your mind are part of the decision too.
- **Mistakes of hurry fall.** [Overtrading](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/02-overtrading) often starts with "let me just try once".
- **It is quick.** Matching a ready list takes a minute.

Prerequisites: [Entry, Exit and Risk Rules](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/02-entry-exit-risk-rules-ek-page-e).

## What is the three-level checklist

**A pre-trade checklist is a list of yes/no questions matched right before entry. One "no" means no trade today.**

| Level       | Question                                                                                           | Example answer  |
| :---------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------- |
| Market      | Has the plan's setup fully matched? Is the path to the target clear? No big news in the next hour? | Yes / Yes / Yes |
| Calculation | Am I within today's loss limit? Did I work out the lot by the formula? Was the stop placed first?  | Yes / Yes / Yes |
| Your state  | Enough sleep? No urge for revenge after a loss? Am I inside the window?                            | Yes / Yes / Yes |

Keep the questions few: 8 to 10 in total. More than that is a bother to match, so they will get skipped.

If all three levels pass, enter. Even if the chart is lovely, stop if one level says "no".

## Step by step: building your own checklist

<Steps>

### Step 1: The market level

Your setup's conditions, [whether the path is clear](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/04-confirmation-vs-trigger), and the [news schedule](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo).

### Step 2: The calculation level

How much loss today, how much of the [daily limit](/en/learn/08-risk-management/05-daily-weekly-limits) is left, whether the lot is right.

### Step 3: The level of your own state

Sleep, mood, the effect of the previous trade. One question is mandatory: "Would I take this trade even in my calmest state?"

### Step 4: Print it on one page

A tick box beside each. The habit of ticking on paper creates a mental pause.

### Step 5: When a "no" comes, write it down

Which question gave the "no", briefly in the journal. Later you will see which question stops you most.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Market:** an H4 downtrend, a rejection at the last lower high, the path clear up to 1.0842, no news.
- **Calculation:** no loss today, risk 1%, the stop placed first.
- **Your state:** sleep fine, inside the window.
- **Result:** sell 1.0912, stop 1.0940, target 1.0842. The target was hit, +2.5R.
- **Lesson:** in the trade that passed the three levels, the decision was made with a cool head.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** sell 1.0930, stop 1.0955, target 1.0870. But on the chart in the middle there was clear support at 1.0905.
- **Result:** price hit 1.0905, turned and touched the stop, -1R.
- **Why it failed:** the market-level question "is the path clear?" had the answer "no", yet the trade was taken. The greed was for the 1:2.4 ratio.
- **Lesson:** one "no" is reason enough. The reality of the path matters more than the ratio on paper.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                          | Why it happens             | What to do                                |
| :------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| Checking only the chart          | The chart is attractive    | Add the calculation and your-state levels |
| Turning a "no" into a "maybe"    | Pain of giving up a chance | One "no" = skip, no exceptions            |
| A list that is too long          | Trying to catch everything | Limit it to 8–10 questions                |
| Ticking after the trade          | Pretending to follow rules | Tick before entry, or no order            |
| Avoiding the your-state question | Shame/discomfort           | Nobody will see it, so answer honestly    |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Mark one question at each level as "fixed" (such as the daily limit). If it is
  a "no", there is no trade even if everything else is "yes".
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  If you keep count of the "no" answers on the checklist, you will see at which
  level you are stopped most. Put the
  [journal](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/05-journaling-habit)'s attention
  there.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If ticking becomes a habit:** if you give "yes" to all without thinking, the checklist means nothing. Now and then write the questions in new words.
- **In a fast market:** the chance goes in minutes, so the checklist has to be memorized and short.
- **If the plan itself is wrong:** even a perfect checklist on wrong rules does harm. You need a [backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest).

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Three levels: market, calculation, your own state.
- One "no" means no trade today.
- 8–10 yes/no questions are enough.
- Tick before entry, not after.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write your three-level checklist and print it. Before each of the next five trades, tick it, and count at which question the "no" came.
