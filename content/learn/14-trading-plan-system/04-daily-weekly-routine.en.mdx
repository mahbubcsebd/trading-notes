# Daily and Weekly Routine: Fitting the Plan Into a Schedule

A good plan on paper is not enough. What you do at which time of the week must be fixed too. In [Trading Routine](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/06-routine) you learned the three parts of a day. Here a weekly level is added on top.

## What you will be able to do

- **Write the daily and weekly** tasks separately.
- **Decide what the weekly review** will look at.
- **Judge the plan** by the **curve of the week, not the result of one day**.

## Why it matters

- **One day's result is noise.** Several losses in a row are normal. Without seeing the bigger picture, the plan changes for no reason.
- **Learning builds up.** Sitting down once at the end of the week catches the same mistake.
- **When the time is fixed, decision fatigue falls.**

Prerequisites: [Journaling Habit](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/05-journaling-habit) and [Pre-Trade Checklist](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/03-pre-trade-checklist).

## A two-level routine

**The daily routine handles today's trades. The weekly routine checks the result of the whole week and keeps the plan right.**

| Level  | When                          | What to do                                                                         |
| :----- | :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Daily  | Before the window             | Calendar, levels, write today's risk limit                                         |
| Daily  | Inside the window             | Only setups that pass the checklist                                                |
| Daily  | After the window              | Fill the journal, screenshots                                                      |
| Weekly | At the weekend, market closed | The list of all trades, rate of following the rules, total R, the most-broken rule |
| Weekly | In the same sitting           | Next week's news and levels, and one goal for improvement                          |

The weekly review is done **while the market is closed**. Reviewing in an open market brings back the pull to trade.

Each candle is one trade. There are red candles here and there, yet the curve is up. The weekly review looks at this bigger picture.

## Step by step: the weekly review

<Steps>

### Step 1: List all trades

From the journal: date, setup, result in R, and whether the plan was followed (yes/no).

### Step 2: Work out two numbers

Total R, and the rate of following the plan (for example 8 out of 10). Look at the rate of following before profit and loss.

### Step 3: Find the most-broken rule

By counting the tags in the [journal](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/03-journal-template). This rule is the goal for next week.

### Step 4: Prepare for next week

News, important levels, holidays. A [news](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo) list on paper.

### Step 5: Do not change the plan, only write it down

It is not right to change rules on the result of one week. Keep the idea of a change in a "think about later" list.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **The week:** ten trades, risk 1%, result +3%. Two trades were outside the plan.
- **Review:** the rate of following was 80%. Of the two outside trades, one won and one lost.
- **Decision:** the winning outside trade was not called "good". Both got the same tag.
- **Lesson:** looking at the rate of following first, rather than the result, separates luck and skill.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** four losses in the first two days. On the third day the trader changed the plan's stop rule.
- **Result:** next week the new rule lost two of three trades in the same market. The data of two weeks got mixed, and it was not clear what was working.
- **Why it failed:** the plan was changed on two days of results, outside a review.
- **Lesson:** the time to change the plan is fixed: the weekend review or the monthly review. Not in the middle of the week.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Why it happens        | What to do                                    |
| :------------------------------------ | :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Not doing the review                  | Tiredness/fear        | Put 30 minutes in the calendar at the weekend |
| Looking only at profit and loss       | Numbers are easy      | Write the rate of following too               |
| Changing the plan on one day's result | Emotion               | Change only in the review                     |
| Reviewing in an open market           | Saving time           | Review when the market is closed              |
| Not looking at next week's news       | Attention on the past | Add preparation to the review                 |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  At the end of the weekend review, write just one goal, for example "this week
  no entry without a retest". More than one goal does not work.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  At the end of the month, put the weekly reviews side by side. The pattern
  shows, such as which week had the most rule-breaking.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the number of trades is very small:** with two or three trades the curve of the week means nothing. Look at several weeks together.
- **If the journal is incomplete:** the review turns into a guess for lack of data.
- **In swing trading:** the weekly level is the real one, and the daily level is light.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The daily routine handles today, and the weekly routine keeps the plan right.
- Review while the market is closed.
- Look at the rate of following the plan before profit and loss.
- Change the plan only in the review, not in the middle of the week.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. What is the difference between the daily and weekly routine?
2. When do you do the review, and why?
3. Why look at the rate of following before profit?
4. When is it right to change the plan?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** The daily one handles today's trades. The weekly one checks the result of the whole week and the following of the rules.

**2.** While the market is closed, so there is no pull to trade.
