# Expectancy: How Much Your System Makes Per Trade on Average

Two friends each made 30 trades in the same month. One won 43% and was in profit. The other won 63% and was at a loss. What is the difference? The difference is not in the win rate. It is in the expectancy.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what an R-multiple is and why it is useful.
- **Calculate** expectancy.
- **Check a system** by combining win rate and ratio.
- **Understand** why losing streaks come even with positive expectancy.

## Why it matters

- **Win rate alone gives a false picture.** Without [Risk:Reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward), the win rate means nothing.
- **Expectancy measures average behavior, not the future.** It tells you what to expect per trade over the long run.
- **It steadies your decisions.** It is the basis for deciding whether to leave a system during a bad run.

Prerequisites: the Risk:Reward ratio and [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).

## What is expectancy

**Expectancy is the average profit or loss you can expect per trade from a system, measured in units of risk (R).**

Think of the opposite of a lottery. A lottery ticket has an average loss, so most people lose. If your system's average is positive, then playing with risk makes sense.

**R-multiple:** the result of a trade divided by its risk. If you risk 1% and gain 2%, that is +2R. If the stop is hit, it is −1R.

Formula:

**Expectancy (R) = (win rate × average win in R) − (loss rate × average loss in R)**

Calculation: 0.433 × 2 − 0.567 × 1 = +0.3R. That is 0.3 times the risk per trade on average. Over 30 trades that is about +9R, which at 1% risk is about 9% (here the account ends at about 10,904).

Notice that this curve does not go straight up. In the middle there are several losses in a row (here, at most 4 in a row). **Positive expectancy does not mean there are no losing streaks.**

## Step by step: your own expectancy

<Steps>

### Step 1: Take at least 20-30 trades

From a backtest, demo or live. The more, the more reliable.

### Step 2: Write each one in R

A stop-out is −1R. A gain of twice the risk is +2R. Leave out costs (spread, commission) for now.

### Step 3: Find the win rate and averages

Wins ÷ total, the average R of the wins, the average R of the losses.

### Step 4: Put it in the formula

Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss).

### Step 5: Evaluate

If it is positive and still positive after costs, the system survives. If it is negative, it must change.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **Win rate:** about 43%. **Average win:** 2R. **Average loss:** 1R.
- **Expectancy:** +0.3R.
- **Result:** about +9R over 30 trades.
- **Lesson:** profit is possible even with fewer wins, if the wins are bigger than the losses.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade. The result depends on the sequence.)

- **What was visible:** most trades win, so everything seems fine.
- **Calculation:** 0.633 × 0.5 − 0.367 × 1 = −0.05R.
- **Result:** over 30 trades the account is at about 9,843. A small loss, but it gets worse after counting costs (spread, commission).
- **Lesson:** even with a high win rate, small wins and big losses make a system unprofitable.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                             | Why it happens                | What to do                       |
| :---------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Deciding on a tiny sample           | Trusting a few trades         | At least 20-30, more if possible |
| Leaving out costs                   | Keeping the formula simple    | Add spread and commission in R   |
| Looking only at win rate            | It is an easy number          | Look at expectancy               |
| Treating expectancy as a guarantee  | Average seems to mean certain | Allow for streaks and drawdown   |
| Changing the system again and again | Little patience in a bad run  | Write review rules first         |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write each trade in your journal in R, not in money. Money changes with the
  risk, while R keeps the quality of the system.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Even in a system with positive expectancy, 4-5 losses in a row can be normal.
  So write the [Drawdown Rules](/en/learn/08-risk-management/04-drawdown-rules)
  in advance.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The expectancy from a backtest almost always comes out a little lower live
  (slippage, emotion, a changed market). Keep an extra safety margin.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the sample is small:** the average means nothing, and luck plays a part.
- **When the market changes:** the earlier average will not stay the same in the future.
- **When risk differs from trade to trade:** it is easy to measure in R, not in money.
- **When results are not consistent:** a few huge wins can pull the average up, which is not reliable.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Expectancy = (win rate × average win in R) − (loss rate × average loss in R).
- A 43% win rate can give +0.3R, and a 63% win rate can give −0.05R.
- Even with positive expectancy, losing streaks come.
- Do not trust the numbers without enough samples and costs.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write your last 20-30 trades in R. Find the win rate, average win, average loss and expectancy. Then take off the costs (about how much in R per trade) and look again.
