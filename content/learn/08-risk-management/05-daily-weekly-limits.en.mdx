# Daily and Weekly Loss Limits: Decide in Advance When to Stop

You lost two trades this morning. Your mind says "this time I will win it back." The decision you make in this moment sets the result of the day. And the best decision was already made the night before.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what daily and weekly loss limits are.
- **Set numbers** for yourself.
- **Write down in advance** what to do when you hit a limit.
- **Recognize from a chart** the damage of a revenge trade.

## Why it matters

- **One bad morning can ruin the whole week.** A limit stops that.
- **Your state of mind changes after a loss.** Trades taken then are usually more rushed.
- **Risk per trade is not enough.** Ten trades at 1% each is 10%, and without a limit the number keeps growing.

Prerequisites: [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade) and [Drawdown Rules](/en/learn/08-risk-management/04-drawdown-rules).

## What is a loss limit

**A loss limit is a limit you set in advance: the most you can lose in a given period (a day or a week) before you stop trading.**

Think of an electric fuse. When too much current comes through, the fuse blows and the house does not burn. A limit is your account's fuse.

| Limit  | Common example (change it to suit you)        |
| :----- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Daily  | 2% to 3% of the account, or 3 losses in a row |
| Weekly | 5% to 6% of the account                       |

These numbers are only common examples, not instructions. With 1% risk, a daily limit of 3% means at most three full losses.

Seeing the rally afterward, you may think "if I had stayed a little longer." But there was no way to know that in advance, and that is exactly what the limit is for.

## Step by step: setting the limits

<Steps>

### Step 1: Set the daily limit

2-3 times the risk per trade is a common start. For example, with 1% risk, the daily limit is 3%.

### Step 2: Set the weekly limit

About double the daily limit. For example, 6%.

### Step 3: Write the response

When you hit the limit: close the platform, write your journal, and no more trading for the rest of the day or week.

### Step 4: Write how to handle open trades

If a trade is open at the limit, either close it, or leave it with its stop where it is. Pick one and write it down.

### Step 5: Write when you come back

Start fresh the next day, but not to get revenge for the previous day's loss.

</Steps>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens             | What to do                       |
| :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Keeping the limit in your head, not on paper | It seems easy              | Write it in your plan            |
| Saying "one last trade"                      | Greed to win the loss back | At the limit, close the platform |
| Moving the limit once you hit it             | Emotion                    | Set it in a calm period          |
| Watching only daily, not weekly              | Not keeping a weekly count | Track both                       |
| Not counting open trades                     | Forgetting                 | Add up the total risk            |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you hit the limit, log out of the platform or turn off notifications.
  Building a barrier is easier than relying on willpower.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  There is a limit on winning days too: many people stop once they reach their
  target profit. That is your own choice, but at the start the loss limit
  matters more.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A limit set by "feeling" will not work. Write it as a number: "at most 3% a
  day".
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If you do not follow the limit:** having a rule and following it are different.
- **If there is a gap on an open position:** the loss can go past the limit.
- **If the limit is too close:** you will have to stop again and again over normal losses. Then rethink the risk or the limit.
- **If you follow only the number and do not understand the reason:** without a journal after the limit, there is no learning.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A loss limit is the most you can lose in a day or a week, set in advance.
- Write the number as a figure and keep it in your plan.
- At the limit, stop, write your journal, and come back on a new day.
- Without a limit, a revenge trade drags one day into a week.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write your daily and weekly limits based on your risk percentage. Then look at last week's trades and say which day the limit would have triggered, and what you actually did then.

## Quiz

1. With 1% risk and a 3% daily limit, how many full losses at most in one day?
2. What do you do when you hit the limit?
3. Why is "one last trade" dangerous?
4. Why is the weekly limit kept bigger than the daily one?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** Three.

**2.** Close the platform, write your journal, and do not trade for the rest of the time.

**3.** The decision then comes from the urge to recover lost money, not from a setup.

**4.** A week has several days. It lets the week survive even after a few bad days, as long as it does not pass the limit.

</details>
