# Risk per Trade: How Much Is Safe to Lose on One Trade

Your analysis was right, but you still lost four trades in a row. Now the question is: how much of the account is left? The answer is not decided by your analysis. It is decided by how much risk you took **before** the trade.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain in your own words** what risk per trade is.
- **Work out the risk in money** from a percentage of the account.
- **Show by calculation** the difference between small and large risk in a losing streak.
- **Set a risk per trade limit** for yourself.

## Why it matters

- **Losses are unavoidable.** No setup works every time. How much you lose is in your hands.
- **Risk is the only thing you can control.** You do not know where the market will go, but you know how much you will lose.
- **Risk is wasted in two steps.** First a big loss, then a bigger risk to win it back.

Prerequisites: [Pip, Lot, Leverage, Margin](/en/learn/01-foundations/03-pips-lots-leverage-margin) and the idea of a stop loss.

## What is risk per trade

**Risk per trade is the part of your account you lose if the stop loss is hit on one trade.** It is usually stated as a percentage of the account (for example 1%).

Think of a business budget, not a gambling table. A shopkeeper risks only a small part of the capital each day, not all of it. One bad day of sales does not close the shop.

Formula: **risk (money) = account × risk percent**.

(Imaginary example) With an account of 10,000 and risk of 1%, the maximum risk on one trade is 100.

## Step by step: setting your own risk

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write down the account amount

Count only the money set aside for trading. Not the rent money.

### Step 2: Pick a percentage

The usual range for beginners is 0.5% to 1%. Many experienced traders also go no higher than 2%. This is not a rule, just common practice.

### Step 3: Convert to money

10,000 × 1% = 100. This is the maximum loss on each trade.

### Step 4: Stop first, lot second

Set the stop by looking at structure. Then work out the lot size from the risk and the stop.

### Step 5: Write it down

In your trading plan, write: "No trade risks more than X% of the account."

</Steps>

## Why a small percentage: the losing streak calculation

<Chart
  id="risk-streak-equity-01"
  height={440}
  caption="Account (educational calculation): 10 losses in a row. The candles show the account risking 1%, the orange line shows the account risking 5%. The calculation is simple multiplication, not real data."
/>

| Risk | After 10 losses (from 10,000) | Gain needed to get back |
| :--- | :---------------------------- | :---------------------- |
| 1%   | about 9,044                   | about 10.6%             |
| 5%   | about 5,987                   | about 67%               |

**Notice that the way back from a loss is not straight.** If you lose 40%, a 40% gain does not bring you back. You need about 67%. More in the [Drawdown Rules](/en/learn/08-risk-management/04-drawdown-rules) lesson.

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The EURUSD chart above:

- **Set in advance:** risk 100 (1%).
- **Entry:** buy 1.0900. **Stop:** 1.0860. **Target:** 1.0980.
- **Result:** price did not touch the stop. It reached the target. Profit about 200.
- **Lesson:** the risk was right even when winning, so the result could be handled calmly.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Trader A risks 5% on each trade. After 10 losses in a row, the account falls to about 5,987. Trader B risks 1%, and the account is about 9,044.

- **What was visible:** both have the same analysis, setups and loss streak.
- **Result:** A needs a gain of about 67% to recover. B needs about 10.6%.
- **Why it was wrong:** A thought "this setup is good" and took a big risk. Good setups lose too.
- **Lesson:** set the size of the risk by thinking about losing, not by hoping to win.

(Synthetic educational calculation. No result is guaranteed.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Why it happens               | What to do                        |
| :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Raising risk because "this one is certain"  | Overconfidence               | Same percentage on every trade    |
| Doubling risk on the next trade to win back | Cannot accept the loss       | Keep the risk fixed               |
| Thinking in lots, not in money              | It seems easier              | Money first, then lots            |
| Moving the stop farther away                | Not wanting to admit loss    | Move the stop only to cut risk    |
| Treating margin as risk                     | Margin seems to be the limit | The limit is the stop, not margin |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If you are new, start with 0.5%. The goal for the first few months is not
  profit. It is building the habit of following the rules.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  When the account grows or shrinks, the money at risk changes but the
  percentage does not. 1% of 9,000 is 90.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  High leverage allows you to take more risk, but it does not require you to.
  However much leverage the broker gives, your limit is in your plan.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **Where the stop slips:** in a fast market or a gap, the loss may not be exactly 1%.
- **With a very small account:** because of the minimum lot, it can be hard to fit 1% risk.
- **On a trade with no stop:** then there is no limit on the risk at all.
- **Small risk does not mean profit:** it is a condition for survival, not a guarantee of profit.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Risk per trade is the part of the account you lose if the stop is hit.
- Set the risk in money first, then set the lot size.
- With small risk you can survive a losing streak. With big risk it is hard to come back.
- Write the percentage in your plan and follow it on every trade.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Write down the amount of your (or an imaginary) account. Write down how much money the risk is at 0.5%, 1% and 2%. Then, for each, work out what the account is after 8 losses in a row (each time: previous amount × (1 − percent)).

## Quiz

1. With an account of 5,000 and risk of 2%, what is the risk on one trade?
2. Why is it a problem to set the lot first and the stop afterward?
3. After 10 losses in a row at 5% risk, by about what percent does the account fall?
4. When the account falls, what happens to the money at risk?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** 5,000 × 2% = 100.

**2.** The risk then depends on the stop distance, which you did not set beforehand. The risk goes by guesswork.

**3.** About 40% (0.95 multiplied 10 times ≈ 0.60).

**4.** The percentage stays the same, so the amount of money falls.

</details>
