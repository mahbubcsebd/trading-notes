# Drawdown Rules: Decide in Advance What to Do When the Account Keeps Falling

What will you do after several losses in a row? Raise your risk "to win it back"? Or stop? A decision made in the middle of a losing run is often wrong. So you have to write it down beforehand.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what drawdown is and how it is measured.
- **Calculate** how much gain is needed to recover a loss.
- **Recognize drawdown** on an equity curve.
- **Write a drawdown rule** for yourself.

## Why it matters

- **Drawdown is normal.** Every system has bad periods. The only question is how deep.
- **Recovery is uneven.** The more you fall, the more you need to climb back.
- **Emotion ruins decisions.** Logic weakens in the middle of losses, and that is when a rule helps.

Prerequisites: [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).

## What is drawdown

**Drawdown is the fall from the account's highest point (peak) to the next lowest point, as a percentage.**

Think of climbing a hill. How far you have come down from the summit is the drawdown. The descent is not over until you touch the peak again by climbing back up.

**Maximum drawdown** is the deepest fall in a given period. For example, with a peak of 10,200 and a low of 8,300, the fall is about 18.6%.

## The uneven math of recovering a loss

The gain needed to get back to where you were = **loss ÷ (1 − loss)**.

| Drawdown | Gain needed |
| :------- | :---------- |
| 10%      | about 11%   |
| 20%      | 25%         |
| 33%      | about 50%   |
| 50%      | 100%        |

**This is why avoiding a big drawdown matters more than profit.** After falling 50%, the pressure to double the account pushes people to take even more risk.

## Step by step: building a drawdown rule

<Steps>

### Step 1: Track the peak

Write down the account's highest point every week. Drawdown is measured from it.

### Step 2: Set the levels

For example: -5%, -10%, -15%. These change with your own tolerance and your system.

### Step 3: Write the response for each level

- -5%: risk stays normal. Review whether anything has changed.
- -10%: cut risk in half.
- -15%: stop live trading and go back to demo. Review your journal.

### Step 4: Write the condition for coming back

For example, after 10 demo trades that follow the plan, or after a week's break.

### Step 5: Put it in your plan

A rule written with a clear head is easy to follow in the middle of losses.

</Steps>

## What changes when there is a rule

Same trade sequence. The only difference: when the account falls 10% from the peak, risk drops from 2% to 1%.

The difference is not dramatic. That is the main point: a rule is not a miracle, it makes the loss smaller. Remember that this depends on the sequence and the timing. With a different sequence the result would be different.

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Rule:** at -10%, cut risk to 1%.
- **What happened:** after the account fell about 10% from the peak, risk was reduced.
- **Result:** the later losses were smaller, and the maximum fall was about 3.4 percentage points less.
- **Lesson:** the rule acts before the feeling does.

(Synthetic educational example.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

The same sequence without a rule: the trader kept 2% even in a bad run, thinking "it will turn this time".

- **What was visible:** several losses in a row, 18.6% below the peak.
- **Result:** about 23% is needed to get back. Mental pressure grew, and so did the risk of bad decisions next.
- **Why it was wrong:** there was no planned response, so emotion made the decision.
- **Lesson:** a rule is like choosing the medicine before you fall ill.

(Synthetic educational example. The result depends on the sequence and is not a guarantee.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens                | What to do                      |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| Raising risk after a loss          | Wanting to win it back fast   | Cut risk or stop                |
| Keeping the rule only in your head | The bother of writing it      | Write it in your plan           |
| Not measuring maximum drawdown     | Looking only at profit        | Watch the equity curve and peak |
| Changing the rule mid-loss         | Emotion                       | Change it in a calm period      |
| No condition for returning         | Not knowing when to come back | Write a specific condition      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Know the maximum drawdown from your own backtest. If a deeper fall arrives
  live, assume something has changed: the market or you.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  A break during drawdown is not defeat. Reviewing your journal and finding
  mistakes is the most useful work to do in this time.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  "I will win it all back on one trade with double the risk" is the most common
  path from drawdown to a wiped-out account.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the rule stays only on paper:** it does not work if you do not follow it.
- **With slippage or a gap:** one event can jump past the levels of the rule.
- **When the rule triggers too often:** the levels are too close or the risk is too high. Check both.
- **When the sample is small:** you cannot call a system bad from a few trades.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Drawdown is the fall from the peak, as a percentage.
- After losing 50% you need 100% to get back, so avoiding a big fall matters.
- Write the rule first: at which level risk is cut, and when you stop.
- Write the condition for coming back too.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

In your plan, write three drawdown levels and the response to each. Then take the R results of 20 trades (yours or imaginary), draw an equity curve, and see which level would have been triggered and when.
