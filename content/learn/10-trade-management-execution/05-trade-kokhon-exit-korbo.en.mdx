# When to Exit: No Closing a Trade Without a Written Reason

Everyone knows the reason for an entry. But ask, "Why did you close this trade?" Many will say, "I got scared." This lesson is to change that answer.

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognize** the four types of exit rule.
- **Write a rule for leaving before the stop** when the idea is proved wrong.
- **Tell the difference** between a fear exit and a rule exit.

## Why it matters

- **Emotion is strongest at the exit.** Greed in profit, hope in loss.
- **A rule-based exit makes results comparable.** You can write it in the [journal](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/05-journaling-habit) with the reason.
- **There are ways out besides the stop.** Sometimes the idea ends first.

Prerequisites: [Stop Loss Placement](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) and [Break of Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure).

## What is an exit

**An exit is the decision to close a trade that has one written reason: the profit target was met, the stop was hit, the idea was proved wrong, or time ran out.**

Think of a train ticket. There is a destination (target), an emergency exit for an accident (stop), a station to get off if the route changes (invalidation), and a timetable (time stop). You cannot get off at just any station.

| Exit type    | Reason                                            | Example                              |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------- |
| Target       | The planned profit is met                         | The target or the trail is hit       |
| Stop         | The maximum loss limit                            | The stop order is hit                |
| Invalidation | The reason for the trade is gone, before the stop | A candle closes below the higher low |
| Time stop    | The move did not come even in the set time        | Close after N candles                |

The exit point comes before the stop. The base of the idea (the higher low) has broken, so there is no more waiting.

## Step by step: writing the exit rules

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write the reason for the trade in one sentence

For example: "The pullback has ended above the higher low." When this reason ends, you exit.

### Step 2: Set the invalidation price

The price at which the reason is proved wrong. Usually a level of the [structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low). Write down also whether it is by a close or by a wick.

### Step 3: Keep the target and the stop

If invalidation happens, exit. If not, let it run to the target or the stop.

### Step 4: Set a time limit (optional)

For example, if the move has not come within 20 candles, close. Capital cannot be tied up.

### Step 5: Write the reason for the exit in your journal

Target, stop, invalidation, time, or fear? If you have to write the last one, a rule was broken.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 1.0840, invalidation 1.0870 (close-based).
- **Result:** a candle closed below 1.0870 at about 1.0867. The exit was at a loss of about 33 pips, before the planned stop (60 pips).
- **Lesson:** when the idea is wrong, you do not have to wait for the whole stop. The rule was written first.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** price rose a little and stopped, and the trader was nervous.
- **Result:** closed with a profit of +12 pips. Later it touched the target (the planned +90 pips).
- **Why it failed:** the exit came from emotion. There was no invalidation, no stop hit, and no target.
- **Lesson:** a small win, but the math of the plan was broken. If it happens again and again, the [Expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy) is ruined.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                              | Why it happens             | What to do                                    |
| :----------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Closing early out of fear            | Fear of losing profit      | Keep a rule for partial close or trail        |
| Holding after invalidation           | Hoping "it will come back" | Exit by the written rule                      |
| Not writing the reason for each exit | Laziness                   | One word for the reason in the journal        |
| Mixing up stop and exit              | Unclear rules              | Write separate definitions for the four types |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Before an exit, ask yourself: "Is the reason for this exit in my written
  plan?" If not, the exit is probably emotional.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  An invalidation exit gives a smaller loss than the stop, but not always.
  Sometimes price touches the invalidation and goes back. Look at the result of
  this trade-off over a long sample.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the invalidation is unclear:** the decision goes back to guesswork.
- **If price touches the level on wicks again and again:** choose a close-based rule.
- **In a fast market:** the exit price can also be worse than planned ([Slippage](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening)).
- **If the time limit is very short:** even good trades are cut.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Four types of exit: target, stop, invalidation, time.
- When the idea is proved wrong you can leave before the stop, but write the rule first.
- A fear exit is not a rule exit.
- Write the reason for each exit in your journal.

</KeyTakeaways>
