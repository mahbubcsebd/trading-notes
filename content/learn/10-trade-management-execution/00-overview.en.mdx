# Trade Management and Execution: The Work After Entry

Entry is getting the plane into the sky. But the flight ends with the landing. Many people think about entry all the time, and make the decisions during a trade on emotion. This chapter is the rules for that landing.

## What is trade management

**Trade management means following rules decided in advance about the stop, target, size and exit after a trade is opened. Execution is the reality of the price and the way your order was actually filled.**

This chapter answers one question: **"After I open a trade, what do I do, and at what price will the market fill my order?"**

## Why learn this now

- **Even a good entry can lose with bad management.** With the same [Entry](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/00-overview) from the earlier chapter, the stop and exit change the result.
- **Emotion works hardest while a trade is running.** Rules written earlier bring you back then.
- **The calculation on paper and the real result differ.** Slippage and spread can change the result.
- **The next chapters stand on this one.** These rules are written in the [Trading Plan](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/01-trading-plan-template).

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Place the stop in a meaningful place** by reading the structure.
- **Plan take profit and partial close.**
- **Protect running profit** with a **trailing stop**.
- **Count the effect of slippage, requotes and spread** in your calculation.
- **Decide on an exit** when the reason for the trade is gone.

## How these lessons work together

In the life of a trade, the questions come in this order:

1. **Where is the stop?** Set it first, then work out the lot (lesson 1).
2. **Where is the target?** Where to take profit, and partial close (lesson 2).
3. **How will the order go?** Market or limit, how much slippage to allow for (lesson 4).
4. **What while it runs?** Whether a trailing stop is needed (lesson 3).
5. **When do I get out?** Target, stop, invalidation or time (lesson 5).
