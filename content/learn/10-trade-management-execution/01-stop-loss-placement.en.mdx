# Stop Loss Placement: Where to Put It So the Idea Is Proved Wrong

You placed a stop loss, price touched it exactly and went back, and the idea of your trade was right. Many traders have had this experience. Often the problem is not having a stop. It is the place of the stop.

## What you will be able to do

- **Place the stop beyond the structure.**
- **Show the harm** of a stop that is too close.
- **State the order** for working out the lot after the stop is set.

## Why it matters

- **The stop decides your maximum loss.** If it is not decided beforehand, it gets decided by emotion.
- **A stop in the wrong place hurts both ways.** If it is close, noise cuts it. If it is far, the risk goes up.
- **The lot depends on the stop.** When the stop changes, the lot has to change.

Prerequisites: [Swing High and Swing Low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low) and [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## What is a stop loss

**A stop loss is a price decided in advance at which the trade will be closed. The right stop is the place where, if price reaches it, the idea behind the trade is proved wrong.**

Think of a river embankment. If you have not decided how high the water can rise before the embankment breaks, you have to decide after the flood comes. The stop is that limit, decided beforehand.

On a buy trade, the stop usually goes below an important swing low. On a sell, above a swing high. If price reaches there, the structure has broken.

## Step by step: placing the stop

<Steps>

### Step 1: Set the stop before entry

First know where you are proved wrong. That is the stop.

### Step 2: Find the structure

The last important swing low (buy) or swing high (sell). Or beyond a [support zone](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones).

### Step 3: Add a buffer

Not exactly at the level. Look at the [ATR](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/04-atr-stop-size) and leave a small gap. Keep the spread in mind too.

### Step 4: Measure the distance and check the ratio

How many pips from entry to stop? If the [ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) is below the minimum, skip the trade.

### Step 5: Then work out the lot

Keep the risk in money fixed and work out the lot from the stop distance. Do not do it the other way round.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 12 pips below the swing low, ratio 1:2.
- **Result:** the pullback did not reach the swing low, and price touched the target.
- **Lesson:** the place of the stop came from the chart, not from wishing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Why it happens               | What to do                                 |
| :--------------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| Pulling the stop too close               | To show smaller risk         | Beyond the structure, then cut the lot     |
| Not placing a stop                       | "I'll watch it"              | Place it in the order along with the entry |
| Moving the stop farther during the trade | Not accepting the loss       | The stop moves only toward less risk       |
| Stop right against the level             | Looking for the perfect spot | Leave a buffer for noise and spread        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The rule for moving a stop: only toward profit (toward less risk). Moving it
  toward a loss means denying your earlier decision.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A "mental stop" (kept in your head) is dangerous. Under pressure it moves.
  Place it as an order with the broker.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **During news:** price can jump past the stop ([Slippage](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening)).
- **When the structure is unclear:** you cannot tell where the idea is wrong, so skip the trade.
- **When the stop is very far:** the ratio or the lot is unacceptable, so drop the trade.
- **When the spread is high:** the stop can be hit early.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The stop goes where the idea of the trade is proved wrong.
- Leave a small buffer beyond the structure.
- First the stop, then the lot. Not the other way round.
- The stop moves only toward less risk.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Find three buy setups on a demo chart. For each, place the stop below the structure, measure the distance, and then work out the lot at 1% risk.
