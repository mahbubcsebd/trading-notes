# Take Profit and Partial Close: Where and How Much Profit to Take

Price went 60 pips your way. You wanted more. Then price came back and touched your stop. The profit on paper never reached your bank.

## What you will be able to do

- **Place the target** by looking at structure and ratio.
- **Decide the rules** for a partial close.
- **State the benefit and cost** of moving the stop to break-even.

## Why it matters

- **If you do not protect profit, the numbers do not add up.** The [ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) is good on paper, but not in reality.
- **Without a target, greed makes the decisions.** A rule set in advance reduces emotion.
- **A partial close reduces mental pressure.** Some profit is secure, and the rest is for the move.

Prerequisites: [Stop Loss Placement](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) and [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones).

## What are take profit and partial close

**Take profit (TP) is a price decided in advance at which the trade closes in profit. Partial close means closing part of the position early and letting the rest run.**

Think of harvesting. You put half the crop in the barn first and let the rest ripen in the field. Even if a storm comes, at least half is safe.

| Method          | How                                     | Benefit                      | Cost                            |
| :-------------- | :-------------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| One target      | The whole position at one place         | Simple, clear rule           | No protection of partial profit |
| Partial close   | Half at 1R, the rest at a bigger target | Part of the profit is secure | Less total profit on a big move |
| Break-even stop | Once in profit, stop at the entry price | Risk of loss close to zero   | Can be cut by a normal pullback |

## Step by step: target and partial close

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark the nearest barrier

Where is the next resistance or swing high? The target goes before it, or just below it.

### Step 2: Match the minimum ratio

It is good if the target distance is at least about twice the stop. If it is below that, skip the trade.

### Step 3: Write the partial close rule

For example: half closed at 1R, the rest at the final target. Write the rule before the trade.

### Step 4: Decide the stop rule

After the partial close, does the stop go to break-even? At once, or below a new [swing low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low)? Decide beforehand.

### Step 5: Follow the rules

When price is near the TP, do not make a new decision. The earlier rule runs.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 30 pips, TP1 half at 1R, final target 3R.
- **Result:** TP1 was secured, price fell a little but did not reach the stop, and then touched the final target.
- **Lesson:** because the rules were written first, there was no need to change the decision on the pullback.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** price reached near 1.0960, with open profit of about 2R.
- **Result:** price came back and touched the stop. The loss is the full 1R.
- **Why it failed:** there was no rule for a partial close or for moving the stop.
- **Lesson:** open profit is not secure. Without a rule, you can lose the profit too.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Why it happens        | What to do                       |
| :------------------------------------ | :-------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Entering without setting a target     | "Let's see"           | Write it before entry            |
| Moving the stop to break-even at once | Fear of a loss        | Leave room for a normal pullback |
| A target at an unrealistic distance   | Greed for more profit | Look at the nearest barrier      |
| Changing the rules mid-profit         | Emotion               | Follow the earlier rules         |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Choose the share for the partial close (half, a third) to match your own
  temperament. The share that keeps you calm is the one that works for you.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  A partial close changes the total ratio. In your
  [Expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy) calculation, add up
  the results of all the parts.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the position is very small:** it cannot be split (minimum lot).
- **When spread and commission are high:** a small TP is wiped out by costs.
- **In a strong trend:** by exiting early you leave the big move.
- **When the break-even stop is very close:** a normal pullback cuts it.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The target is set by looking at structure and the minimum ratio.
- A partial close secures part of the profit, and can lower the total profit.
- Write the partial close and stop rules before the trade.
- Open profit is not secure.

</KeyTakeaways>
