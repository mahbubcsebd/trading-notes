# Candle Context: Why Location Matters More Than Shape

The same bullish engulfing lifted price 70 pips on one chart and hit the stop on another. Their shapes were exactly the same. So where is the difference? The answer is not inside the candle; it is around the candle.

## What you will be able to do

- **Reject** a pattern **by its context**, even when its shape is right.
- **Run** a **three-question context test** that combines trend, level and timeframe.
- **Compare** the successful and failed forms of the same pattern side by side.
- **Decide** when to wait instead of jumping into an entry when you see a pattern.

## Why it matters

- **A large share of false signals comes from the wrong location.** If you trade whenever the shape matches, the middle of every range will call you in.
- **Context teaches you to take fewer trades.** When more patterns are filtered out, risk goes down and focus goes up.
- **It is the base of the next chapter.** Support, resistance and supply-demand are all really ways of recognising "location".

Prerequisites: [Two Candle Patterns](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/03-two-candle-patterns) and [Trend and Range](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl).

## What candle context is

**Candle context is the setting around a candle or pattern: the trend before it, the nearby price levels, and the direction of the bigger timeframe.**

Imagine someone shouting "Help!". Inside a cinema, it is acting; on an empty street at midnight, it is an emergency. Same words, different meaning. A candle pattern is the same: the shape is the same, but when the location changes, the meaning changes.

Recall the formula: **Pattern = shape + location + confirmation.** This whole lesson is about "location".

## Three questions: the context test

When you see a pattern, ask three questions before you enter.

| Question                              | What to look at                            | A good answer (for a bullish pattern)      |
| :------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| 1. **What came before?**              | The direction of the last 8–10 candles     | A clear downtrend (something to turn from) |
| 2. **Where is it happening?**         | Nearby support/resistance, previous swings | On support or demand                       |
| 3. **What does the big picture say?** | The trend and levels one timeframe up      | No opposing wall on the bigger timeframe   |

At least two of the three must match, and **the second one (location) is almost required**. The other two are witnesses in support of the location.

<Callout type="note">
  For a bearish pattern everything is reversed: an uptrend before, a location at
  resistance or supply, and an open path down on the bigger timeframe.
</Callout>

### Question 1: What came before

A reversal pattern wants to _change_ direction, so there has to be a direction to change. Without a downtrend, what is a bullish engulfing reversing? That is why these patterns are cheap inside a range: they come again and again and say nothing.

### Question 2: Where is it happening

This is the heaviest question. A bullish pattern on or near support means "buyers were here before". The same pattern below resistance means "buyers are running toward a barrier". You will find the detailed method for recognising locations in the [Levels vs Zones](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) lesson.

### Question 3: The big picture

A nice bullish engulfing on H4, but on the D1 chart there is big resistance right above. The small-timeframe signal hits the big-timeframe wall. This top-down habit is covered in [Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis).

## Step by step: taking a pattern after checking context

<Steps>

### Step 1: Step back before naming the pattern

Zoom out and look at the last 20–30 candles. Which story are you in the middle of?

### Step 2: Mark the nearest levels

Draw the two nearest levels above and below the pattern. For a bullish pattern, you want support below and resistance far away above.

### Step 3: Measure the room to move

Is the distance from the entry to the next resistance at least twice the stop distance? If not, the trade's risk/reward is weak. See also [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward).

### Step 4: Look one timeframe up

If the pattern is on H4, open D1. Is there an opposing big level or a big trend?

### Step 5: Score the three questions

How many matched? If location does not match, skip the trade, however well the other two match.

### Step 6: Then confirmation and stop

Only if context passes, wait for the next candle's close and place the stop outside the pattern.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

Look at the chart above.

- **What could be seen:** price touched the 1.0700 support, rose once, and came back down. Bearish candle 6 closed above support.
- **Decision:** the shape (engulfing) and the location (a support that held before) both match. But the entry is not yet.
- **Confirmation:** candle 8 went past 1.0742 and closed at 1.0770. Buy at about 1.0772. Stop a little below the engulfing's low (1.0696), at 1.0690.
- **Result:** price rose step by step to 1.0815.
- **Lesson:** the level gave extra safety here. The stop was small (about 82 pips), and it was clear where you would be proven wrong.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade, and no result is guaranteed.)

## Example 2: when it becomes a trap (synthetic)

Now exactly the same shape, but in another location.

  id="context-engulfing-midair-01"

  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example) — a bullish engulfing below a broken support (now resistance). The confirmation candle is small, then price falls"
/>

- **What could be seen:** price broke the 1.0800 support and fell. It rose a little and came back. Candle 7 fully engulfed candle 6's body, like the textbook.
- **Wrong decision:** "I saw an engulfing, and candle 8 also closed higher, so now it will rise." Buy at 1.0808, stop at 1.0758.
- **What happened:** candle 9 fell hard and broke the stop. Then came two more bearish candles.
- **Why it failed:** the shape was perfect, but the pattern formed right below a broken support, which is now acting as resistance (looking ahead: [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal)). The big trend was down, and the confirmation candle barely touched resistance and could not get past it.

**Lesson:** the engulfing is exactly the same in both examples. The only difference is location. The question that is easiest to forget is the most valuable: "What is right above or below this candle?"

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Why it happens                          | What to do                                       |
| :-------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| Trading on shape alone                  | The excitement of recognising a pattern | Run the three-question test first                |
| Buying below an opposing wall           | Resistance was not drawn                | Draw the levels above and below before entering  |
| Looking only at the small timeframe     | Too lazy to open the bigger chart       | Make looking one timeframe up a rule             |
| Treating a level as one thin line       | Missing the idea of a zone              | Treat a level as a zone, allow a few pips        |
| Convincing yourself the context matches | The urge to take a trade                | Write down what answer you got to each question  |
| Treating any confirmation as safe       | Confirmation is not always strong       | Look at the confirmation candle's size and close |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you open a chart, draw levels first, not patterns. Once you know the
  levels, patterns show up on their own, and the ones outside the levels are
  easy to ignore.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Is the confirmation candle small, but right below resistance? Treat it as a
  "weak confirmation". A strong confirmation closes through or past the level.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The context test is no guarantee of success. It only filters out bad odds.
  Even where everything matches, do not trade without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When levels are too dense:** if the chart has a line at every price, the context test cannot filter anything. Keep fewer, thicker levels.
- **Just before and after big news:** however good the context, news can turn everything upside down.
- **When a level is new:** a level that has never been tested is less reliable. Once it holds, trust grows.
- **On small timeframes:** on M1–M5, levels break quickly and context is unstable.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The same shape says different things in different places; shape alone is not enough.
- Three questions: what came before, where it is happening, what the big picture says. Location is the heaviest.
- Draw levels first, look for patterns after.
- Even if context passes, confirmation and a stop are required.

</KeyTakeaways>

Open the EUR/USD H4 chart on TradingView. Find 5 engulfings or hammers over the last 2 months. For each, draw the two nearest levels and write the answers to the three questions. Then count: how many had a good location, and what did price do after them?

## Quiz

1. What are the three questions of the context test?
2. Which question is the heaviest, and why?
3. What should you think when you see a bullish engulfing below a broken support?
4. If the context matches, can you enter without confirmation?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** What came before (trend), where it is happening (level), and what the bigger timeframe says.

**2.** Location (level). Because the same shape means one thing above support and the opposite below resistance.

**3.** The broken support may now be acting as resistance, so buyers are running toward a wall. The risk is high, and it is usually better to skip it.

**4.** No. Context only qualifies the pattern; it does not give an entry signal. You still need the next candle's confirmation and a stop set in advance.

</details>
