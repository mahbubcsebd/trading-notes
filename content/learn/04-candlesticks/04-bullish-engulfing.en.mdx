# Bullish Engulfing: How to Tell a Strong One from a Weak One

Put two bullish engulfings side by side. In the rulebook, both are "engulfing". Yet after one, price rises; after the other, the stop is hit. Same name, different quality. In this lesson you will learn how to measure quality and where to enter to lower the risk.

## What you will be able to do

- **Check** the quality of a bullish engulfing **with three questions**.
- **Explain** the difference between the confirmation, 50% retest and close entries.
- **Place** the stop and target from the pattern's low.
- **Avoid** engulfings that meet the rule but are weak.

## Why it matters

- **Same pattern, different risk.** Where you enter changes the stop distance, and so the risk.
- **Meeting the rule is not enough.** In the [previous lesson](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/03-two-candle-patterns) you learned the rules for the shape. Here you will learn the strength inside the shape.
- **Location is in a separate lesson.** Where the pattern formed is covered in [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context). This lesson assumes the location is right.

Prerequisites: Two Candle Patterns and [Anatomy of a Candle](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/01-anatomy-of-a-candle).

## What a bullish engulfing is

**A bullish engulfing is two candles where the body of a big bullish candle fully covers the body of the bearish candle before it.**

Picture a tug-of-war. All morning the sellers were pulling the rope. Suddenly the buyers took it all back in one pull, and even more. There is only one question: how strong was the pull? A strong pull takes a lot of rope. A weak pull takes just as much as the rule needs.

Recall the rule: the second candle's open is at or below the first one's close, and its close is at or above the first one's open. This rule says "there or not there". The three questions below measure strength.

## Three questions to measure strength

| Question                            | What to look at                                            | Why                                                |
| :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. What is the earlier candle like? | A clear bearish body, not a doji or a small one            | A small one is easy to cover, so the proof is weak |
| 2. Where is the close?              | In the top third of its own range, with a small upper wick | A big wick means sellers pushed it back at the top |
| 3. How big is the body?             | About at least 1.5 times the earlier body                  | A big body means a big change                      |

<Callout type="note">
  The 1.5 times and the one-third are my working rules, not verified statistics.
  Test them on your own chart and set your own limits, but once you set them,
  use the same measure on every chart.
</Callout>

**Many people think a pattern is strong as long as the body is covered, but in fact if any one of the three questions fails, the signal is weak.** If one question fails, be careful; if two fail, avoid it.

## Three entries: which one goes where

With the same pattern, you can enter at three places. Each has a cost.

| Entry                  | Where                                                             | Advantage                  | Disadvantage                                    |
| :--------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **Close entry**        | As soon as the engulfing candle closes                            | You do not miss the chance | No confirmation; you are entering early         |
| **Confirmation entry** | When the next candle goes past the engulfing's high               | More proof                 | The stop is far away, small R:R                 |
| **50% retest entry**   | A limit when price comes back to the middle of the engulfing body | Small stop, good R:R       | If price does not come back, you miss the trade |

For the 50% retest, finding the middle of the body is easy: add the engulfing candle's open and close and divide by two.

<Callout type="note">
  In a retest entry, "missing the trade" is not a loss but a cost. A trade where
  the risk is not right is better missed.
</Callout>

## Step by step: a bullish engulfing trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Confirm the location

Is it near a [support](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) after a downtrend? If not, the rest of this lesson does not apply.

### Step 2: Wait for the candle to close

A running candle can show an engulfing and then change before it closes.

### Step 3: Check the quality with the three questions

The earlier candle, where the close is, the size of the body. If two fail, stop.

### Step 4: Choose the type of entry

Measure the stop distance first. Choose the entry at which the R:R works.

### Step 5: Place the stop

A little below the pattern's lowest low (including the wick). Allow at least the spread as a buffer. See also [Stop Loss Placement](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement).

### Step 6: Set the size and target

Work out the [position size](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) from the stop distance. Keep the target before the previous swing or the nearest resistance.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above again.

- **What could be seen:** a steady downtrend; price touched the 1.0700 support, rose once, and fell again. Candle 7 was bearish with a low of 1.0690 (support broke slightly). Candle 8 is the engulfing.
- **Quality check:** all three questions pass.
- **Decision:** with a confirmation entry, buy at 1.0764 and stop at 1.0682: a risk of 82 pips. With a target of 1.0810, the gain is only 46 pips. The R:R is about 0.56, less than the risk. So a limit was placed at the body's 50% (1.0725).
- **Result:** candle 9 touched exactly 1.0725 and rose. Entry 1.0725, stop 1.0682 (43 pips of risk), target 1.0810 (85 pips of gain), R:R about 2. Candle 11 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** the pattern was the same; changing the entry cut the risk by about half.

## Example 2: when it becomes a trap (synthetic)

<Chart
  id="engulfing-weak-quality-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example) — it covered a small candle, but has a long upper wick. The close is in the lower part of the range, then price falls"
/>

- **What could be seen:** candle 6's body is only 6 pips. Candle 7 covered it, with a 12-pip body. The rule matches, and the body ratio is double.
- **Wrong decision:** "The rule matches, and the body is double." Close entry at 1.0726, stop at 1.0702: 24 pips of risk.
- **What happened:** candle 8 rose a little and stopped at 1.0745; it could not reach the engulfing's high of 1.0762. Candle 9 fell and broke the stop, then fell further.
- **Why it failed:** candle 7's range was 52 pips, but its body was 12 pips. The upper wick was 36 pips, and the close sat at only 31% of the range. Buyers had pushed up, but sellers pushed it straight back. And the earlier candle was so small that covering it was no proof at all.

**Lesson:** the body ratio alone is not enough. The second of the three questions (where the close is) would have caught this. Because the stop had been set in advance, the loss was held to 24 pips.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                          | Why it happens                              | What to do                                                    |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Taking a pattern just because the rule matches   | The rule is easy, measuring quality is hard | Check with the three questions                                |
| Ignoring a long upper wick                       | Confidence from a big body                  | First check where the close landed                            |
| Keeping the stop just below the pattern's body   | Ignoring the wick                           | Use the lowest low (including the wick)                       |
| Chasing price instead of waiting for the retest  | Fear of missing the trade                   | Set a limit and leave it; good if it comes back, if not, fine |
| Confirmation entry on an extra-large engulfing   | Trust from the shape                        | If the stop is far, use the retest or skip it                 |
| Always using the same entry for the same pattern | Habit                                       | Measure the stop distance each time and choose                |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Check what time the engulfing candle formed. Compared with the quiet middle of
  a session, a big candle at the
  [session](/en/learn/01-foundations/05-trading-sessions) open is often the
  result of news or rushing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  If the engulfing's low slightly breaks the previous swing low and comes back
  (as in Example 1), the return came after the stops below were taken. This fits
  with a [liquidity
  sweep](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) and adds
  strength.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  If you hear a specific percentage as a pattern's "success rate", do not accept
  it without checking. The rate depends on the market, the timeframe and how you
  count.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **Without a location:** in empty space with no support or demand. See Candle Context.
- **Without a trend:** in the middle of a range it means nothing.
- **When the earlier candle is very small:** covering it proves little.
- **During big news:** candles get artificially big, and the next direction is random.
- **When there is resistance right above on the higher timeframe:** the small-timeframe signal gets stuck at the big wall.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Measure a bullish engulfing with three questions: what the earlier candle is like, where the close is, how big the body is.
- Meeting the rule is not proof of strength. Covering a small candle is easy, and a long upper wick is weakness.
- Three entries: close (fast, more risk), confirmation (more proof, stop far away), 50% retest (small stop, may miss the trade).
- The stop goes below the whole pattern, including the wick. Set the size from the stop distance.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. A bullish engulfing can be weak even when the rule matches. Why?
2. What does an engulfing candle with a long upper wick mean?
3. What are the main advantage and disadvantage of the 50% retest entry?
4. Where does the stop go, and why is the wick included?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** The rule only checks that the body is covered; it does not measure strength. If the earlier candle is small or the close is weak, the signal is weak.

**2.** Buyers pushed up, but sellers brought price back from there. The close lands in the lower part of the range.

**3.** Advantage: a small stop and good R:R. Disadvantage: if price does not come back, you miss the trade.

**4.** A little below the pattern's lowest low. Including the wick, because the wick shows how far price actually fell.
