# Wyckoff Basics: Reading the Sequence of an Accumulation Range

After a long downtrend, price keeps moving around one place. Everyone says "range". Wyckoff asks: **who is buying in this range, who is selling, and who is tired?** In this lesson you will learn a simple, testable form of that question.

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognize the six stages of an accumulation range.**
- **Pick an entry** by reading the stages **in order**.
- **Tell a spring** from a real breakdown.
- **Know the limits** of Wyckoff.

## Why it matters

- **It shows the story inside a [range](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout).** One step beyond just "buy if it breaks up".
- **It teaches reading a sequence.** Not one candle, but a row of events.
- **You get the reason for the stop.** Each stage has a condition under which it becomes invalid.

Prerequisites: [Volume Profile](/en/learn/16-advanced/02-volume-profile-market-profile) and [Support-Resistance Zones](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones).

## What is Wyckoff

**The Wyckoff method is a framework for reading a range as an accumulation (gathering) or distribution (releasing) phase by looking at price and volume.** The name comes from Richard Wyckoff, who gave this idea at the start of the last century.

To be honest: it is a **model**. Not every range is accumulation; many ranges break and go down. Measure the result in your own backtest.

## The stages of an accumulation range

| Short  | Name                  | What happens                                                           |
| :----- | :-------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SC     | Selling Climax        | Sharp selling, big volume, settling lower at the end                   |
| AR     | Automatic Rally       | A natural bounce after the SC, which sets the upper limit of the range |
| ST     | Secondary Test        | A return near the SC to test, with low volume                          |
| Spring | Spring                | A brief dip below the range and a quick return                         |
| SOS    | Sign of Strength      | A firm rise above the upper limit of the range                         |
| LPS    | Last Point of Support | A pullback after the SOS, a higher low, the area for entry             |

Not every range has every stage. This is the ideal form.

## Step by step: reading Wyckoff

<Steps>

### Step 1: First recognize the trend

Accumulation comes after a downtrend. If there was no downtrend before, this framework does not apply.

### Step 2: Draw the limits of the range

The low of the SC and the high of the AR. These two are the structure of the range.

### Step 3: Look for the sequence

Did volume fall on the ST? Did the spring return quickly? If the sequence does not match, wait.

### Step 4: Wait for the SOS

Has the upper limit of the range been broken firmly? Do not enter yet.

### Step 5: Enter on the LPS

If a higher low forms on the pullback after the SOS, there. The stop below the LPS, the target at least 2R.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What there was:** from a downtrend, an SC around 1.0900, an AR at 1.0975, an ST, then a spring at 1.0880.
- **While running:** price broke the upper limit of the range (SOS), then pulled back around 1.0988 (LPS). Buy 1.0988, stop 1.0958, target 1.1048.
- **Result:** the target was hit, +2R.
- **Lesson:** entry after the sequence was complete, and the reason for the stop was clear.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** price fell below the range, and the trader assumed it was a spring.
- **Result:** buy 1.0893, the stop at 1.0866 was hit, -1R.
- **Why it failed:** the condition for a spring is a **quick return**. That did not happen. The assumption in advance was a guess.
- **Lesson:** before calling it a spring, wait for the return. Buying before the return means catching a breakdown against it.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens              | What to do                                             |
| :------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| Thinking every range is accumulation         | Bias toward the model       | First see whether there is a downtrend                 |
| Buying before the spring                     | Hurry                       | Wait until the quick return is confirmed               |
| Ignoring volume                              | Looking only at price       | Look for low volume on the ST and big volume on the SC |
| Entering without an SOS                      | Thinking it is "cheap"      | You need proof of strength                             |
| Mixing up the opposite order of distribution | A tangle of memorized names | First master accumulation alone                        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Set a "cancel line" outside the range, such as far below the low of the SC. If
  it breaks, the idea of accumulation is cancelled. Then do not wait; get out.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Forex volume is tick-based (the limit from the Volume Profile lesson applies
  here too). So signals about volume are a help, not reliable proof.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the range is small or unclear:** you cannot recognize the stages.
- **On a news shock:** the whole structure breaks in one candle.
- **On a lower timeframe:** more noise, and it is easy to make up stages (hindsight bias).
- **If you place the stages by looking back:** it is easy to go back on the chart and put labels like "SC, AR"; it is hard live. Practice on demo first.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Accumulation has six stages: SC, AR, ST, spring, SOS, LPS.
- When the sequence is complete, enter on the LPS, after seeing proof of strength.
- A spring means a quick return; if it doesn't return, it is a breakdown.
- This is a model; check it in your own backtest.

</KeyTakeaways>
