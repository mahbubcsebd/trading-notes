# Volume Profile and Market Profile: At Which Price the Market Spends the Most Time

A normal chart shows **when** price was at what level (by time). A volume profile asks the opposite question: **at which price** did the most trading happen? This shows which price the market thought was "fair".

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognize the POC, Value Area, VAH and VAL.**
- **Understand how a profile is built.**
- **Tell apart a failed break** and **acceptance**.
- **Know the limits** of using volume in forex.

## Why it matters

- **A different way of seeing support/resistance.** Prices with more trading often become important again.
- **You can tell inside and outside the range.** Whether price holds outside is the key question.
- **The link between [volume](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/06-volume-vwap) and price** goes a step further here.

Prerequisites: [Range and Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout).

## The main terms

**A volume profile is a vertical histogram of how much total volume traded at each price over a period.**

| Term                   | Meaning                                                                           |
| :--------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| POC (Point of Control) | The price with the most volume                                                    |
| Value Area             | The price range in which about 70% of the total volume happened (the usual value) |
| VAH / VAL              | The highest and lowest price of the Value Area                                    |
| Market Profile         | The same idea, time-based (drawn with TPO letters)                                |

**The limit in forex:** Forex has no central exchange. The volume a platform shows is usually **tick volume** (how many times price changed), not real trading. So the profile is approximate. In futures (such as 6E) or shares, volume is real.

## How it is built

Building a profile is a simple idea:

1. Divide price into small steps (for example every 5 pips).
2. Spread each candle's volume across the steps within its high-low range.
3. Add up by step.
4. The highest step is the POC. From the POC, keep taking the bigger side of the two sides and extend until 70%. That is the Value Area.

## Step by step: using a profile

<Steps>

### Step 1: Take the profile of the earlier session

Of yesterday or last week. Write down the POC, VAH and VAL.

### Step 2: Where did price open today?

Inside the Value Area (balance) or outside (a possible trend)?

### Step 3: Ask: is price holding outside?

After going above the VAH, is price **there for hours**, or did it come back inside fast?

### Step 4: Plan

If it comes back: a chance of a move to the other end of the Value Area (VAL). If it holds: not a fade, but waiting or going with the trend.

### Step 5: Stop and size

The stop beyond the spike. The risk is fixed, by the [rules](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/02-entry-exit-risk-rules-ek-page-e).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Profile:** POC 1.0930, VAH 1.0945, VAL 1.0920.
- **While running:** in the next session price rose to 1.0958 and passed the VAH, but did not hold, and came back inside to 1.0944. Sell 1.0944, stop 1.0960, target VAL 1.0920.
- **Result:** the target was hit, +1.5R.
- **Lesson:** the trade back inside comes only after seeing that price could not hold outside.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What happened:** the same setup, a sell at the same price. But price did not come back inside. It stayed above the VAH.
- **Result:** the stop at 1.0960 was hit, -1R.
- **Why it failed:** price stayed above for hours and built a new "fair" area (acceptance). Here the fade is against the move.
- **Lesson:** acceptance and a failed break are different. Do not trade against the first.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                              | Why it happens                    | What to do                                 |
| :----------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| Treating tick volume as real volume  | Looking at the platform's picture | Know the limit and treat it as approximate |
| Fading just on a touch of VAH/VAL    | The "level" looks nice            | Enter after seeing whether it holds        |
| Mixing profiles of different periods | Too many tools                    | Fix one period (day/week)                  |
| Treating the POC as a sure magnet    | A simple model                    | It is a tendency, not a rule               |
| Mean reversion with no stop          | "It will surely return"           | Always the stop first                      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  At the start of every day, draw the three lines of yesterday's POC/VAH/VAL.
  Where price is today is the first question.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Market Profile and Volume Profile are the same family, but with a different
  form. Pick one at first. There is no need to memorize all of it.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **At the moment of news:** price jumps over all levels.
- **If the volume data is of low quality:** tick volume and real volume can tell different stories.
- **On very little data:** a profile of a few hours is unstable.
- **On a trending day:** the idea of balance does not apply; price keeps moving away from the Value Area.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Profile = total volume at each price; the POC, VAH and VAL are the main marks.
- Forex volume is approximate (tick).
- Failed break vs acceptance: the question is whether it holds.
- Do not fade against acceptance.

</KeyTakeaways>
