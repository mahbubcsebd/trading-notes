# FOMO and Revenge Trading: Two Emotions, One Rule to Stop

Price suddenly jumps, and you are out. Or you have just had a loss, and your mind says "take it back right now." Both are emotional urges. The medicine for both is the same: stop.

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognize the signals** of FOMO and revenge trading.
- **See the damage** of both **in numbers**.
- **Build a rule for stopping.**

## Why it matters

- **Emotional trades are the most expensive.** The entry is in a bad place, the size is big, and the stop is unclear.
- **It is the most common path to a wrecked account.** A small loss turns into a big drawdown.
- **The two emotions pull on each other.** Missing a move brings FOMO, a loss brings revenge, and then it starts again.

Prerequisites: [Accepting Losses](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/03-accepting-losses).

## What are FOMO and revenge

**FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out) is entering late for fear of missing a move that is leaving. Revenge trading is taking a new trade in a hurry, often at a bigger size, to win back a loss.**

Think of running after a bus that is leaving. Sometimes you catch it. But often the next bus comes on time, and in the rush you fall. In the market too, the next bus comes.

|                | FOMO                          | Revenge                          |
| :------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Trigger        | Price has gone up, I am out   | A loss has happened              |
| Emotion        | Fear of missing out           | Anger, a sense of injustice      |
| Common mistake | Entry at the top, a wide stop | Raising size, entry with no plan |
| How to stop    | Wait for a pullback           | A break, and the earlier size    |

## Step by step: the rule to stop

<Steps>

### Step 1: Recognize the signals

A shaking hand, looking at the chart again and again, the thought "if I don't take it now it's over", or a new entry right after a loss.

### Step 2: The 10-minute rule

When you get a signal, step away from the chart for 10 minutes. Emotion falls and the decision gets clear.

### Step 3: The plan's question

"Does this entry follow my written setup? Where is the stop?" If there is no answer, skip.

### Step 4: After a loss, the same size or smaller

Go by the [position sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) formula. Raising it is forbidden.

### Step 5: Wait for a pullback or the next setup

The market gives another chance, often at a better price.

</Steps>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens               | What to do                       |
| :--------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Running after the top              | Fear of missing out          | Wait for a pullback or retest    |
| Entering again right after a loss  | Anger                        | A 10-minute break, then the plan |
| Raising size to win back a loss    | The urge to pay it back fast | Hold the size by the formula     |
| Looking for "luck" on another pair | Wanting to distract the mind | Close at the daily limit         |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write on a piece of paper: "A missed move is not a loss." Read it as soon as
  FOMO rises.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  If you hit the daily limit after revenge trading, no more trading that day.
  This is part of the plan, not a punishment.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the next setup really does not come:** waiting means missing it. That is acceptable too.
- **In a slow market the 10-minute rule may not work:** then use a loss limit (the daily limit) instead of time.
- **If there is no rule outside the plan:** write the rule for stopping in advance too.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- FOMO makes you enter late at the top, and revenge raises the size.
- A pullback often gives a second chance.
- After a loss, keep the size the same or smaller.
- When you get a signal, stop for 10 minutes.

</KeyTakeaways>
