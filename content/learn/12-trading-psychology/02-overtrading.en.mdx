# Overtrading: Why More Trades Invite More Losses

The market is open, the chart is moving, and you are sitting with your hands folded. It feels like you should do something. That urge to "do something" is overtrading.

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognize overtrading** from **three signals**.
- **Understand the reasons** why we trade too much.
- **Set a limit** on the number of trades.

## Why it matters

- **Every trade has a cost.** You pay the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) every time.
- **Attention is split.** More trades means less care on each.
- **When a good setup comes, you have no capital or confidence left.**

Prerequisites: [Discipline](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/01-discipline).

## What is overtrading

**Overtrading is taking more trades than the plan has, or trades that are not in the plan, where the reason is not a setup but the urge to do something.**

Think of a fisherman. A good fisherman casts the net when he sees a school. A bad fisherman casts all day, school or no school. In the end he is tired, and the boat is empty.

Nobody can give a fixed number for how many trades is too many. The limit depends on your plan. The test: **did today's trade follow the plan's setup?**

| Signal                               | Meaning                                                                      |
| :----------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| An entry with no setup               | Boredom or hurry                                                             |
| A new entry right after a loss       | Revenge ([Revenge](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/04-fomo-revenge-trading)) |
| More trades than the plan, every day | No limit, or the limit is not followed                                       |

## Step by step: stopping overtrading

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write the daily trade limit

For example at most two. The number depends on your strategy. Set it yourself.

### Step 2: Build a setup checklist

If the [entry model checklist](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/05-entry-model-checklist) does not match, skip.

### Step 3: One question before entry

"Am I seeing a setup, or do I just want to do something?"

### Step 4: Close the platform at the limit

Follow the [daily limit](/en/learn/08-risk-management/05-daily-weekly-limits) rules.

</Steps>

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

The "six trades" chart above is this failed example.

- **What was visible:** price in the 1.0902–1.0944 range, coming back after every touch of the high or the low.
- **Result:** six entries, six stops, -6R. The spread was paid every time too.
- **Why it failed:** there was no edge. The trader did not see an opportunity. They saw the urge to "do something".
- **Lesson:** saying "there is no direction in the range" is also a decision.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Why it happens              | What to do                                                                        |
| :--------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading from boredom                     | Tired of watching the chart | Keep trading time limited ([Routine](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/06-routine)) |
| Back-to-back entries on many timeframes  | Fear of missing a chance    | Analyze on one timeframe, enter on one                                            |
| More trades to win back a loss           | The urge for revenge        | Take a break after a loss                                                         |
| Thinking you are "active" by trade count | The feeling of working      | Judge by the quality of results                                                   |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write "did not trade today" in the journal. That is also a successful day, if
  no setup came.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Fewer trades is not always better. If you skip all the setups in the plan,
  that is also breaking the rules. The test is the setup, not the number.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If it is a scalping strategy:** naturally more trades. Set the limit and kind from that plan.
- **If the limit is too strict:** even good setups are dropped. Test and set the limit.
- **If the definition of a setup is unclear:** you cannot tell which is a trade of the plan.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Overtrading is trades without a setup or more than the plan.
- Every trade has a cost in spread and attention.
- Write the trade limit in advance, and skip when the setup does not match.
- Waiting is also a decision.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Count last week's trades. How many followed the plan's setup? Write the reason for the rest: boredom, revenge, or something else?
