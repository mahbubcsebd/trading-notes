# Discipline: Write the Plan and Follow It, With Three Simple Rules

When opening a trade, everyone knows where the stop is. But when price comes near the stop, the hand shakes. Thinking "let me watch a little more", the stop gets moved. This lesson is for that moment.

## What you will be able to do

- **Define discipline** as behavior that can be measured.
- **Recognize the three common ways** of breaking the plan.
- **Start the habit of following the plan** with **three rules**.

## Why it matters

- **If you do not follow the plan, you cannot test a strategy.** There is no way to know which rule is working.
- **One broken rule spoils the work of many correct trades.** Moving the [stop](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) raises the risk.
- **Discipline works in a moment of emotion.** Following rules in a calm period is easy.

Prerequisites: [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade).

## What is discipline

**Discipline is running the entry, stop, target and size written before the trade exactly as written, without changing the rules while it runs.**

Think of an airplane pilot's checklist. Even an experienced pilot works from the checklist, because memory makes mistakes under pressure. The plan is your checklist.

Discipline does not mean a harsh mind. It means rules written beforehand, so that you do not have to decide in a moment of pressure.

| Type  | Right                  | Wrong                        |
| :---- | :--------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| Entry | When the setup matches | When the mind says so        |
| Stop  | Stays where written    | Moves when price comes close |
| Size  | A fixed % of risk      | Changes with profit or loss  |
| Exit  | For a written reason   | From fear or greed           |

## Step by step: three rules

<Steps>

### Rule 1: Write before entry

Entry, stop, target, risk. In one line. If it is not written, there is no trade.

### Rule 2: The stop moves only toward less risk

Moving the stop away from price is forbidden. Bringing it closer or a [trailing](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/03-trailing-stop) stop is allowed, if it is written in the plan.

### Rule 3: After each trade, ask yourself

"Did I follow the written plan?" If yes, the trade was good, whatever the result. If not, it was a mistake.

</Steps>

Notice: a good trade does not mean a profit. A good trade means following the rules.

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, stop 1.0885, target 1.1020.
- **While running:** price fell to 1.0905 and did not touch the stop. The trader did nothing.
- **Result:** the target was hit. R:R about 1:2.
- **Lesson:** the job was resisting the urge to change the plan.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

- **What was visible:** price is falling toward the stop, and the trader thinks "it will come back".
- **Result:** closed at the moved stop. Three times the planned loss.
- **Why it failed:** moving the stop cancels the earlier calculation. Risk per trade was no longer 1%.
- **Lesson:** the decision to move a stop always comes from emotion, because in a calm state you would have written it in advance.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens            | What to do                                            |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------- |
| Moving the stop farther away       | Hard to accept the loss   | Write the stop before entry and place it in the order |
| Raising size after a win           | "Today is going well"     | Size from the formula, not from mood                  |
| Entering on "feeling" with no plan | Excitement from the chart | If you cannot write it, skip                          |
| Judging yourself only by profit    | Thinking by result        | Judge by following the rules                          |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Place the stop and target at the very moment you place the order. To move them
  later you have to change them on purpose, so the decision slows down.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Changing the rules is not forbidden. The condition: change them when no trade
  is open, in writing, after testing. Not in a running trade.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the plan itself is unclear:** "enter if it feels good" cannot be followed. Make the rules measurable.
- **If the plan was never tested:** if you follow a wrong plan, you will lose steadily.
- **If the risk is too high:** it is hard to follow rules at a big size. Cut your [position sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).
- **When tired or under mental stress:** not trading that day should be part of the plan.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Discipline means running the written plan without changing it while it runs.
- A good trade means following the rules, not making a profit.
- Never move a stop away from price.
- Write before entry, check after the trade.

</KeyTakeaways>
