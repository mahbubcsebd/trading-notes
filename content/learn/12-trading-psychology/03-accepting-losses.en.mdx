# Accepting Losses: See Loss as a Cost of Doing Business

Two losses in a row. The third setup comes. Does your hand freeze, or does stubbornness take over? Both are wrong reactions. This lesson is about the third way.

## What you will be able to do

- **Understand why** a loss hurts.
- **Count a loss as a cost** in your accounts.
- **See the price of not following the stop** in numbers.

## Why it matters

- **If you do not accept a loss, the stop breaks.** And one broken stop invites many losses.
- **A losing streak is normal.** Streaks come even when [expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy) is positive.
- **Your next decision gets worse with emotion.** [Revenge](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/04-fomo-revenge-trading) starts here.

Prerequisites: [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade) and [Drawdown Rules](/en/learn/08-risk-management/04-drawdown-rules).

## What is accepting losses

**Accepting losses means treating a planned loss as a normal cost of the business, taking it without emotion, and staying ready for the next setup.**

Think of a shopkeeper. Some goods will spoil, and the prices are set with that in mind. The shop does not close because of spoiled goods.

Research finds a tendency called **loss aversion** (the pain of a loss feels bigger than the joy of a gain). Because of it, we move the stop or hold the loser to avoid a loss. This is not weakness. It is human nature. That is why rules are needed.

|            | Accepting the loss   | Refusing the loss               |
| :--------- | :------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| Stop       | Closed when hit      | Moved or removed                |
| Next setup | Checked by the rules | Taken from stubbornness or fear |
| Accounting | In R (such as -1R)   | In money, with emotion          |

## Step by step: handling losses

<Steps>

### Step 1: Count the loss in R

Saying "-1R" is less emotional than "I lost 100 dollars".

### Step 2: Set the losing streak limit in advance

For example, a break after three losses in a row. Take the limit from your [daily and weekly limits](/en/learn/08-risk-management/05-daily-weekly-limits).

### Step 3: Stop for one minute after a loss

Get up, drink water. Close the chart. Then come back and look at the plan.

### Step 4: Write whether the loss followed the plan

If yes, it is a "cost". If not, it is a "mistake", which can be corrected.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk 1%, reward 1:2.
- **Result:** after five losses the account fell about 4.9%. In the following trades the streak turned, and the account ended above the start.
- **Lesson:** a run of losses is normal. With a small size you can survive it.

(Synthetic educational example. The result is not consistent. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the fifth trade is near the stop. The trader removes the stop hoping "it will come back".
- **Result:** the account is at about 9,378. If the stop had been followed, it would have been about 10,092.
- **Why it failed:** one loss not accepted wipes out the work of all the other correct trades.
- **Lesson:** take the small loss, avoid the big one.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Why it happens               | What to do                                                                                          |
| :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Removing the stop                           | Not wanting to take the loss | Keep the stop placed in the order                                                                   |
| Holding the loser, closing the winner early | Loss aversion                | A [written reason](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/05-trade-kokhon-exit-korbo) for the exit |
| Blaming yourself for a loss                 | Pressure to be perfect       | If it followed the plan, count it as a cost                                                         |
| Hiding a loss                               | Shame                        | Write it honestly in the journal                                                                    |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Call a loss a "fee". You do not bargain over a price; with a fee you buy a
  chance.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  After a losing streak, raising size or moving the stop makes the run worse. In
  the next lesson this is called revenge trading.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the strategy's expectancy is negative:** even if you accept the loss, the money will go. First test the strategy.
- **If the size is large:** even a small loss hurts. Cut the risk.
- **Money you cannot lose without endangering your living:** you should not trade with that money.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A loss is a normal cost of the business, if the risk is small and the rules are followed.
- People feel the pain of loss more, so the tendency to break the stop comes.
- One loss not accepted can spoil many correct trades.
- Count a loss in R, not in emotion.

</KeyTakeaways>
