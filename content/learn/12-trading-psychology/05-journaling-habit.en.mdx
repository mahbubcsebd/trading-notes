# Trading Journal: A Simple Habit for Catching the Pattern of Your Mistakes

At the end of the month you look at the account and feel "something is wrong," but you cannot say what. A journal keeps the answer to that question.

## What you will be able to do

- **Decide what information** to write in the journal.
- **Separate the types of mistakes** with **tags**.
- **Build the rules** for a **weekly review**.

## Why it matters

- **Memory is biased.** Wins are remembered as big and mistakes as small.
- **If you do not know the mistakes, they cannot be corrected.** To measure [Discipline](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/01-discipline) you need a journal.
- **You get the real result of the strategy.** To count [Expectancy](/en/learn/08-risk-management/07-expectancy) you need a list of trades.

## What is a journal

**A trading journal is a record where you write the reason, plan, result and emotion of each trade, to be reviewed later.**

Think of a cricketer's video analysis. After the match they watch the record and catch the weaknesses. A journal is the video of your own game.

At minimum, the journal should have these columns:

| Column                  | Example                            |
| :---------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Date, pair, direction   | 2026-11-03, EURUSD, Buy            |
| Setup name              | Breakout retest                    |
| Entry, stop, target     | 1.0958, 1.0940, 1.1040             |
| Risk (%) and result (R) | 1%, +2.4R                          |
| Reason for exit         | Target                             |
| Tag                     | Plan / FOMO / Moved stop / Revenge |
| One-line comment        | "I waited on the pullback"         |

**The tag is the life of the journal.** If it followed the plan, "Plan". If not, the type of mistake.

## Step by step: keeping a journal

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write the plan before entry

Setup, stop, target, risk. This is part of the [Plan](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/01-trading-plan-template).

### Step 2: At the end of the trade, write the result and the reason for the exit

Write in R. The reason: target, stop, invalidation, time, or emotion?

### Step 3: Give one tag

Make a short list of your mistakes. If it followed the plan, just "Plan".

### Step 4: Keep a screenshot

The chart at entry and at exit. When you look later, you will see the facts, not the feeling.

### Step 5: Review once a week by tag

How many R went to each tag? Make the most expensive mistake the only goal for the next week.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

<Chart
  id="journal-plan-only-equity-01"
  height={440}
  caption="An imaginary account: the candles show all twelve trades, and the orange line shows only the eight 'Plan' trades. The gap was shown by the journal."

- **Set in advance:** a tag on every trade.
- **What the review showed:** in the eight Plan trades, a total of +10R (taking 1% = 1R). In the four mistake trades, a total of -7R.
- **Result:** the most expensive mistake is Revenge (-3R). That is the goal for the next week.
- **Lesson:** the strategy was right. The problem was behavior.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** the trader wrote only profit and loss: "+120, -80, -60, +150." No tag, reason or screenshot.
- **Result:** at the end of the month the result is small. It was not clear which mistake kept happening.
- **Why it failed:** the numbers say what happened, but not why. Analysis is impossible.
- **Lesson:** without a reason and a tag, a journal is only an accounts book.

(Synthetic educational example.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                          | Why it happens         | What to do                                |
| :------------------------------- | :--------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| Writing only the winning trades  | Shame                  | All trades, especially the mistakes       |
| Writing from memory at day's end | Laziness               | Two minutes right at the end of the trade |
| Making many columns              | Pressure to be perfect | Start small, with the minimum columns     |
| Writing and never reading        | No time for review     | Set 30 minutes a week in the calendar     |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write the emotion in the journal too: "fear," "excitement," "boredom." After
  three weeks the pattern shows.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  Paper, Excel or an app: whichever you will write in regularly is the best. The
  habit matters more than the tool.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **With few trades:** it is hard to say a pattern from fewer than ten trades. Let more build up.
- **If you write lies:** if you cheat yourself, the journal means nothing.
- **If you only write and do not review:** the habit does not change.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A journal cuts the bias of memory.
- Write a reason and one tag on every trade.
- Review by tag once a week.
- Aim to fix one mistake at a time.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Build a simple spreadsheet: date, setup, R, reason for exit, tag. Write the next 10 trades in it (demo is fine too).
