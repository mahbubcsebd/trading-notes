# Gold Contract Spec and Pip Value: How Much Money Is a $1 Move

You are used to hearing "10 pips" on EURUSD. On gold, the word "pip" means different things at different brokers. So first see how much leaves your account for each $1 move.

## What you will be able to do

- **State the contract size** of gold.
- **Work out the value of each $1 move** for a given lot.
- **Avoid the confusion** between pip and point.

## Why it matters

- **A wrong calculation makes risk ten times bigger.** If you get the definition of a pip wrong, the stop amount is wrong.
- **Lot size stands on it.** [Gold Risk and Lot Size](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/08-gold-risk-lot-size) uses these numbers.
- **It differs by broker.** The same lot may not be equal everywhere.

Prerequisites: [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## What is a contract spec

**A contract spec is a list of how much of the asset one lot holds, the smallest step of the price, and the value of that step.**

Think of a kind of weight measure. One lot means a fixed weight. If you know that weight, you know how much is lost when the price moves one dollar.

At most brokers the rule is like this (check it with your broker):

| Item                        | Usual value     |
| :-------------------------- | :-------------- |
| 1.00 lot                    | 100 troy ounces |
| 0.10 lot                    | 10 ounces       |
| 0.01 lot                    | 1 ounce         |
| Price moves $1, on 1.00 lot | about $100      |
| Price moves $1, on 0.01 lot | about $1        |

Formula: **profit or loss = change in price ($) × number of ounces**.

## Pip vs point

In forex, the definition of a pip is almost universal. In gold it is not. Some brokers count 0.10 as one pip, and some count 0.01 as one point. So **when you hear "10 pips", confirm how many dollars it means from your broker's spec.**

A safe habit: when you say stop or target, say it **in dollars**. "Stop $8 away" is never unclear.

## Step by step: checking your own broker's spec

<Steps>

### Step 1: Open XAUUSD on the platform

In Market Watch, right-click the symbol and look at the Specification.

### Step 2: Find the contract size

It will usually show "100", which means 100 ounces.

### Step 3: Look at the minimum lot and step

See whether it is 0.01. On a small account this matters.

### Step 4: Check with a small calculation

On demo, open 0.01 lot and see how much the account changes when price moves $1.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** 0.05 lot, stop $6 away.
- **Calculation:** 0.05 lot = 5 ounces. Loss = 6 × 5 = $30.
- **Result:** if the stop is hit, exactly $30 is lost, matching the earlier calculation.
- **Lesson:** if you know the spec, you know the risk in money beforehand.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was assumed:** "A gold pip and a EURUSD pip are the same", so out of habit with EURUSD lots, 1.00 lot was opened.
- **What happened:** the stop was $5 away. On one lot $1 = $100, so when the stop was hit about $500 went.
- **Why it failed:** a forex habit was applied to gold.
- **Lesson:** when the instrument changes, look at the value of the lot again.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens                    | What to do                                                           |
| :--------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mixing up pip and point            | The definition differs by broker  | Say everything in dollars                                            |
| Using the forex lot habit          | Habit                             | Work out the value of each $1 first                                  |
| Assuming without checking the spec | Thinking all brokers are the same | Open the Specification and look                                      |
| Not counting the spread            | Mind on profit and loss           | See the [Spread lesson](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/07-spread-slippage) |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Make a small table: lot, ounces, value of each $1, and the loss if the stop is
  $5/$8/$10. Look it up instead of working it out every time.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Do not assume that at every broker 1 lot = 100 ounces. At some brokers it
  differs. Confirm from your own spec.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the account currency is not USD:** the conversion rate comes into the calculation, and the value changes slightly.
- **If the spec differs for CFD or futures:** look at the spec of your own product.
- **Leverage and margin calculations:** that is a separate topic, not in this lesson.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- At most brokers 1 lot = 100 ounces, so a $1 move is about $100.
- Profit or loss = change in price × number of ounces.
- The value of a pip differs by broker, so say it in dollars.
- Check the spec on your own platform.

</KeyTakeaways>
