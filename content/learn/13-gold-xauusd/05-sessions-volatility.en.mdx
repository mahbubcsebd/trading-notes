# Sessions and Volatility: When Gold Moves Most

The same stop is right in the morning and too small in the afternoon. Gold runs 24 hours, but the 24 hours are not all the same. If you know when the pace picks up, you can keep the stop and size right.

## What you will be able to do

- **Describe the character of the three main sessions.**
- **Understand the risk of the overlap.**
- **Arrange your plan by session.**

## Why it matters

- **Volatility is not equal.** The same stop is sometimes enough and sometimes too small.
- **Trades at the wrong time cost more.** In a quiet time a breakout is false, and in a busy time the stop is blown away.
- **It is the base of the later lessons.** The [London breakout](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/10-london-breakout-ny-reversal-setup) stands on this idea.

Prerequisites: [Why Gold Moves](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/01-why-gold-moves).

## What is a session

**A session is the part of the day when the main market of a region is open and the number of participants rises.**

Think of a bazaar. At dawn the shops are opening and there are few people. At noon there is a crowd and shouting of prices. In the evening it thins again. The gold market has the same rhythm.

| Session  | Usual character                           | Caution                              |
| :------- | :---------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Asia     | Comparatively calm, a narrow range        | A breakout is often false            |
| London   | Pace picks up, the day's direction is set | Both a range break and a false break |
| New York | The biggest moves, the center of news     | A reversal is possible               |
| Overlap  | London + NY together, the biggest crowd   | A small stop is hit fast             |

The exact times change with daylight saving and with the broker's server time. Mark on your own platform which candle each session starts at.

## Step by step: arranging by session

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark the sessions in your broker's time

Draw vertical lines on the chart for when London and New York open.

### Step 2: Write down the Asia range

The high and low: the reference for the London breakout.

### Step 3: Look at the session's ATR

In a busy time the stop needs to be bigger. See the [ATR lesson](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/09-gold-typical-atr).

### Step 4: Cut the size in a busy time

If the stop is bigger, the lot is smaller for the same risk.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** Asia calm, and in London the range breaks and the pace picks up.
- **Decision:** wait in Asia, and take a setup by the rules in London.
- **Result:** fewer bad signals, and a good move was caught.
- **Lesson:** if you pick your time to trade, you have to put yourself through less trouble.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** at the NY open, price broke a small range and went down.
- **Entry:** a sell with a small stop sized for Asia.
- **What happened:** the stop was hit by NY's big candle, and then price went back the earlier way.
- **Why it failed:** a quiet-time stop used in a busy time.
- **Lesson:** when the session changes, change the size of the stop too.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                        | Why it happens       | What to do                         |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Chasing breakouts in Asia      | Boredom              | Write the range limits and wait    |
| The same stop in every session | Convenience          | A different stop by looking at ATR |
| Not knowing the server time    | Assuming             | Mark the session starts            |
| A big lot in the overlap       | The chance feels big | Cut the lot                        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Draw three vertical lines on your chart: Asia start, London start, NY start.
  After a week the rhythm of the pace will catch your eye by itself.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  When daylight saving changes, the session start shifts by an hour. Match the
  lines twice a year.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On big news days:** the rhythm of the sessions breaks.
- **On holidays:** participation is low and the pace is unusual.
- **In the first and last hour of the week:** gaps and low liquidity.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Asia is usually calm, London has pace, and New York has the biggest moves.
- In the overlap the crowd is biggest and stops are hit fast.
- When the session changes, change the stop and size.
- Mark the sessions in your own broker's time.

</KeyTakeaways>
