# Safe-Haven Flow: Why Gold Rises on News of Fear

Suddenly there is news of a big conflict. In a few minutes the price of gold jumps. Many people enter in the middle of that jump, and a little later price comes back by half. Why?

## What you will be able to do

- **Say what** a **safe-haven flow** is.
- **Recognize on a chart** the pattern of spike and fade.
- **Set the rule** of waiting instead of chasing.

## Why it matters

- **On a spike the stop lands far away.** Chasing gives a bad risk-reward.
- **A fade is normal.** After the first panic passes, price often comes back a bit.
- **Emotion is highest here.** The most common stage for [FOMO](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/04-fomo-revenge-trading).

Prerequisites: [Risk-off and Gold](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/04-risk-on-risk-off).

## What is safe-haven flow

**Safe-haven flow is investors moving money from risky assets to assets seen as safe, such as gold, when uncertainty rises.**

Think of news of a fire, when everyone crowds at one door. The crowd is biggest in the first minute, and calms down a little later.

| Phase        | What happens             | What the trader does               |
| :----------- | :----------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| News arrives | A fast spike             | Stay out                           |
| Panic peaks  | Big candles, wide spread | No hurry                           |
| Calming down | A partial fade           | Look at the structure              |
| New balance  | A new range or trend     | Look for setups by the rules again |

## Step by step: what to do on news of fear

<Steps>

### Step 1: Do not enter on the first spike

In the first few candles the spread and slippage are big. Watch, do not grab.

### Step 2: Understand the kind of event

A one-time event, or one likely to spread? If it is the second, the effect is longer.

### Step 3: Wait for structure to form

Look for a retest of the old high, a new range, or a break of structure.

### Step 4: Small size, adjust for the big stop

If the stop is far, cut the lot. Follow the [lot size lesson](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/08-gold-risk-lot-size).

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** after the spike, gold came back part of the way and formed a new range.
- **Decision:** wait for a break above the range and a retest.
- **Result:** keeping the risk small, a setup matching the rules came.
- **Lesson:** if you wait, the risk-reward is better.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** gold is jumping up, with the fear "I will miss it".
- **Entry:** bought near the end of the spike, with no stop plan.
- **What happened:** price faded and came back down, and the loss was far bigger than planned.
- **Why it failed:** bought near the top, with a big stop and from emotion.
- **Lesson:** chasing a spike often means entering at the worst price.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                        | Why it happens             | What to do                               |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| Entering on the spike          | Fear of missing out        | At least wait for the retest             |
| Holding without a stop         | Hoping "it will come back" | Place the stop in the order              |
| Treating a fade as a new trend | Seeing a big candle        | No decision without looking at structure |
| Taking a big lot               | The chance feels big       | Cut the lot when the distance grows      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write a rule of no trading in the first 30 minutes on a day of fear news. When
  the rule is set in advance, emotion wins less.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  At the moment of news the spread can be very large. Even if your stop was not
  touched, you can be closed out because of the spread.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the event is big and continuing:** there may be no fade, and a new trend may begin.
- **If the dollar also runs the same way:** two forces pull opposite ways.
- **On a small timeframe:** more noise, unclear rules.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- In fear people take shelter in gold, so a fast spike happens.
- After a spike there is often a partial fade.
- Entering on the first spike is usually entering at a bad price.
- Waiting for a retest and structure gives a better risk-reward.

</KeyTakeaways>
