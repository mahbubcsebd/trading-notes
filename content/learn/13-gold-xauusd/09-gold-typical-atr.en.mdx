# Gold ATR: Measuring Your Own 'Normal Movement'

"How many dollars away should the stop be on gold?" has no fixed answer. The pace changes. ATR gives you a tape for measuring the current pace.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say what ATR is.**
- **Set the stop distance with ATR.**
- **Build the habit of measuring ATR** on your own timeframe.

## Why it matters

- **The pace of gold changes from time to time.** The stop that is enough today is too small tomorrow.
- **Without ATR the stop is a guess.** If it is small, noise takes it. If it is big, the risk rises.
- **Lot size stands on it.** When the stop changes, the [lot](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/08-gold-risk-lot-size) changes too.

Prerequisites: [ATR-based stop](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) and [Sessions](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/05-sessions-volatility).

## What is ATR

**ATR (Average True Range) is the average of how much price has moved, on average, in the last several candles.**

Think of a car's speedometer. It says how fast you are going now, not how fast you will go tomorrow. ATR is the same: a measure of the current pace.

In gold, ATR is read in dollars. An ATR of $5 (on H1) means each candle over the last few hours has moved about $5 on average.

| ATR         | Meaning                | Effect on the stop                |
| :---------- | :--------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Rising      | The pace is picking up | Bigger stop, smaller lot          |
| Falling     | The market is calm     | The stop can be smaller           |
| Sudden jump | News or an event       | Be careful, staying out is better |

No "normal" gold ATR is given here, because it changes with time and timeframe. Measure it from your own chart.

## Step by step: the stop with ATR

<Steps>

### Step 1: Add ATR(14)

On the timeframe of your trade.

### Step 2: Read the current value

In dollars, for example 5.0.

### Step 3: Pick a multiplier

For example stop = 1.5 × ATR. The multiplier is part of your plan; here it is an imaginary example.

### Step 4: Match it with structure

Place it beyond the swing high/low, not only by the number.

### Step 5: Work out the lot

Run the lot formula with the new stop distance.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the H1 ATR steadily at $4, with room of about $6 below the swing low.
- **Decision:** stop = 1.5 × 4 = $6, which matches the structure.
- **Result:** a small push of noise did not hit the stop.
- **Lesson:** using ATR and structure together makes the stop sensible.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was done:** last week's "worked well" $3 stop used again today.
- **What happened:** today the ATR is $7. The stop is passed by a normal candle.
- **Why it failed:** a measure of the old pace in a new market.
- **Lesson:** set the stop every day from the current ATR.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it happens               | What to do                        |
| :--------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| A fixed stop all the time          | Simplicity                   | Look at ATR each time             |
| Taking ATR as the future           | Trusting the number          | It is a measure of past pace      |
| Mixing ATR of different timeframes | Carelessness                 | The ATR of your trade's timeframe |
| Trusting ATR after news            | Thinking the jump is lasting | Look again when it calms          |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Think of ATR as a "tape". The tape does not say where you will go. It says how
  much room you need. The direction comes from structure and the setup.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  ATR is a lagging indicator. Even if ATR looks low just before news, price can
  move many times that at the release.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On big news or a gap:** ATR looks smaller than reality.
- **On a very new chart or with few candles:** the average is unreliable.
- **When the session changes:** the pace changes fast, and ATR lags.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- ATR is a measure of recent average movement. Read it in dollars.
- Stop = multiplier × ATR, then match it with structure.
- When ATR rises, the stop is bigger and the lot smaller.
- There is no fixed "normal" value. Measure it from your own chart.

</KeyTakeaways>
