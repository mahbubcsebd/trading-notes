# Spread and Slippage in Gold: Counting the Hidden Cost

The moment you open a trade, the account is a little red. But no loss has happened? That is the spread. And the stop closed at an unexpected price? That is slippage. Both can be big in gold.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say the difference** between spread and slippage.
- **Recognize when they rise.**
- **Count the cost** in the calculation of the stop and target.

## Why it matters

- **The price of gold is large, so the dollar amount of the spread is large too.**
- **On a small stop the cost is huge in proportion.** The spread can eat a big part of the stop.
- **It rises on news and in low liquidity.** Directly linked to [News Events](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/06-news-events).

Prerequisites: [Slippage and Execution](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening).

## What are spread and slippage

**The spread is the difference between the buy (ask) and sell (bid) prices, counted as a cost as soon as the trade opens. Slippage is the difference between the price you asked for and the price you actually got.**

Think of a currency exchange counter. The buying price is higher and the selling price is lower. The gap in between is the shopkeeper's income. The moment you open a trade, you are on one side of that gap.

| Item     | What                                        | When it is larger                         |
| :------- | :------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------- |
| Spread   | The difference between ask and bid          | News, the end of a session, low liquidity |
| Slippage | The gap between the asked and the got price | Fast market, news, stop orders            |

## Step by step: counting the cost

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write your broker's usual spread

How much in a quiet time and how much in a busy time: two numbers.

### Step 2: Compare it with the stop

If the spread is a big part of the stop (for example 20% or more), the cost of the trade is high.

### Step 3: Avoid news times or use a limit

A market order fills at a bad price during news. A limit order controls the price, but gives no certainty of being filled.

### Step 4: Leave room for slippage in the risk

Plan allowing for small slippage. On news it can be more than that.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** spread an imaginary $0.30, stop $6.
- **Calculation:** the spread is 5% of the stop, acceptable.
- **Result:** the trade runs by the rules, and the cost is within the calculation.
- **Lesson:** if you know the cost first, the stop and target are realistic.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** spread an imaginary $0.30, stop only $1.5.
- **Calculation:** the spread is 20% of the stop. In a busy time, when the spread grows, even more.
- **What happened:** right at entry about one-fifth of the risk is cost. A small move hit the stop.
- **Why it failed:** the cost was far too big in proportion to the stop.
- **Lesson:** on a very small stop the cost eats it. Set the size of the stop from ATR.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Why it happens           | What to do                          |
| :------------------------------ | :----------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Ignoring the spread             | The advertised "low"     | Measure it on your own platform     |
| A market order on news          | Hurry                    | Stay out, or enter on the retest    |
| A very small stop               | The illusion of low risk | Check the spread ratio              |
| Trading at the end of a session | Seeing it as a chance    | Liquidity is low, the spread is big |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write down the spread for a week: morning, noon, night. If you know your own
  broker's average and maximum, the cost will not surprise you.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A low spread is not always good. At some brokers the spread is low but there
  is a commission. Compare the total cost.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **By broker type:** fixed or variable spreads behave differently.
- **On a very big position:** extra slippage because of size.
- **When the market opens after being closed:** with a gap, the stop may not work.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Spread is the cost at the moment of entry, and slippage is the gap between asked and got price.
- Both rise on news and in low liquidity.
- When the stop is small, the share of the spread is big.
- Count the cost before the trade.

</KeyTakeaways>
