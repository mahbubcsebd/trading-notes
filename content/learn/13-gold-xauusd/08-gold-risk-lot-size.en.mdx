# Gold Risk and Lot Size: Working Out the Lot From the Stop

If you set the lot first and then place the stop, the risk goes by guesswork. Work it the other way: first the risk, then the stop, and last the lot. In gold this order matters even more.

## What you will be able to do

- **Work out the lot size** from the stop's dollar distance.
- **Understand why** to keep the lot small.
- **Know what to do** when the account is small.

## Why it matters

- **The value of each $1 move in gold is big.** By the [contract spec](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/02-gold-contract-spec-pip-value), on 1 lot $1 = about $100.
- **The stop is often bigger in dollars than in forex.** So the lot is smaller for the same risk.
- **A wrong lot multiplies the risk several times.**

Prerequisites: [Risk per Trade](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade) and [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## The formula

**Lot = risk in money ÷ (stop distance in $ × 100)**

Here 100 is taken for 1 lot = 100 ounces. If your broker differs, put in that number.

| Step                 | Example (imaginary) |
| :------------------- | :------------------ |
| Account              | $5,000              |
| Risk (1%)            | $50                 |
| Stop distance        | $8                  |
| Lot = 50 ÷ (8 × 100) | 0.0625              |
| Rounded down         | **0.06 lot**        |

Round down, not up, so that the risk does not pass the limit. If the stop is hit on 0.06 lot, the loss is 8 × 6 = $48.

## Step by step: working out the lot

<Steps>

### Step 1: Set the risk in money

1% of the account, or the limit in your plan.

### Step 2: Place the stop from the chart

By looking at structure or [ATR](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/09-gold-typical-atr), not by looking at the lot.

### Step 3: Measure the distance in dollars

The difference from entry to stop.

### Step 4: Put it in the formula, round down

To your broker's lot step (for example 0.01).

### Step 5: If the risk is over on the minimum lot, drop the trade

If the risk is above the limit even at 0.01 lot, do not shrink the stop. Leave the trade.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** risk $50, stop $8, lot 0.06.
- **Result:** if the stop is hit, the loss is $48, within the limit.
- **Lesson:** stop first, lot after, and the risk stays under control.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was done:** a rule of "always 0.50 lot" on a $5,000 account.
- **What happened:** when an $8 stop is hit, the loss is 8 × 50 = $400, which is 8%. In three losses, 24%.
- **Why it failed:** the lot was not matched to the stop distance.
- **Lesson:** not a fixed lot, but a fixed risk.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Why it happens                   | What to do                                    |
| :------------------------------ | :------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Using a fixed lot               | Simplicity                       | The formula every time                        |
| Fitting the stop to the lot     | Wanting a big lot                | Stop from the chart, lot from the calculation |
| Rounding up                     | Thinking the difference is small | Always round down                             |
| Leaving out spread and slippage | Keeping the math simple          | Keep a small buffer                           |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Put the formula in Excel or notes: give it risk $ and stop $ and it returns
  the lot. A 10-second job before a trade.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Gold is hard on a small account. On 0.01 lot ($1 ≈ $1), an $8 stop means $8
  risk. On a $200 account that is 4%, far above the limit.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the account currency is not USD:** add the conversion.
- **With a gap or slippage:** the real loss can be more than the calculation.
- **If the broker's contract size is different:** change the 100 in the formula.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- First risk, then stop, last lot.
- Lot = risk ÷ (stop $ × 100), rounded down.
- On a small account, gold's minimum lot alone can be a big risk.
- Not a fixed lot, but a fixed risk.

</KeyTakeaways>
