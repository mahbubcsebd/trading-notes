# DXY and US Yields: How the Dollar and Interest Rates Push Gold

Gold is falling, but there is no news. On the chart beside it, the dollar and the yield are rising. These two charts are a gold trader's closest allies.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say what the DXY and the US 10-year yield are.**
- **Recognize on a chart** the relationship with gold.
- **Be careful** when the relationship breaks.

## Why it matters

- **They are the daily background of gold.** The fastest way to read direction.
- **You can avoid the wrong side.** When the dollar and yield rise, it is hard to buy gold.
- **The knowledge of the earlier chapter gets deeper.** You come here from [central banks and interest rates](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/01-central-bank-interest-rate-inflation).

Prerequisites: [Why Gold Moves](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/01-why-gold-moves).

## What are the DXY and the yield

**The DXY is an index of the strength of the dollar against six major currencies. The US 10-year yield is the annual rate of return on a 10-year US government bond.**

Think of two shops. In one you get interest (the bond), and in the other you can keep gold, which gives nothing. When the bond interest rises, people move from gold to bonds, and pressure falls on gold.

| Change      | Usual effect on gold |
| :---------- | :------------------- |
| DXY rises   | Pressure down        |
| DXY falls   | Favorable up         |
| Yield rises | Pressure down        |
| Yield falls | Favorable up         |

## Step by step: checking before a trade

<Steps>

### Step 1: Put three charts side by side

XAUUSD, DXY, US10Y, on the same timeframe.

### Step 2: Write the direction of the two

Write whether the DXY and the yield are up, down or sideways.

### Step 3: Does gold's setup match that direction

While the dollar and yield are rising, a sell setup in gold matches, not a buy setup.

### Step 4: If it does not match, cut the size or drop it

When the signals conflict, waiting is the cheapest decision.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the yield rising steadily, and gold making lower highs.
- **Entry:** sell on gold's retest, stop above the recent high.
- **Result:** because the yield stayed in its trend, gold fell and reached the target.
- **Lesson:** when macro and chart are in the same direction, confidence in the setup rises.

(Synthetic educational example. The result is not consistent. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the yield is rising, so a sell.
- **What happened:** the same week, on news of fear, gold and the yield both rose together for a while. The sell hit its stop.
- **Why it failed:** the relationship is not always fixed; another force overwhelmed it.
- **Lesson:** the macro signal is background, not a trigger. Without a stop and an entry from the chart, it is not enough.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens             | What to do                                                 |
| :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating the DXY as an entry signal          | Looking for an easy rule   | The DXY is background, the entry comes from the gold chart |
| Comparing without matching timeframes        | Hurry                      | All charts on the same timeframe                           |
| Being stubborn after the relationship breaks | Memory of earlier profit   | Respect the signal of a break                              |
| Ignoring the yield completely                | Looking only at the dollar | Look at both                                               |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you see the relationship between gold and the DXY break (both up
  together), write it down. It is often a hint of another big force, such as a
  flow of fear.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The euro has the biggest weight in the DXY. So the movement of the DXY is
  largely a shadow of EURUSD. It is not the dollar's strength against all
  currencies.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **During a safe-haven flow:** gold and the dollar can rise together.
- **At the moment of high-impact news:** the relationship breaks for a while.
- **On a small timeframe:** the relationship is blurred, with more noise.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A rising dollar and yield is usually against gold, and a falling one is for it.
- They are background, not a trigger.
- The entry and stop come from gold's own chart.
- Be careful when the relationship breaks.

</KeyTakeaways>
