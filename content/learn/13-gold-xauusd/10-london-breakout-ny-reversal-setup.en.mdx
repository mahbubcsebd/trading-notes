# London Breakout and NY Reversal: Two Session-Based Setups

In Asia price sleeps, and in London it wakes. An old setup was born from this rhythm. The other, the NY reversal, is attractive but often a trap. This lesson shows both with rules.

## What you will be able to do

- **Write the rules** of the **London breakout setup**.
- **Understand why** the **NY reversal is hard**.
- **Set the stop, target and the conditions to skip** for both setups.

## Why it matters

- **The session rhythm is the most repeated pattern.** A direct use of the [session lesson](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/05-sessions-volatility).
- **When the rules are clear, emotion falls.** Entry, stop and target can be written in advance.
- **Trying to catch a reversal causes the most losses.**

Prerequisites: [Breakout and Retest](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/01-breakout-retest-pullback-entry).

## What the two setups are

**The London breakout is entering in the direction in which London closes after breaking the Asia session's range. The NY reversal is trying to enter in NY against the direction of London.**

Think of a door closed overnight. When the door opens in the morning, the crowd runs in one direction, and the pressure in the first hour is that way. In the afternoon, it is hard to stand against that crowd.

| Item       | London breakout                  | NY reversal                          |
| :--------- | :------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| Idea       | Pace comes when the range breaks | A tired trend can reverse            |
| Entry      | A close outside the range        | After the structure breaks           |
| Stop       | Just inside the range            | Beyond the last extreme of the trend |
| Risk       | A false breakout                 | The trend keeps going                |
| Difficulty | Medium                           | Hard                                 |

## Step by step: the London breakout

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark the Asia range

Mark the high and low at the end of Asia. It is good if the range is narrow enough.

### Step 2: Wait for London to open

Just watch the first few candles. See whether the spread is normal.

### Step 3: Look for a close outside the range

Not just a wick, but a close with the candle body.

### Step 4: Set the stop, target and lot

The stop just inside the range, the target at least 1:2. Work out the lot with the [lot](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/08-gold-risk-lot-size) formula.

### Step 5: Check the news and the previous day's direction

If big news is near, skip.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** a narrow Asia range, and a close above it in London.
- **Entry:** a buy after the close; the stop inside the range.
- **Result:** the 1:2 target was reached (shown on the chart).
- **Lesson:** when the range is narrow, the stop is small and the risk-reward is good.

(Synthetic educational example. The result is not consistent. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** a steady rise in London, and a sell in NY, thinking "it has come up a lot".
- **Entry:** a short with no proof that the structure broke.
- **What happened:** in NY the trend got strong again, and the stop was hit.
- **Why it failed:** going the other way against a running trend, only because of a "high price".
- **Lesson:** a reversal needs proof. A high price is not proof.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                       | Why it happens            | What to do                                                       |
| :---------------------------- | :------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entering on a wick alone      | Hurry                     | Wait for a close with the body                                   |
| Trading a wide Asia range too | Loose rules               | If the range is big, skip                                        |
| An NY reversal with no proof  | The idea "it's very high" | Not without seeing the structure break                           |
| A breakout near news          | Seeing it as a chance     | Follow the [news rules](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/06-news-events) |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  First practice only the London breakout, 20 times on demo. Before moving to
  the NY reversal, see how many times the structure broke and the reversal was
  real.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  These setups are no guarantee. A false breakout is normal. Always set the stop
  in advance.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On big news days:** the range breaks early or runs away.
- **A wide Asia range:** the stop is big and the risk-reward is bad.
- **On holidays:** London participation is low and the breakout is weak.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- London breakout: a close outside the Asia range, the stop inside, the target at least 1:2.
- A narrow range gives a good risk-reward.
- The NY reversal is hard; not without proof of a structure break.
- Avoid both near news.

</KeyTakeaways>
