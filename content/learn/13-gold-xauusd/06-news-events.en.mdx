# News Events (NFP, CPI, FOMC): The Most Dangerous Moments for Gold

The chart is calm. The setup is clean. Then in one minute price runs both ways and blows away the stop. In gold, such news moments come many times a year.

## What you will be able to do

- **Say briefly what NFP, CPI and FOMC are.**
- **Recognize a whipsaw** on news.
- **Set the rules** for staying out or entering later.

## Why it matters

- **Gold moves on news through the dollar and yields.** All three news items shake exactly those drivers ([Why Gold Moves](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/01-why-gold-moves)).
- **Spread rises, and there is slippage.** The trade may not close at the price where the stop was placed.
- **One event can wipe out a week's profit.**

Prerequisites: [Economic Calendar](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/02-economic-calendar-nfp-cpi-fomc) and [News Trading](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo).

## The three news items

| News | Meaning                                      | Why it moves gold                                              |
| :--- | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| NFP  | The US employment report (monthly)           | Strength of the economy, and interest rate expectations change |
| CPI  | Consumer price index (inflation)             | Interest rates and inflation expectations                      |
| FOMC | The US central bank's interest rate decision | Directly interest rates and the dollar                         |

Look up the time and date of release on the economic calendar. This lesson does not give dates.

## Step by step: the rules for a news day

<Steps>

### Step 1: Look at the calendar in the morning

Write down when the high-impact news is.

### Step 2: Decide on open trades before the news

Close them beforehand, or make the stop safer. No new trades.

### Step 3: Wait after the release

Nothing in the first few minutes. Look when the spread is normal.

### Step 4: Decide by looking at the retest

When structure forms after the spike, check the setup by the rules.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Set in advance:** no new trades from 30 minutes before NFP.
- **What happened:** a whipsaw in both directions at the release.
- **Result:** the account is untouched. Later a clean setup on the retest.
- **Lesson:** a trade not taken is also a decision, and often the best.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** a setup to break above the range, with the release one minute away.
- **Entry:** a buy, thinking "this time the data will be good".
- **What happened:** the data came out opposite to expectation. Price ran past the stop, with slippage.
- **Why it failed:** guessing the result and ignoring event risk.
- **Lesson:** guessing whether data is good or bad is not a trade. It is a gamble.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                     | Why it happens            | What to do                               |
| :-------------------------- | :------------------------ | :--------------------------------------- |
| Entering before the release | Greed                     | Write the rule: closed 30 minutes before |
| Chasing the first spike     | FOMO                      | Wait for the retest                      |
| Keeping a narrow stop       | The habit of a normal day | Cut the size or stay out around news     |
| Not checking the calendar   | Forgetting                | Add it to the morning routine            |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Print the weekly calendar and highlight the high-impact news. For gold,
  USD-related news is usually the heavy kind.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  On news, a stop loss does not guarantee closing at a certain price. With a gap
  or slippage, the real loss can be bigger than planned.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **On unexpected news** (not on the calendar): staying out in advance is not possible. That is why a small size matters.
- **On speeches or statements:** the time may not be exactly known in advance.
- **In a long-term position:** avoiding news is hard, so keep the size small.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- NFP, CPI and FOMC move gold's interest rate and dollar expectations.
- At the release price can run both ways.
- Staying out before, and entering on the retest after, is safer.
- Guessing the data is not a trade.

</KeyTakeaways>
