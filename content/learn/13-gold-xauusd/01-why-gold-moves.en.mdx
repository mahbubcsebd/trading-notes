# Why Gold Moves: What Makes the Price of Gold Rise and Fall

Gold has no company and no profit and loss account. Still, its price moves every day. Why? This lesson gives the list of reasons.

## What you will be able to do

- **Name the five main drivers** of gold.
- **Recognize which cause pushes which way.**
- **Understand the danger** of deciding from **one cause**.

## Why it matters

- **If you do not know the causes, you cannot understand the news.** On the same news, gold sometimes rises and sometimes falls.
- **Technical signals break in front of news.** You will see the result in the [News Events](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/06-news-events) lesson.
- **All the later lessons stand on this one.**

Prerequisites: [Gold vs DXY](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/05-correlation-gold-vs-dxy).

## What is the price of gold

**The price of gold is the price of an asset that has no interest or dividend, but that people buy for safety and to hold value.**

Think of a strongbox. Gold kept in a strongbox earns nothing. So if you can get more interest somewhere else, gold becomes less attractive. And when fear grows, the strongbox becomes more attractive.

## The five main drivers

| Driver                           | Usual effect on gold | Why                                                                       |
| :------------------------------- | :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A strong dollar                  | Pressure down        | Gold is measured in dollars, and when the dollar is dear gold looks cheap |
| Rising interest rates and yields | Pressure down        | Gold pays no interest, so the cost of holding it rises                    |
| Fear and uncertainty             | Pull up              | Bought as a safe haven                                                    |
| Inflation worries                | Often up             | Seen as an asset that holds value                                         |
| Demand (banks, jewelry, ETFs)    | Long-term effect     | Real buying and selling                                                   |

These are **general tendencies**, not certainties. On the same day two causes can pull in opposite directions.

## Step by step: who is pushing gold today

<Steps>

### Step 1: Which way is the dollar

Look at the DXY chart. Rising is against gold, falling is for it.

### Step 2: Which way are yields

See whether the US 10-year yield is rising or falling. More in the [next lesson](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/03-dxy-us-yields).

### Step 3: Is there fear in the market

See whether there is news of a big event or conflict.

### Step 4: Are the causes in the same direction

If all three point the same way, the pressure is strong. If they conflict, the signal is weak, and waiting is better.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the DXY is rising step by step, yields are rising too, and there is no news of fear.
- **Decision:** do not try to buy gold. The signal is down, so look for a sell setup.
- **Lesson:** when two causes point the same way, the picture is clear.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What was visible:** the DXY is rising, so it was assumed gold would fall. A sell was placed.
- **What happened:** at the same time news of a big conflict arrived. With fear buying, gold reversed up.
- **Why it failed:** one cause was seen and the others were ignored.
- **Lesson:** [safe-haven](/en/learn/13-gold-xauusd/04-safe-haven-flow-geopolitical-event) flow can overwhelm the pressure of the dollar.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Why it happens           | What to do                                  |
| :------------------------------------ | :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| Trading by looking only at the DXY    | The pull of an easy rule | Look at yields and news too, at least       |
| Treating a relationship as a rule     | It worked in the past    | Take it as a tendency, not a certainty      |
| Entering right after reading the news | Hurry                    | Watch how price reacts first                |
| Dropping technicals completely        | Trusting fundamentals    | You still need the chart for entry and stop |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Write three lines every day: dollar, yield, fear. Once it is a habit, you can
  read the "mood" of gold's day at a glance.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Relationships change. At some times gold and the dollar can rise together.
  Never rely on a relationship without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **At the moment of big news:** all relationships can break.
- **On a small timeframe:** on M1–M5 there is more noise and less cause.
- **When two causes pull opposite ways:** there is no clear direction.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Gold pays no interest, so it usually moves opposite to the dollar and yields.
- In times of fear, gold rises as a safe haven.
- Look at the causes together, not alone.
- A relationship is a tendency, not a certainty.

</KeyTakeaways>

**1.** Gold is measured in dollars. When the dollar is dear, gold is expensive for buyers using other currencies.

**2.** Gold pays no interest, so when the alternative of interest is attractive, the cost of holding gold rises.

**4.** If another cause pulls the other way, the signal can be wrong.
