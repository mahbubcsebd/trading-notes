# Sessions and Kill Zones: When in the Day to Look for SMC/ICT

The same setup works in the morning and not in the afternoon. The reason is that who is in the market, and how much they trade, changes through the day. ICT put this timing at the centre of the teaching. This lesson shows what to look for at which time, and how to use time as a filter.

## What you will be able to do

- Describe the role of the three sessions.
- Understand what a kill zone is and why it exists.
- Draw an Asia range.
- Convert times to your own time zone (Bangladesh).

## Why it matters

Liquidity is taken most when participation rises. Without knowing those hours, you will watch the chart for hours when nothing happens, or when something happens and you are asleep. For gold, the London and New York overlap is especially active, a general observation.

## Core idea

### The three sessions

- **Asia:** usually quiet, builds a narrow range. In the ICT picture, this range's high and low are the next liquidity.
- **London:** often the first big move; takes one side of the Asia range, then shows the direction of the day.
- **New York:** may continue or reverse London's move. News is also heavier here.

Analogy: a day at the market. Quiet at dawn, then the crowd gathers, busiest around noon.

### Kill zone

**A kill zone is a time window set by ICT in which sweeps and large moves are most often looked for.** ICT teaching commonly cites (in New York time):

| Zone        | New York time |
| :---------- | :------------ |
| Asia        | 20:00–00:00   |
| London      | 02:00–05:00   |
| New York AM | 07:00–10:00   |

These times differ a little by teacher, and the hours shift when daylight saving time (DST) changes. Verify them yourself; do not memorise them. To convert to Bangladesh time (UTC+6), work out the difference from New York (UTC−5 in winter, UTC−4 in summer).

## Failed example: the day that did not reverse

<Chart
  id="smc-sess-02"

A move above the Asia high in the London kill zone is not always a sweep. Here the body closed above, never came back inside, and the Asia low was never touched. The time was "right" but there was no signal.

The rule: **a kill zone tells you when to look, not which way to trade.**

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it hurts                  | Fix                         |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| Treating the kill zone as a signal | Right time, wrong setup       | Need a sweep plus structure |
| Forgetting DST                     | Wrong hours, missed setup     | Verify the times            |
| Trading every session              | Impatience and overtrading    | Pick your own 2–3 hours     |
| Mixing up broker time and UTC      | Asia range in the wrong place | Match the chart's time zone |

<Callout type="tip">
  Pick one window that fits your life (for example the first two hours of
  London) and watch only that for a few months. Trying to catch every hour tends
  to give worse results; this is common experience, not proven.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Asia builds the range; London and New York take its high and low.
- Kill zone = ICT's time window (London 02:00–05:00, NY AM 07:00–10:00, New York time).
- Times vary by teacher and by DST; verify them.
- Time is a filter, not a signal; no entry without a sweep and structure.

</KeyTakeaways>
