# SMC/ICT Entry Model: Sweep, CHoCH, FVG and the Silver Bullet

So far you have learned the parts separately. Now you will join them into one written rule so that you ask the same questions in the same order on every trade. Without a written rule, trades are made on "feel", and cannot be backtested.

## What you will be able to do

- Write a complete entry model step by step.
- Place the stop and target by the model's rules.
- Understand ICT's Silver Bullet.
- Know why even a perfect sequence can fail.

## Why it matters

Lessons 1–6 gave the ingredients. With the ingredients apart, every gap becomes an entry. Putting the sequence and the filters together cuts the number of signals and raises their quality. For stops and position size, see [Stop Loss Placement](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement) and [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## Core idea

### The basic entry model (long)

1. **Bias:** the higher timeframe (H4/D1) is bullish, or at least not bearish.
2. **Time:** a kill zone or a window you chose.
3. **Sweep:** price takes SSL (goes below a low and the body returns inside).
4. **CHoCH:** a body close breaks the last lower high.
5. **Zone:** the FVG (or OB) the impulse left behind.
6. **Entry:** when price returns to the zone, ideally in discount.
7. **Stop:** below the sweep low.
8. **Target:** opposing liquidity (the previous high), at least 1:2.

For a short, reverse everything.

### Silver Bullet

**The Silver Bullet is an ICT time-based model: after liquidity is taken inside a specific one-hour window, enter on the FVG.** The windows commonly cited in ICT teaching (New York time) are 03:00–04:00, 10:00–11:00 and 14:00–15:00. Times differ by teacher, so verify them. The idea is the same: a sweep inside the window, a return to the FVG, a target at opposing liquidity. It is a narrower form of the model above.

<Steps>

### Step 1: Bias and time

Confirm the direction and the window.

### Step 2: Sweep

The low is taken and the body comes back inside.

### Step 3: CHoCH

A body close above the last lower high.

### Step 4: Draw the FVG

The three-candle gap in the impulse.

### Step 5: Enter on the pullback

With a limit order or after a reaction signal, with the stop in place.

### Step 6: Target

The previous high or 1:2, whichever comes first.

</Steps>

## Failed example: a perfect sequence against the bigger trend

<Chart
  id="smc-entry-fail-01"
  height={460}
  caption="XAUUSD H1 (illustrative synthetic data) — low swept, CHoCH up, buy in the FVG. Every step is textbook, but the higher-timeframe trend is down: the bounce ends and the stop is hit."
/>

The sequence is perfect, yet it loses. The reason is that the lower-timeframe CHoCH was a pullback inside a larger move down. If the first checklist question (bias) is "no", the trade should not have been taken even with all the others "yes".

The rule: **the model only times the entry; the bias decides the direction.**

This loss is normal. The stop was small, so the loss was controlled. That is the real job of the model.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Why it hurts                                       | Fix                          |
| :------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| Skipping the bias               | A small-timeframe CHoCH drowns in the bigger trend | First checklist question     |
| Not placing a stop              | Unlimited loss if the model is wrong               | Stop before entry            |
| Looking for the model every day | Quality falls, count rises                         | Sit out when no signal comes |
| Not writing the target          | Exiting from greed or fear                         | Write the target first       |
| Changing the rules repeatedly   | Backtest is meaningless                            | One version, 50 trades       |

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Model: bias → time → sweep → CHoCH → FVG/OB → pullback entry → stop → target.
- Silver Bullet = a narrower, one-hour-window form of ICT.
- Even a perfect sequence fails; most often when there is no bias.
- Place the stop first; keeping losses controlled is the model's job.
- Write the rules and backtest 50 trades.

</KeyTakeaways>
