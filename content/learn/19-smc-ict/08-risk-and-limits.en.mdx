# Limits and Risks of SMC/ICT: Where It Fails and How to Test It

Every lesson in this chapter said how to use it. This lesson says how far to trust it. In videos the model looks perfect, because the examples shown are selected. Your own money does not run on selected examples.

## What you will be able to do

- State the evidence status of SMC/ICT honestly.
- Recognise hindsight bias.
- Count the model's real risks (news, spread, subjectivity).
- Apply a rule set to test the model in your own backtest.

## Why it matters

High subjectivity means two people make different decisions on the same chart. Then the question "does the model work?" can only be answered with your own written rules and your own data.

## Core idea

### Evidence status

- **Independent research:** we know of no verified, independent research on the specific SMC/ICT models (order block, FVG, kill zone, AMD). This does not mean they do not work; it means they are unproven.
- **General technical analysis:** research on the profitability of chart-based methods is mixed, with evidence that results shrink over time ([Park & Irwin, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6419.2007.00519.x)). That is about technical analysis in general, not SMC.
- **Teachers' claims:** videos, courses and screenshots are not evidence; they are selected examples.

### Hindsight bias

**Hindsight bias is the feeling, after the event, that it was obviously clear beforehand.** It is especially strong in SMC, because looking back you can find a sweep, a CHoCH and an FVG in almost any move.

### Real risks

| Risk                | Why                                                          | What to do                                                                                                          |
| :------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Subjectivity        | Definitions of swing, OB and CHoCH differ by teacher         | Your own written rules, one version                                                                                 |
| News                | Many news releases fall in kill zones, price jumps           | Avoid news times or widen the stop                                                                                  |
| Spread and slippage | Spreads widen at the start of a window, gaps on limit orders | Count it as an entry cost ([Slippage](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening)) |
| Stop size           | Taking the whole OB range makes risk large                   | Size the lot from the stop                                                                                          |
| Overtrading         | Looking for the model every day                              | Do not enter when no signal comes                                                                                   |

## How to test it

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write the rules

The lesson 7 checklist in your own words.

### Step 2: Go back with the right side covered

Scroll the chart with the right side hidden; avoid hindsight.

### Step 3: Record every setup

Winners and losers, all of them. At least 50.

### Step 4: Include costs

Compute results with spread and slippage included.

### Step 5: Read the results

Expectancy, maximum consecutive losses. If you change the rules, start again.

### Step 6: Move on to demo

If the results are good, at least one month on a demo account.

</Steps>

More detail: [How to Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest).

<Callout type="warning">
  Even 50 trades is a small sample; good results can still be luck. Start small
  and keep risk low even when the results look good.
</Callout>

## When to drop the model

- Negative expectancy over 50 trades by your own rules.
- You are repeatedly tempted to take trades that break the rules.
- Recognising a signal gives a different decision every time (the rules are not clear enough).

Dropping it is not failure; it is a decision based on data. Other approaches: [Methodology comparison](/en/learn/16-trading-methodologies/00-overview).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | Why it hurts           | Fix                         |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| Treating a video example as proof                  | It is selected         | Your own 50 trades          |
| Recording only the winning setups                  | Results inflate        | Record the losses too       |
| Leaving out costs                                  | Real results are lower | Include spread and slippage |
| Changing rules after bad results and testing again | Overfitting            | Test on new data            |

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- We know of no independent, verified evidence for the SMC/ICT models; neither proven nor disproven.
- Hindsight bias is strong: test with the right side covered.
- News, spread, subjectivity and stop size are real risks.
- Your own written rules, at least 50 trades, with costs, wins and losses both.
- Start small, on demo first.

</KeyTakeaways>
