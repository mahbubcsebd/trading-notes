# Premium, Discount and OTE: At What Price to Enter

The zone is right, the direction is right, yet the entry price puts the stop far away and the target close. The risk:reward is poor. This lesson shows which part of a range is cheap to buy, which part is expensive, and what ICT's OTE zone is.

## What you will be able to do

- Draw a dealing range.
- Recognise equilibrium, premium and discount.
- Work out the OTE zone (0.62–0.79).
- Understand when this idea does not work.

## Why it matters

Same idea, better entry price: a smaller stop and a bigger target. The basic idea is in the [Premium and Discount lesson](/en/learn/03-market-structure/06-premium-discount-zones) and the Fibonacci base is in the [Fibonacci Retracement lesson](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/05-fibonacci-basics). Here is ICT's specific use.

## Core idea

**A dealing range is the span from a recent important swing low to a swing high.** Its middle is **equilibrium (EQ)**, 50%.

- **Premium:** the upper half above EQ. Good for selling, expensive for buying.
- **Discount:** the lower half below EQ. Good for buying, cheap for selling.

Analogy: prices in a market. Buying below the middle price leaves more room for profit; buying above leaves less.

### OTE (Optimal Trade Entry)

**OTE is the area between the 62% and 79% retracement of an impulse.** ICT teaching singles out 70.5%. It is drawn with Fibonacci levels, and the numbers are a common convention; the market is not obliged to respect them.

### The rule: direction first, price second

Premium/discount does not give direction. Direction comes from structure (lesson 1). In an uptrend, look for buys only in discount; avoid buying in premium.

<Steps>

### Step 1: Pick the swing

In an uptrend, the last important low to the new high.

### Step 2: Draw EQ

50% between the low and the high.

### Step 3: Place the OTE zone

62% and 79% retracement from the high.

### Step 4: Wait for a signal in the zone

Price arriving in the zone is not enough; only if an OB/FVG from lesson 3 lines up.

### Step 5: Stop and target

Stop below the range low. Target near the high.

</Steps>

In this example the risk:reward is about 1:2. An entry at a cheaper price allows a smaller stop. That is the real benefit of the idea.

## Failed example

<Chart
  id="smc-pd-02"

/>

Discount only says that price is cheaper inside the range. Once the range low breaks, the range no longer exists, so the zone means nothing. That is why the stop sits beyond the range low: the idea ends there.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                            | Why it hurts            | Fix                               |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Entering because of the zone alone | No reason               | Match OB/FVG and structure        |
| Picking the wrong swing            | Zone in the wrong place | Higher-timeframe swing            |
| Buying discount in a downtrend     | Cheap gets cheaper      | Direction first                   |
| Treating the numbers as law        | 62–79 is not a rule     | Think of it as a zone, not a line |

<Callout type="tip">
  When the range changes (a new high forms), redraw EQ and OTE. Do not hold on
  to the old zone.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Dealing range = swing low to swing high; EQ = 50%.
- Premium for selling, discount for buying; but direction comes from structure.
- OTE = 62–79% retracement; a common convention, not a law of nature.
- If the range breaks, the zone is void; the stop is there.

</KeyTakeaways>
