# SMC / ICT in Depth: From Liquidity to Entry Models

In SMC videos everything looks perfect: a sweep, then a CHoCH, then an FVG, then profit. Open your own chart and the same pattern appears everywhere, and none of it works. The reason: the terms were learned one by one, but the **order** and the **context** were not.

This chapter fills that gap: each idea gets its own lesson, then everything is combined into one entry model, and the chapter ends with honest limits.

## What SMC and ICT are

**ICT** (Inner Circle Trader) is the teaching of Michael J. Huddleston, where most of the terms and models come from. **SMC** (Smart Money Concepts) is the simplified, community-built version of those ideas.

|                 | SMC                           | ICT                                       |
| :-------------- | :---------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| Main focus      | Structure, liquidity, OB, FVG | Time (sessions, kill zones), daily models |
| Scope           | Small, simple                 | Larger, more terms and models             |
| In this chapter | Lessons 1–4                   | Lessons 5–7                               |

They are one family, so they are taught in sequence rather than apart. SMC is the base; ICT adds time and models on top.

The one question of this chapter: **Where is liquidity resting, who takes it, and where does price go afterwards?**

## Why this chapter

- The SMC/ICT lesson in the [Methodologies chapter](/en/learn/16-trading-methodologies/06-smc-ict) is a one-page introduction. Here each idea gets its own lesson, with charts.
- The basics of structure, liquidity and premium/discount are in the [Market Structure chapter](/en/learn/03-market-structure/00-overview). Here they are read by SMC/ICT rules, not repeated.
- SMC is highly subjective. Without the sequence, every gap becomes an entry.

## What you will be able to do

- Mark swings, BOS and CHoCH by a fixed rule.
- Recognise liquidity types and tell a sweep from a real breakout.
- Pick the relevant zone among many order blocks and FVGs.
- Draw a dealing range, premium/discount and the OTE zone.
- Read the day's pattern using sessions and kill zones.
- Write a complete entry model with stop and target.
- Test the limits of SMC in your own backtest.

## How the lessons connect

<Steps>

### Step 1: Bias

Read structure on a higher timeframe and decide the direction (lesson 1).

### Step 2: Liquidity

Mark where stops rest and check whether a sweep has happened (lesson 2).

### Step 3: Zone

If structure breaks after the sweep, look for an OB/FVG and check where it sits in premium/discount (lessons 3–4).

### Step 4: Time

Check whether the zone lines up with a session and kill zone (lessons 5–6).

### Step 5: Entry and risk

Write the rules, place the stop and target (lessons 7–8).

</Steps>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- ICT is the source; SMC is its simplified, community version.
- What works is the **sequence** and the **context**, not the terms: bias → liquidity → structure → zone → time → risk.
- SMC is highly subjective, with little independent evidence.
- Every lesson has a failed example; do not skip them.

</KeyTakeaways>
