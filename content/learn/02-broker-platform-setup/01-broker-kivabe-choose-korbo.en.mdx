# How to Choose a Broker: Checking Safety, Costs and Execution

A broker is the door between you and the market. When the door is good, you don't notice it. When it is bad, every trade is a hassle, and in the end you may not be able to withdraw your money at all. That is why you need to learn how to check a broker before you learn to trade.

## What you will be able to do

- **Compare** brokers against seven criteria.
- **Recognise** the warning signs.
- **Know** what to test before depositing real money.

## Why it matters

- **Your money is in their hands.** If a broker is incompetent or dishonest, even a good strategy is worthless.
- **Execution and costs change your results.** [Slippage and spread](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening) differ from broker to broker.
- **Fraud is real.** Forex attracts many fake brokers and "signal services".

Prerequisites: [Trading Styles, Platforms and Tools](/en/learn/01-foundations/07-types-of-trading-platforms-tools).

## What a broker is

**A Forex broker is the firm that shows you market prices and executes your orders.**

First, know the two broker models: market maker and STP/ECN ([Market Participants](/en/learn/01-foundations/02-market-participants)). Neither is bad in itself; the criteria are the seven below.

## Seven criteria

| #   | Criterion                | What to check                                                                                  |
| :-- | :----------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Regulation               | Which regulator, in which country? Match the licence number on the regulator's **own website** |
| 2   | Safety of funds          | Are client funds held in separate (segregated) accounts? Negative balance protection?          |
| 3   | Costs                    | Spread, commission, swap, deposit and withdrawal fees                                          |
| 4   | Execution                | Slippage and requotes during news, stability                                                   |
| 5   | Platform                 | MT4/MT5/cTrader/web; is it enough for your style?                                              |
| 6   | Deposits and withdrawals | Methods, time, fees; test a small withdrawal first                                             |
| 7   | Support and transparency | Contact, terms and conditions, complaint handling                                              |

**On regulation:** Brokers under a strict regulator usually offer more protection (such as leverage limits and keeping client funds separate). But the rules differ in every country, and the law where you live also matters. There is a [separate lesson on this](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/06-bangladesh-e-retail-forex-niyom-ar-tax).

## Warning signs

If you see any of these, walk away or check further:

- Claims of **"guaranteed profit"** or **"risk-free"**.
- Pressure to take **large bonuses** that make withdrawal conditions complicated.
- **No regulatory licence**, or one that does not match the regulator's site.
- **Delays or excuses** when you withdraw.
- Contact through **private messages**, an "account manager" suddenly asking you to deposit more.
- Offers from **strangers online** to "trade for you".

## Step by step: checking a broker

<Steps>

### Step 1: Make a shortlist

Two or three brokers. Use their own websites and the regulator's documents, not ads.

### Step 2: Check the regulation

Match the licence number in the regulator's official register.

### Step 3: Read the terms

Especially on funds, bonuses, withdrawals and negative balance.

### Step 4: Open a demo account

Test the costs and the platform. ([Demo vs Live](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/02-demo-vs-live-account))

### Step 5: Small deposit, small withdrawal

Start with a little real money. Trust them only once a withdrawal succeeds.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Situation:** A trader compared three brokers.
- **Action:** They matched the licences on the regulators' sites, read the terms, checked spreads and the platform on demo, and tested a small deposit and a small withdrawal.
- **Result:** One broker was ruled out (slow withdrawal); two remained.
- **Lesson:** Checking takes time, but it saves money.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Situation:** After seeing a "100% bonus" ad on social media, someone made a large deposit with an unknown broker.
- **Result:** Under the bonus terms, withdrawals were blocked unless they traded a huge volume. The licence had never been checked.
- **Why it failed:** The decision was driven by the ad, with no checks.
- **Lesson:** A bonus is often a trap, not a benefit. Safety first, costs second.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Why it happens     | What to do                         |
| :------------------------------ | :----------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Choosing on low spread alone    | Advertising        | Compare against the seven criteria |
| Not checking the licence number | Taking it on trust | The regulator's official register  |
| Being drawn in by bonuses       | Greed              | Read the terms; usually skip them  |
| A large deposit straight away   | Overconfidence     | Small deposit and small withdrawal |
| Believing reviews               | Fake reviews exist | Several independent sources        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  You can switch brokers, but it is dangerous if withdrawing is hard. So check
  first, and don't keep all your money with one broker.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When information is out of date:** Rules, fees and licences change; check the latest information each time.
- **When there are legal limits:** First find out whether using this service is allowed in your country.
- **When transfers are not secure:** Logging in or sending money through unknown links is always a risk.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Seven criteria: regulation, safety of funds, costs, execution, platform, withdrawals, support.
- Match the licence on the regulator's official site.
- Be wary of bonuses and "guaranteed profit".
- Test with a small deposit and a small withdrawal.

</KeyTakeaways>
