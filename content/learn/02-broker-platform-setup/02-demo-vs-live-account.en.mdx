# Demo vs Live Account: When, Why and How to Move from One to the Other

Pilots fly a flight simulator first; nobody claims the simulator is the same as the real sky. Demo is the same: safe practice, but not everything the real thing involves. This lesson explains what demo teaches, what it doesn't, and the safe path to live.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** the differences between demo and live.
- **Decide** how long and how to run a demo.
- **Write down** your conditions for going live.
- **Know** the rules for your first live week.

## Why it matters

- **Learning the rules and losing money are two different things.** On demo, the cost of learning carries no risk.
- **Good on demo, bad on live** is common. If you know why, you can prepare.
- **It is the place to test your [plan](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/00-overview).**

Prerequisites: [How to Choose a Broker](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/01-broker-kivabe-choose-korbo).

## Demo and live

**A demo account is an account with virtual money, where you practise at real market prices; in a live account, real money is at risk.**

| Item      | Demo                                       | Live                                      |
| :-------- | :----------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| Money     | Virtual                                    | Real                                      |
| Emotion   | Almost none                                | Fear, greed, restlessness                 |
| Execution | Often ideal; little or no slippage         | Real slippage, requotes, widening spreads |
| Balance   | Often unrealistically large (e.g. 100,000) | Your own capital                          |
| Liquidity | Assumed to be unlimited                    | There may be limits on large orders       |

## What demo teaches, and what it doesn't

**It teaches:**

- Running the platform and placing orders ([Order Types](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/04-order-types)).
- Trading by your rules and keeping a journal.
- Recognising setups, calculating lot size and stops.

**It doesn't teach:**

- How it feels to lose real money.
- Poor execution (slippage on news).
- Getting a grip on yourself after a big loss.

On demo, "a market order at 1.0932" feels easy, because it always fills at the ideal price. On live, in a fast market, the price you get and the price on your order can differ. That makes patience with limit orders even more important.

## Step by step: from demo to live

<Steps>

### Step 1: Make the demo like the real thing

Set the balance to your planned capital (for example, 1,000 USD). Practising on a huge balance is misleading.

### Step 2: Write a plan and follow it

Fill in the [plan template](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/01-trading-plan-template). Log every trade in a journal.

### Step 3: Enough time and enough trades

A few weeks, at least a few dozen trades (a suggested target, not a rule). See different market conditions.

### Step 4: Write your conditions for going live

For example: following the plan 80%+ of the time, never moving a stop, keeping to your risk % and daily limit. Write the conditions down in advance.

### Step 5: Start small on live

Risk half your normal amount or less. Keep your first 20 trades at small risk.

### Step 6: Note the differences

In your first live week, what felt different from demo (emotions, execution)?

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Demo:** 6 weeks, more than 50 trades, plan followed 85% of the time.
- **Going live:** Capital 1,000 USD, risk 0.25%.
- **Result:** Some emotion in the first month, but the risk was small and no rules were broken. Risk was raised slowly to 0.5%, then 1%.
- **Lesson:** Write your conditions for going live in advance and start with small risk.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **Demo:** 10 days, good results with large lots on a 100,000 USD balance.
- **Going live:** A 500 USD deposit, trying to keep the same lot ratio (which meant extremely large lots).
- **Result:** The first stop took a large part of the account. Out of fear, they moved the stop on the second trade and lost even more.
- **Why it failed:** The demo size was unrealistic, and there were no conditions for going live.
- **Lesson:** Keep the demo balance and risk like the real thing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Why it happens        | What to do                    |
| :------------------------------------------ | :-------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| An unrealistically large demo balance       | The default setting   | Set your own capital          |
| Going live after 1 week of demo             | Impatience            | A time target with conditions |
| Not following the rules on demo             | "It's not real money" | Behave as if it were real     |
| Full risk straight away on live             | Overconfidence        | Start small                   |
| Treating demo results as a promise for live | A taste of success    | Prove it again on live        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Before every demo trade, ask yourself: "If this were a real 100 USD, would I
  do the same?" If the answer is "no", taking this trade is a mistake.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  There is no deadline for going live. Treat the first month or two as learning
  only, not for profit; that lowers the pressure.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the demo's execution is too easy:** Reality hits on live. Be careful during news.
- **The lack of emotion on demo:** To learn about fear and greed, you need a small live account.
- **When you stay on demo indefinitely:** At some point you have to go live, or your learning stalls.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Demo = safe practice, but the emotions and execution are not real.
- Keep the balance and risk like the real thing.
- Write your conditions for going live in advance.
- Start live with small risk.

</KeyTakeaways>
