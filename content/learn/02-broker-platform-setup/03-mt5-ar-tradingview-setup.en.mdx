# MT5 and TradingView Setup: First Setup and Safety Settings

The platform is your desk. On a messy desk, work is slow and you press the wrong button. Spend a little time setting it up on day one and it pays off all year. This lesson gives general steps; menu names can differ slightly by version and app.

## What you will be able to do

- **Tell apart** the roles of MT5 and TradingView.
- **Set up** your first chart.
- **Place** alerts.
- **Check** the safety settings before an order.

## Why it matters

- **Wrong settings cause real losses.** The wrong lot, the wrong symbol, the wrong direction — each is one button away.
- **A good setup saves attention.** With alerts, you don't have to sit in front of the chart.
- **It is where you apply [Order Types](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/04-order-types) and risk calculations.**

Prerequisites: [Demo vs Live](/en/learn/02-broker-platform-setup/02-demo-vs-live-account).

## Two platforms

| Item     | MetaTrader 5 (MT5)                            | TradingView                                      |
| :------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| Main job | Trading directly with the broker, running EAs | Advanced charts, analysis, alerts, sharing ideas |
| Orders   | Directly with the broker                      | From the chart, if a supported broker is linked  |
| Strength | Execution, automation                         | Chart quality, drawing tools, easy alerts        |
| Limit    | Charts are relatively basic                   | Not every broker is supported                    |

Many people analyse on TradingView and place orders on MT5. Both are fine; choose what suits you.

## First setup: general steps

**MT5:**

1. Connect with the **demo login details** your broker gave you (server name, login, password).
2. Show your pair (for example, EURUSD) in **Market Watch**.
3. Open a chart and choose a timeframe (M15, H1, H4, D1).
4. Keep the Navigator and Terminal panels open (they show your orders and account).
5. Always double-check the **volume (lot)** in the order form.

**TradingView:**

1. Choose the symbol (for example, EURUSD) and timeframe.
2. Mark swings and levels with the drawing tools.
3. Place an **alert** (for when price touches or breaks a level).
4. If a broker is linked, open the trading panel, but use demo first.

## Step by step: a safe setup

<Steps>

### Step 1: Connect to demo

Not to a real account. Keep your login details safe and don't give them to anyone.

### Step 2: Keep the chart simple

Candlesticks, one timeframe, one or two indicators. ([Indicators](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/00-overview) come later.)

### Step 3: Place alerts

At important levels. You will need to sit at the screen less.

### Step 4: Check the order form settings

Is the symbol right? The direction? **The lot size?** Are the stop and target set? Keep one-click trading off until you are sure.

### Step 5: Place one small demo order

At the smallest lot size, then close it. Get to know the process.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **What they did:** The trader marked levels on TradingView and set alerts, and kept MT5 connected to demo.
- **During the session:** The alert went off; they opened the chart and went through their checklist, then placed the order on MT5 after double-checking the lot and stop.
- **Result:** The trade was taken at the planned 1% risk.
- **Lesson:** Alerts save attention, and a second look catches mistakes.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** Instead of 0.10 lot, the trader typed 1.00 in the volume box by mistake (ten times larger), with one-click trading switched on.
- **Result:** When the 30-pip stop was hit, the loss was 300 USD instead of the planned 30 USD (on a 3,000 account, 10% instead of 1%).
- **Why it failed:** A one-decimal-place mistake, and no second look.
- **Lesson:** Always read the lot box twice. Switch on one-click trading only once you are used to the platform.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Why it happens         | What to do                                   |
| :--------------------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| Not reading the lot box                  | Rushing                | Check it twice, every time                   |
| Filling the chart with indicators        | Hunting for signals    | A simple chart                               |
| Logging into live on an unknown computer | Convenience            | A safe device, two-factor security           |
| Not using alerts                         | Not knowing about them | Alerts at important levels                   |
| Leaving the default settings             | Saving time            | Check one-click, notifications and time zone |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Save a chart template (your colours, timeframe, minimum indicators). Every new
  chart then opens with the same simple look, which means fewer mistakes even
  when you are in a hurry.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Keep your platform password strong and two-factor authentication (2FA) on.
  Don't download fake "software" or "EAs"; your account details could be stolen.
  This is educational material.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **Menus differ by version:** The steps here are general; see the help pages for your version.
- **If your broker doesn't offer MT5:** You will use MT4, cTrader or its own platform; the ideas are much the same.
- **If TradingView doesn't support your broker:** Use it for analysis only, and place orders on MT5.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- MT5 = trading and execution; TradingView = charts, analysis, alerts.
- Connect to demo and set up a simple chart.
- Double-check the lot, stop and direction before an order.
- Security: password, 2FA, no unknown software.

</KeyTakeaways>
