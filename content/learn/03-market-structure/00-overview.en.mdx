# Market Structure: A Map for Reading Charts

If you walk through a strange city without a map, you will keep walking, but you will not know where you are going. Opening a chart without an indicator or a strategy is the same. **Market structure is the map of the chart.** By the end of this chapter you will be able to open any chart and say which way the market is going, where it is breaking, and where a trade carries less risk.

## What market structure is

**Market structure is the ongoing rhythm of price's highs and lows. From it you can read the market's direction and strength.** You do not need a separate indicator. It is built only from candle highs and lows.

The whole chapter is about one question: **"How are price's highs and lows lining up right now, and what is that telling me?"**

## Why learn this first

- **Entering without knowing the direction is close to gambling.** Structure tells you whether you are going with the trend or against it.
- **Stop-loss and target levels come from structure.** Almost everything in the later chapters (Support/Resistance, Patterns, Entries) is built on these turning points.
- **It keeps you out of mistakes.** You learn to tell when a trend has ended, when it is only a pullback, and when price has been set up as a trap.
- **It is the base of price action and SMC/ICT.** If you later move on to Smart Money or Wyckoff, the same words come back.

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- **Mark** swing highs and swing lows on a chart.
- **Tell apart** an uptrend, a downtrend and a range.
- **Spot** whether a trend is continuing or changing, using BOS and CHoCH.
- **Recognise** the traps of stop hunts and liquidity sweeps.
- **Combine** small and large timeframes to reach one clear decision.
- **Understand** whether price is in the discount or premium part of a range or swing.

## How these lessons work together

Say you are looking for a buy setup. The questions come in this order:

1. **Turning points (Lesson 1):** Which highs and lows actually matter?
2. **Direction (Lesson 2):** Are these turning points making higher highs and higher lows?
3. **Change (Lesson 3):** Did any turning point break? If so, is it a continuation or a reversal?
4. **Trap (Lesson 4):** Is the break real, or a move to take out stops?
5. **Big picture (Lesson 5):** Does the higher timeframe support my decision?
6. **Price (Lesson 6):** Am I entering in the discount part, or the premium part?

When all six answers line up, the trade has a reason behind it. If any one question is skipped, the risk goes up.

## What this chapter does not cover

- **Entry strategies or signals:** Market structure shows direction and location. It does not give an entry signal. Entries are in the [Price Action Entries](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/01-breakout-retest-pullback-entry) chapter.
- **Support and resistance zones:** These are in a separate chapter ([Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones)).
- **Risk management:** Lot size and stop-loss are covered in depth in the [Risk Management](/en/learn/08-risk-management/01-risk-per-trade) chapter. Each lesson here still ties in the main ideas.

## How to use this chapter

- **Do not change the order.** If you go to Lesson 4 without understanding Lesson 3, things get foggier.
- **Practise on your own chart after each lesson.** Understanding something on paper and recognising it by eye are not the same.
- **Start with one timeframe (H4 or D1).** Small timeframes have more noise, which confuses beginners.
- **Practise on a demo account.** Do not risk real money first.
