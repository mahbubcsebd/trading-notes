# Trend and Range: Read the Market's Direction with HH/HL

You take a buy trade, and price drops straight down. Later you zoom out and see that the market was in a full downtrend at the time. You never read the direction. So before any trade, the first question is just one: **which way is this market moving right now, or is it not moving in any direction?**

## What you will be able to do

- **Tell apart** an uptrend, a downtrend and a range.
- **Mark** HH, HL, LH and LL on a chart.
- **Separate** impulse (the wave of movement) from correction (the wave of rest).
- **Spot** the first sign that a trend is breaking.
- **Plan** trades in the direction of the trend.

## Why it matters

- Indicators tell you late. But looking at HH/HL tells you right now who is in control of the market.
- Trading in the direction of the trend raises the chance that price stays on your side. It is not a guarantee.
- Recognising a range keeps you from entering where price is going nowhere.

To follow this lesson, first read [Swing High and Swing Low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low). Here we line up those same turning points to read direction.

## What are trend and range?

**A trend is price moving steadily in one direction, with the turning points (swings) getting higher or lower in the same rhythm.** A **range** is a period when price moves up and down between a ceiling and a floor, without breaking either.

Think of stairs. Going up, each step is higher than the last. Going down, each step is lower. Walking along a flat veranda, your height does not change; you only move back and forth.

Remember four short names:

| Short  | Full name   | Meaning                                  |
| :----- | :---------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| **HH** | Higher High | A new high above the previous swing high |
| **HL** | Higher Low  | A new low above the previous swing low   |
| **LH** | Lower High  | A new high below the previous swing high |
| **LL** | Lower Low   | A new low below the previous swing low   |

According to Dow Theory (Charles Dow's theory of trends), a trend keeps going until there is a clear opposite signal. The whole logic of this lesson rests on that idea.

In an uptrend, price rises and makes a high, drops a little, but does not go below the previous low. Then it rises again past the previous high. This rhythm is what defines an uptrend.

In a range, highs and lows stay at about the same levels. No clear HH or LL forms. [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) forms near these boundaries.

## Impulse and correction: the two waves of a trend

A trend does not move in a straight line. It moves in two kinds of waves:

|               | Impulse (wave of movement)       | Correction (wave of rest)     |
| :------------ | :------------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **Direction** | With the trend                   | Against the trend             |
| **Look**      | Large-bodied candles, fast moves | Small bodies, slow and choppy |
| **Result**    | A new HH (in an uptrend)         | Forms an HL                   |

A simple but useful rule: when a trend is healthy, the correction is usually smaller and weaker than the impulse before it. If a correction goes beyond the impulse, you can suspect the trend is running out of steam. This is a warning sign, not a final verdict.

## Step by step: finding the direction on a chart

<Steps>

### Step 1: Start on the higher timeframe

Open an H4 or D1 chart. Small frames are full of noise. The reason is in the [Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis) lesson.

### Step 2: Mark the major swing highs and swing lows

Mark the swings with the 5-candle test. Do not count swings that are not confirmed.

### Step 3: Compare the last 3–4 swings side by side

Match each high with the previous high, and each low with the previous low.

### Step 4: Name it

If both highs and lows are higher, it is an uptrend. If both are lower, it is a downtrend. If the highs are higher on one side but the lows are lower on the other, or neither is clearly rising, it is a range or an area where the trend is changing.

### Step 5: Plan in the direction

In an uptrend, look for buys near an HL; in a downtrend, sells near an LH. In a range, be careful near the ceiling and floor, or stay out.

</Steps>

<Callout type="tip">
  Do not call a trend from two highs or two lows. Unless you find at least two
  highs and two lows in the same rhythm, it is safer to wait.
</Callout>

**For example (synthetic trade):** In the uptrend chart above, near candle 24, price did not break the previous low (candle 11). Instead it made a new low above it (HL).

- **What could be seen:** the HH → HL → HH sequence was intact.
- **Decision:** buy at about 1.0940 after price began to rise from the HL. Stop-loss a little below the HL, at about 1.0910 (about 30 pips of risk).
- **Target:** near the previous high of 1.0997 (about 57 pips), so a Risk:Reward of roughly 1.9:1.
- **Result:** on the chart, price got there and rose further. But this is synthetic data. In reality, such a result is not guaranteed.
- **Lesson:** when you enter in the direction of the trend, based on structure, with the stop set in advance, the trade's logic stays clear. See how to place a stop-loss in [Stop Loss Placement](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/01-stop-loss-placement), and how many lots to take in [Position Sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

## Failed example: mistaking a range for a trend (synthetic/educational)

- **What could be seen:** in the range chart, the high at candle 28 (about 0.8605) is slightly above the high at candle 15 (about 0.8602). Someone thought, "New HH, the uptrend has started!"
- **Decision:** they bought right near the ceiling.
- **Result:** price could not break the ceiling. It came back down toward the floor (about 0.8500). The stop was hit.
- **Lesson:** a gap of just a few points is not a clear HH. If the highs are "about equal", it is a range. Before you call an HH, check: did price close clearly beyond the previous high, and has it held above it since?

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Why it happens                            | What to do                                                                               |
| :-------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mistaking a range for a trend                 | Counting a small gap as HH/LL             | If the highs are about equal, treat it as a range                                        |
| Looking for a trend in small-timeframe noise  | On M5 everything looks like a trend       | Set the direction on H4/D1 first                                                         |
| Calling "trend over" as soon as one HL breaks | Rushing                                   | Then check what [BOS and CHoCH](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure) say |
| Jumping against the trend during a correction | Mistaking a small move for a trend change | Avoid trading against the direction of the larger frame                                  |
| Taking a trade on every candle in a trend     | Thinking a trend always means profit      | Wait for a good structural spot (such as an HL)                                          |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  In a range, "doing nothing" is also a decision. Resist the urge to trade every
  day. When there is no clear trend, staying out is often the best trade.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  A trend changes only when structure breaks, not before. In an uptrend, a close
  below the last HL is the first warning sign. More on this in the next lesson.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **During big news:** when price jumps, swings do not form properly and the rhythm breaks.
- **On very small timeframes (M1–M5):** everything is so small that HH/LL becomes meaningless noise.
- **At the moment a trend changes:** when highs and lows come in a mixed rhythm (for example HH but LL), there is no clear direction. Wait then.
- **Different views:** some people mark swings using only closing prices, others use the wick's high and low. Pick one rule and use it everywhere.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Uptrend = HH + HL; downtrend = LH + LL; range = a roughly equal ceiling and floor.
- Use major swings to set the trend, starting from the higher timeframe.
- Impulse is movement in the direction of the trend; correction is its rest. A small correction is a healthy sign.
- Mistaking a range for a trend is a big beginner mistake.
- Plan trades in the direction of the trend, from a good structural spot, with the stop set.

</KeyTakeaways>

3. If a correction becomes bigger than the impulse before it, what can that suggest?
4. Why is judging the trend on small timeframes risky?
