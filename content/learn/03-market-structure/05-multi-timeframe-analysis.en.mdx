# Multi-Timeframe Analysis: From the Big Timeframe Down to the Entry

You see a nice uptrend on the M15 chart and buy. Soon after, price drops hard. The reason: on the H4 chart, price was inside a big downtrend, and your entry was right below an H4 resistance. When you look at one timeframe, you see only one corner of the picture.

## What you will be able to do

- **Describe** the separate job of each of three timeframes.
- **Set** your **bias** from the higher timeframe.
- **Time** your entry by watching for a **CHOCH** on the lower timeframe.
- **Know** what to do when two timeframes say opposite things.

## Why it matters

- A trend on the lower timeframe is often just a pullback on the higher timeframe.
- Entering from a higher-timeframe level makes the stop smaller and the target bigger.
- You trade in the wrong direction less often, because the direction is set in advance.

## What is top-down?

**Multi-Timeframe Analysis (MTFA)** means looking at the same instrument on more than one timeframe. **Top-down** means starting from the big timeframe and stepping down to smaller ones.

**Analogy:** on Google Maps, first look at the whole city, then the area, then the street. If you look at the street first, you cannot tell where in the city you are.

## The job of each of the three levels

| Level      | Job                                         | Example (day trader) |
| :--------- | :------------------------------------------ | :------------------- |
| **Higher** | Trend and big levels, that is, bias         | D1, H4               |
| **Medium** | What price is doing near a level, structure | H1, M15              |
| **Lower**  | Trigger and a small stop                    | M5, M1               |

The common rule is to keep a gap of 4 to 6 times between the three timeframes. This is a habit among teachers, not a strict rule. Fix one set for your own style and stick to it.

## Step by step

<Steps>

### Step 1: Bias on the higher timeframe

Look at the [trend](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) on D1 or H4: HH/HL, LH/LL, or a range. If it is a range, treat it as no bias.

### Step 2: Mark the big levels

Mark the H4 swing highs/lows and [liquidity pools](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) near price.

### Step 3: Watch price's behaviour on the medium timeframe

What does price do when it comes near the level? Is structure changing? Wait.

### Step 4: Trigger on the lower timeframe

When a CHOCH or BOS appears in line with the bias, enter on the pullback. Wait until the candle closes.

### Step 5: Stop and size

Set the stop beyond the structure. Set the lot size with [position sizing](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing).

</Steps>

## When two timeframes say opposite things

| Situation                                      | What to do                                     |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| H4 bullish, H1 bearish                         | Wait for the H1 pullback to end, then buy      |
| H4 bullish, H1 bearish, and not at an H4 level | Do not trade                                   |
| H4 in a range                                  | Watch the edges of the range, avoid the middle |

The simplest rule: if you trade against the higher timeframe, raise the bar for proof, or skip it.

## Real example

  been added yet.

**Example (synthetic trade):** bullish bias on H4, price dropped to the demand level at 2340. On H1, a new LL formed at 2336, but then price closed above the last LH at 2356 (CHOCH). Price came down and made a higher low near 2347. You bought at 2353, with the stop below 2345 and a target of 2372. The risk is 8 points, the possible gain 19 points (about 2.4 times).

## Failed example: ignoring the big picture (synthetic/educational)

- **What could be seen:** a bullish CHOCH on M15, a nice setup.
- **Decision:** buy, but H4 was in a downtrend at the time, right below an H4 supply level.
- **Result:** price hit the supply, fell, and the stop was hit.
- **Lesson:** a lower-timeframe CHOCH inside a higher-timeframe trend is often just a pullback.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                          | Why it happens                        | What to do                  |
| :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------ | :-------------------------- |
| Looking only at M1/M5            | The pull of quick action              | Look at H4/D1 first         |
| Looking at too many timeframes   | Wanting to be certain                 | Fix three and stick to them |
| Changing timeframes every time   | Restlessness when results do not come | Keep your own set steady    |
| Ignoring higher-timeframe levels | Looking only at your own setup        | Mark the big levels first   |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  When you open a chart, first write down on the higher timeframe: "bias = ?,
  nearest big level = ?". Then move down to the lower timeframe. This habit cuts
  down on rushing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  When you change timeframe, the candle size changes, so the stop changes too.
  Set the stop each time from ATR or structure, not the same number of pips.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **During big news:** all timeframes jump at once.
- **In a range market:** the higher timeframe has no bias.
- **In scalping:** for an M1 trade, D1 analysis is often not relevant.
- **When used alone:** it helps you pick direction and timing; it does not guarantee profit.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- The higher timeframe gives bias, the medium shows structure, the lower gives the trigger.
- Step down from big to small, not the other way round.
- A lower-timeframe CHOCH against the higher timeframe is often just a pullback.
- Fix three timeframes and keep them steady.
- When there is no bias, not trading is also a decision.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice

Open the XAUUSD D1, H4 and H1 charts side by side. Write the trend on each (bullish/bearish/range). Then find the last 10 CHOCHs on H1. How many were in the direction of the H4 bias, and how did price move after them? Write down the results.
