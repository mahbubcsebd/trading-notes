# Premium and Discount: At What Price Should You Buy or Sell?

You saw an uptrend, read the direction correctly, and bought. But the entry was right near the swing high. A small pullback came and the stop was hit. The direction was right; the **price** was not.

## What you will be able to do

- **Find** the **equilibrium** (50%) of a swing range.
- **Recognise** the **premium** and **discount** parts.
- **Say** which part to look for buys or sells in, based on the trend's direction.
- **Know** the limits and mistakes of this rule.

## Why it matters

- In trades in the same direction, changing the entry price changes the ratio between stop and target (R:R).
- Buying in the discount makes the stop smaller and the target bigger.
- Buying in the premium often means "entering late".

## What are equilibrium, premium and discount?

Take a **range**: a swing low at the bottom, a swing high at the top. The rules for spotting swings are in the [first lesson](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low).

- **Equilibrium:** the price exactly in the middle of the range, that is, 50%.
- **Premium:** the part above 50%. Price is relatively "expensive".
- **Discount:** the part below 50%. Price is relatively "cheap".

**Analogy:** something normally costs 100 taka. If you get it for 70 taka, you are happy to buy; at 130 taka, you are not. Premium and discount are the same comparison, but inside a range.

The formula is simple:

```
equilibrium = (swing high + swing low) / 2
```

For example, if the low is 2300 and the high is 2340, equilibrium = (2340 + 2300) / 2 = 2320.

## What the rule is

| Trend     | Where to look             | What to avoid           |
| :-------- | :------------------------ | :---------------------- |
| Uptrend   | **Buy** in the discount   | Buying in the premium   |
| Downtrend | **Sell** in the premium   | Selling in the discount |
| Range     | The edges, not the middle | Near the equilibrium    |

The key point: [trend](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) first, price second. Being in the premium is not a reason to sell. In an uptrend, the premium usually means a strong market, so just wait.

## Step by step

<Steps>

### Step 1: Set the bias

Look at the trend on the higher timeframe ([Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis)). If it is bullish, look only for buys in the discount.

### Step 2: Pick the right range

Draw from the swing low to the swing high of the most recent impulse leg. If you set 0 and 100 on the chart's Fibonacci retracement tool, the 0.5 level shows the equilibrium.

### Step 3: Wait

Do not look for an entry until price comes below the 50% line.

### Step 4: Trigger in the discount

Once price is in the discount, look for a [CHOCH or BOS](/en/learn/03-market-structure/03-break-of-structure) on the lower timeframe. Touching 50% alone is not a reason to enter.

### Step 5: Stop and target

Place the stop below the pullback's low. Keep the target at the previous swing high or the [liquidity](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) above it.

</Steps>

**Example (synthetic trade):** H4 is bullish. On H1, the impulse runs from 2300 to 2340. Equilibrium is 2320. Price dropped, made a low at 2312, then turned. You bought at 2316, with the stop below 2309 and a target of 2340. The risk is 7 points, the possible gain 24 points (about 3.4 times).

Comparison: if you took the same trade in the premium at 2332, even with the stop at 2325, the target of 2340 is only 8 points away. Risk 7, gain 8. Same direction, but a much worse ratio.

## Failed example: discount does not mean buy (synthetic/educational)

- **What could be seen:** an uptrend, and price came below 50%.
- **Decision:** bought right away because it was the discount.
- **Result:** price fell further and broke the range's low.
- **Lesson:** a broken low means the uptrend's structure broke. That range is then no longer valid. The discount is not protection, just an area of better prices.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Why it happens                         | What to do                       |
| :-------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Selling in the premium without checking trend | Being misled by the word "expensive"   | Bias first, price second         |
| Drawing the range from the wrong swing        | Not knowing which swing is the big one | Use the most recent impulse leg  |
| Keeping the old line after the range breaks   | Habit                                  | Draw a new range after a new BOS |
| Entering as soon as price touches 50%         | Treating the line as a signal          | Wait for a trigger               |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Split the discount part further. The lower price gets between 50% and the
  range low, the better the price, but the higher the risk of structure
  breaking. Check this balance by writing down your own trades.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **When the range is small:** the 50% line ends up close to the spread.
- **During big news:** price ignores the line and runs.
- **In a strong trend:** price may not pull back deeply. Then waiting makes you miss the trade.
- **When used alone:** it is a filter for picking price, not a guarantee of profit.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Equilibrium = the average of the swing high and swing low, that is, 50%.
- Above 50% is the premium, below is the discount.
- Buy in the discount in an uptrend, sell in the premium in a downtrend.
- Trend first, then price, then the trigger.
- If an edge of the range breaks, draw a new range.

</KeyTakeaways>
