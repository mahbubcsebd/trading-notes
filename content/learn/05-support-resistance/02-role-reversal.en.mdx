# Role Reversal: How Broken Support Becomes Resistance

Price broke a support zone and fell. You thought, "This is the chance. When price comes back I will buy, it stopped here before." Price came back, and fell again right over your buy. The mistake was one idea: **after the break, that zone no longer has its old role.**

## What you will be able to do

- **Say** what role reversal is and **why it happens**.
- **Tell a real break from a false break.**
- **Plan the entry, stop and target** on a retest.
- **Recognise when a flip has failed** and keep the loss small.

## Why it matters

- **Half of all trades centre on broken levels.** After a breakout, the real chance is on the retest.
- **You avoid trading the wrong way.** Looking for a buy at broken support is the most common trap.
- **The reason for your stop becomes clear.** The place where the flip is proven wrong is your stop.

Prerequisites: drawing zones from the [Levels vs Zones](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) lesson.

## What role reversal is

**Role reversal is when broken support later acts as resistance, or broken resistance later acts as support.**

Why does it happen? Three reasons:

- **Trapped buyers:** Those who bought at support are at a loss when price goes lower. When price comes back, they sell, saying "let me get out at break even".
- **New sellers:** Those who did not sell when they saw the break get in at a better price when price comes back.
- **Gap left by old orders:** The earlier buy orders are used up or have moved, so there are fewer buyers above to hold price.

Think of a floor. You break through the floor and fall to the level below. Now when you try to climb up, that broken floor hits your head as a ceiling. **The area that once saved price is now blocking it.**

The whole event in four steps:

1. **Hold:** the zone stopped price three times.
2. **Break:** candle 10 **closed** below the zone.
3. **Retest:** price came back up to the zone from below (candle 13).
4. **Rejection:** it could not get through the zone and fell again.

Below, the two directions compared:

| Point         | Support → Resistance                     | Resistance → Support                     |
| :------------ | :--------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| Type of break | Close below the zone                     | Close above the zone                     |
| Retest        | Touch the zone from below                | Touch the zone from above                |
| Entry         | Sell when the retest candle's low breaks | Buy when the retest candle's high breaks |
| Stop          | Above the upper edge of the zone         | Below the lower edge of the zone         |
| Proven wrong  | Close back above the zone                | Close back below the zone                |

## Step by step: how to trade a flip

<Steps>

### Step 1: Draw the zone first

A zone where price has stopped at least two or three times. A flip of a one-touch zone is hard to trust.

### Step 2: Check the quality of the break

If a wick touches and comes back, that is not a break. Check whether the candle **closed** outside the zone, and whether the body is strong. On the chart above, candle 10 closed almost fully below the zone with a big body.

### Step 3: Do not rush, wait for the retest

If you enter on the break candle, you get in at the worst price, and the stop ends up far away. Wait until price comes back to the zone. Not every break gets a retest; if it does not come, let the trade go.

### Step 4: Look for rejection on the retest

A long wick into the zone, or a bearish engulfing, or a close on the other side of the zone. The same confirmation as in the [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context) lesson.

### Step 5: Enter on the trigger

For a sell, when the retest candle's low breaks (on the chart, candle 14, entry 1.0806). For a buy, when the retest candle's high breaks.

### Step 6: Put the stop outside the zone

For a sell, above the upper edge of the zone (1.0848). If price closes back above the zone, the idea of the flip is wrong.

### Step 7: Set the target

The next clear zone or the previous swing. If it is not at least twice the stop distance away, skip the trade.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

Look at the chart above.

- **What you could see:** The support zone 1.0820-1.0842 held three times. Candle 10 closed at 1.0796 and broke the zone.
- **Waiting:** On candles 11-12, price went lower and then rose again. Candle 13 went into the zone up to 1.0834 and closed at 1.0809, with a long upper wick.
- **Decision:** Candle 14 broke the low of candle 13 (1.0806), the sell trigger. Entry around 1.0806.
- **Stop:** above the zone at 1.0848, a risk of about 42 pips.
- **Target:** the previous swing at 1.0722, a gain of about 84 pips, which is 2 times the risk. More in the [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) lesson.
- **Result:** Price reached the target step by step.
- **Lesson:** Think about where your stop would have been and how bad your entry would have been if you had sold on the break. The retest gave you a small stop and a better price.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade, and no result is guaranteed.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Now the other direction: a broken resistance expected to become support, which did not happen.

<Chart
  id="role-reversal-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example): the resistance zone (1.0920-1.0942) breaks with a close above, bounces on the retest, then candle 16 closes back below the zone"
/>

- **What you could see:** Resistance held price down three times. Candle 10 closed at 1.0968 and broke it. Candle 13 came down into the zone, touched 1.0924 and closed bullish.
- **Decision:** Candle 14 broke the high of candle 13 (1.0966). Buy at 1.0966, stop below the zone at 1.0912, a risk of about 54 pips.
- **What happened:** Candle 15 went down. Candle 16 closed at 1.0902, below the zone. The stop was filled.
- **Why it failed:** The bounce had no strength; candle 14 had a small body. On the retest, buyers could not hold the zone, and the break above was really a trap.
- **Lesson:** Even when a flip looks reasonable, it is not certain. The loss was the planned 54 pips. A close back through the zone means the idea is wrong; it is not a place to move your stop and hope.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Why it happens                     | What to do                                          |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating a wick break as a real break          | Rushing to see a level break       | Look at the close and the strength of the body      |
| Getting in on the break candle itself          | Fear of missing out                | Wait for the retest; if it does not come, let it go |
| Entering on the retest without rejection       | "The zone is there anyway"         | You need a confirmation candle and a trigger        |
| Placing a limit order before the retest touch  | The habit of setting up in advance | See the reaction, then enter                        |
| Moving the stop away even after the flip fails | "It will turn a bit later"         | A close outside the zone means the trade is off     |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The stronger the break (big body, small wick), the better the chance of a flip
  on the retest. A weak break often comes back and moves around inside the zone.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  The older a zone is and the more times it has been tested, the more reliable
  its flip. But a flip also weakens if it is hit again and again on retests: the
  second retest is usually less reliable than the first.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Role reversal is a tendency, not a rule. Many zones break and price never
  comes back, and many others are retested and then broken straight through. Do
  not take a flip trade without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **After news:** In a sudden spike, price breaks through the zone and comes back, so you cannot tell the break from the retest.
- **Inside a range:** In a range, price hits the edges and turns back. See the [Trend and Range](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) lesson; there, not every break is real.
- **A very fast return right after the break:** If price returns to the other side of the zone within a few candles, that is a false break, not a flip.
- **On very small timeframes:** On M1-M5, zones and retests are unstable, and the reasoning is shaky.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Broken support usually becomes resistance, and broken resistance becomes support.
- Confirm a break with a close outside the zone, not a wick.
- Enter on rejection at the retest, not on the break.
- The stop goes outside the zone; a close back through the zone cancels the flip.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. Why does role reversal happen? Give two reasons.
2. Why should I look at the close and not the wick to confirm a break?
3. Why is it better to wait for the retest instead of entering on the break candle?
4. After the retest, if price closes back on the other side of the zone, what does that tell you?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** Trapped buyers sell to get out when price returns, and new sellers get in at a better price. Also, the earlier buy orders below are no longer there.

**2.** A wick is a brief touch; price can come back. The close shows where price settled at the end of the candle.

**3.** On the retest you get in at a better price, and the stop stays small near the zone. If you enter on the break candle, the entry is worse and the stop is far away.

**4.** The flip has failed. Get out at your stop; the idea is proven wrong.

</details>
