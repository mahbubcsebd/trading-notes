# Fibonacci Retracement: Where a Pullback Might Stop

Price rose strongly, then started to fall. You know the trend is still there, but where do you buy? On every candle it feels like "it will turn right here." Fibonacci retracement gives you a few places set in advance for this question. But those places are not predictions, only a list of places to watch.

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what Fibonacci retracement means in your own words.
- **Place** the tool by choosing the swing low and swing high correctly.
- **Read** what the 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6% levels mean.
- **Plan** an entry, stop and target by combining Fibonacci with other levels.

## Why it matters

- **Entries on pullbacks are better.** If you rush in and buy in the middle of a trend, your stop ends up far away. If you buy at the end of a pullback, the stop is small and the chance of profit is bigger.
- **You know where to wait.** Price is falling, and you know which area to watch.
- **Being wrong costs less.** The levels also show how deep a pullback has to be before the idea of the trend is off.

Prerequisites: recognising swing highs and lows from [Market Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl), and [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal).

## What Fibonacci retracement is

**Fibonacci retracement is a tool that splits the distance from the swing low to the swing high of an impulse leg into a few percentages. It shows the possible places where price might stop during a pullback.**

Think of a staircase. Price ran up and is out of breath. On the way down, on which step will it stand and catch its breath? Fibonacci draws those steps in advance. Price does not stop on every step; it skips some.

Main levels:

| Level | Usual meaning                                                                                   |
| :---- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 23.6% | Shallow pullback, the trend is very strong                                                      |
| 38.2% | Healthy pullback, a common place in a trend                                                     |
| 50%   | The middle of the impulse. It does not come from the Fibonacci numbers, but everyone watches it |
| 61.8% | Deep pullback, a favourite area for many                                                        |
| 78.6% | Last chance; after this the trend is weak                                                       |

The levels come from ratios in the Fibonacci number sequence (such as 61.8% and 38.2%). There is no proven explanation of why the market stops at these ratios. A possible reason the levels work is that **many traders watch the same levels**, so orders build up there too. It is like a self-fulfilling prophecy.

## Step by step: how to use it

<Steps>

### Step 1: Find the trend and the impulse leg

You need a clear leg where price moved strongly in one direction. Do not draw Fibonacci on a range or on messy price action.

### Step 2: Mark the swing low and swing high

In an uptrend, draw **from the swing low to the swing high**. In a downtrend, the other way, from the swing high to the swing low. Use the very end of the wick. Choose a swing that is clear to the eye, not a very small one.

### Step 3: Place the tool

Take the Fib Retracement tool on TradingView. Put 0 where the leg ends (the swing high) and 100 where the leg starts (the swing low). The levels appear on their own.

### Step 4: Look for confluence

A Fibonacci level alone is not enough. Is there anything else in the same area? An old swing high or low, a role reversal zone, a [demand zone](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/03-supply-demand), a trendline. The more independent reasons, the stronger the place.

### Step 5: Look at the character of the pullback

How is price falling? Small bodies and slow movement are good. If big bearish bodies come one after another, be careful; sellers are in control.

### Step 6: Wait for rejection

Do not buy as soon as price touches a level. Look for a rejection candle (a long lower wick or an engulfing), as in [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context).

### Step 7: Entry, stop and target

Enter when the high of the rejection candle breaks. Stop below the next level and the confluence zone. Target the previous swing high. If the reward is not at least twice the risk, skip it.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

Look at the chart above.

- **What you could see:** A leg of 160 pips (1.0800-1.0960). The levels: 38.2% = 1.0899, 50% = 1.0880, 61.8% = 1.0861, 78.6% = 1.0834. Price fell slowly over 5 candles and reached the 61.8% area.
- **Confluence:** The earlier highs (candles 0 and 3) were also in the 1.0856-1.0868 area, so old resistance was now acting as support.
- **Decision:** Candle 13 fell to 1.0852 and closed higher with a long lower wick. As soon as candle 14 broke its high (1.0872), buy, entry 1.0873.
- **Stop:** just below the 78.6% level at 1.0844, a risk of about 29 pips.
- **Target:** the previous swing high at 1.0960, a gain of about 87 pips, which is 3 times the risk. More in the [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) lesson.
- **Result:** Candle 17 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** Two independent reasons (Fibonacci and the old high) met, the pullback was slow, and a rejection candle came. Without any one of the three, the risk would have been higher.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

Even when the level is right, the trade does not hold if the character of the pullback is wrong.

<Chart
  id="fib-retracement-fail-01"
  height={440}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example): from the swing low at 1.0700 to the swing high at 1.0880. Five strong bearish candles on 8-12, a small bounce at the 61.8% (1.0769) area on candle 13, then price breaks the stop and the swing low and falls"
/>

- **What you could see:** A leg of 180 pips. 61.8% = 1.0769, 78.6% = 1.0739. Price came straight down in five big-bodied bearish candles, with no pause.
- **Decision:** Candle 13 touched 61.8% and went down to 1.0758, but the body was small and the bounce weak. As soon as candle 14 broke its high (1.0774), buy, entry 1.0775, stop below 78.6% at 1.0732, a risk of about 43 pips.
- **What happened:** Price fell again. Candle 16 hit the stop. Later it broke the swing low at 1.0700 and went down to 1.0682.
- **Why it failed:** The level was the only reason; there was no confluence. On top of that, sellers controlled the pullback: five strong bearish candles, and nobody stood against them.
- **Lesson:** The loss stopped at the planned 43 pips. Without a stop, price would have broken 78.6% and gone below the swing low, a big loss. A Fibonacci level is a place, not a reason.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Why it happens                                         | What to do                                           |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Entering as soon as a level is touched          | Fear of missing the price                              | Wait for a rejection candle and a trigger            |
| Choosing the wrong swing                        | The urge to take any high or low                       | Take a big swing that is clear to the eye            |
| Only Fibonacci, without confluence              | The tool is easy, so it feels enough                   | Find at least one more independent reason            |
| Drawing the tool in a range or sideways market  | Thinking there is a leg everywhere                     | Confirm the trend first                              |
| Seeing 5 levels at once and trading all of them | When the chart is full, everything looks like a chance | No more than 2-3 levels; delete the rest             |
| Ignoring the speed of the pullback              | Looking only at the level                              | If big bodies come one after another, skip the trade |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The most popular area is 50% to 61.8%. Many people treat this area as a zone,
  not a line. It matches directly with the 50% (equilibrium) idea in the
  [Premium and
  Discount](/en/learn/03-market-structure/06-premium-discount-zones) lesson.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Draw Fibonacci on a higher timeframe (H4, D1). On small timeframes the swings
  are small, the levels come close to each other, and you cannot tell whether a
  "match" is real or a coincidence.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  There is no proven explanation of why Fibonacci levels work. It is a tool
  based on assumptions, not a sure signal. Do not take a trade at any level
  without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **No trend:** In a sideways market the impulse leg is not clear, and the levels mean nothing.
- **Around big news:** Data can come out and push price past every level.
- **The pullback is very deep:** If 78.6% breaks, the idea of the impulse leg itself is weak.
- **The choice of swing is unclear:** If two people take two different swings, they get two different sets of levels. The choice itself is subjective.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Fibonacci retracement shows possible pullback places on the swing low and high of an impulse leg.
- 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% are watched the most. If price goes past 78.6%, the trend is weak.
- A level alone is not a reason. When it meets an old swing, role reversal or a zone, the place is strong.
- You need a slow pullback and a rejection candle. Price that falls with strong pushes does not respect levels.

</KeyTakeaways>
