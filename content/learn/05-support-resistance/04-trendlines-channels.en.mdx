# Trendlines and Channels: A Simple Guide to Reading the "Slope" of Price

Has it ever happened that you saw "the trendline has broken", sold, and price flew the opposite way? This is not a coincidence. Most new traders know how to draw the line, but they do not know **which line can be trusted and which break is a trap**. In this lesson you will learn that step by step.

## What you will be able to do

- **Draw** a valid trendline (drawn with 2 points, confirmed on the 3rd touch).
- **Recognise** steep, weak and over-touched lines.
- **Use** channels to set your entry, stop and target.
- **Tell apart** a real break and a fakeout.

## Why it matters

If you draw a trendline wrong, every signal is wrong. If you draw it right, the same line gives you all three: entry, stop-loss and target. Before learning this, make sure you understand [Market Structure](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) (recognising HH and HL) and [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones).

## What is a trendline?

**A trendline is a straight line that joins the swing points of a trend.**

Think of a ball rolling down a hillside. The ball does not stop on flat ground; it follows the slope. Price is the same: when buyers win, it rises step by step, and the line is the line of that slope.

Technically it is **dynamic support or resistance**: a horizontal level stays fixed, while a trendline moves up or down over time.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Trendline = dynamic support/resistance.** A horizontal level is fixed; a trendline moves up or down over time.
- **It can be drawn with 2 points and becomes valid on the 3rd touch.** A two-point line is only a "proposal".
- **More touches make a line weaker.** After the 4th-5th touch, the chance of a break goes up.
- **Steep lines do not last.** The steeper the slope, the easier the break.
- **A wick break and a close break are not the same.** Do not count a break until a candle closes outside the line.
- **A trendline alone is not enough.** Trade only when a supply/demand zone, divergence and a retest come together as confluence.

</KeyTakeaways>

## 1. Why do trendlines work?

Saying "they work because everyone draws them" is incomplete. There are three real reasons:

1. **The shape of a trend:** In an uptrend, buyers are willing to buy a little higher each time than before (Higher Low). When those lows fall on a straight line, it shows that buyers' interest is steady.
2. **Order clusters:** Many traders see the same line and place buy orders and stops in the same place. So liquidity builds up near the line.
3. **Self-fulfilling effect:** The more people watch the line, the more it "works". That is why lines on higher timeframes (H4, D1) are more reliable: more people watch them.

<Callout type="tip">
  A trendline is not a prediction, it is a **possible reaction area**. When
  price comes near the line, you watch what happens and then decide.
</Callout>

## 2. Three types of trendline

| Type               | Where to draw                           | Role                          |
| :----------------- | :-------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **Uptrend line**   | **Below** the swing lows (Higher Lows)  | Dynamic **support**           |
| **Downtrend line** | **Above** the swing highs (Lower Highs) | Dynamic **resistance**        |
| **Channel**        | Trendline + a parallel copy of it       | Trading between the two edges |

A rule to remember: **in an uptrend, join the lows; in a downtrend, join the highs.** If you draw a line through points on the opposite side, it is not a trendline but the far edge of a channel.

## 3. Rules for drawing correctly

If you read different guides (Investing.com, TrendSpider, Capital.com), everyone agrees on some rules and disagrees on others. You need to know both.

### What everyone agrees on

<Steps>

### Step 1: Start with a higher timeframe

First look at the big trend on a D1 or H4 chart. Without the big picture, a line on M15 is often noise.

### Step 2: Choose two clear swing points

Not small noisy lows; swing points from which price really turned (see the earlier Market Structure lesson).

### Step 3: Extend the line forward

Join the two points and extend to the right. This is now a **tentative line**.

### Step 4: Wait for the 3rd touch

If price comes back and reacts to the line a third time, the line is **valid**. Before that, do not take big risk on it.

### Step 5: The line should not cut through candle bodies

A good line touches the swing points and does not cut the candles in between. If it keeps cutting them, the line is in the wrong place.

</Steps>

### Where they disagree: wick or body?

Some join the tips of the wicks, others the close or the edge of the body. There is no "one right" rule. The advice of most guides: **choose one method and use it everywhere.** With wicks the line fits well; with closes you get caught by fewer fakeouts.

### When to cancel a line, and when not to?

- If a candle **closes with its body** through the line and does not come back: the line is cancelled.
- If only a wick goes through and comes back: the line usually stays (this is a fakeout, see below).
- Do not keep moving the line to fit price. This is called **curve-fitting**. If the line does not "fit", maybe it is not a trend at all.

### Be careful with slope

<Callout type="warning">
  A very steep trendline (well above 45°) does not last. When price rises this
  fast, even a small pullback breaks the line. When a steep line breaks, do not
  think it is a trend reversal; often it is only slowing down. Then draw a new
  line with a moderate slope.
</Callout>

Another subtle point: **linear vs log scale.** On a linear scale, equal price distances look equal; on a log scale, equal **percentages** look equal. On a long chart of several years (especially crypto and stocks), a line drawn on log scale and a line on linear scale will look different. If you change the scale, check the line again. On small timeframes (H1, M15) the difference is tiny.

## 4. See it on a chart: 3 touches and a bounce

On the chart below, look at the marks 1, 2, 3 on the lows. The first two draw the line, the third proves it, and the fourth touch is the bounce entry.

  caption="EURUSD H1 (educational example): a trendline valid after 3 touches, bounce entry on the 4th touch"
/>

**Rules for a bounce trade:**

- **Entry:** after a rejection candle (pin bar, bullish engulfing) closes at the line.
- **Stop loss:** a little below the line, leaving room for the spread and wicks.
- **Target:** the previous swing high or the next horizontal resistance.
- **Filter:** if the trend on the larger timeframe is in the same direction, the chance is better.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The trap of too many touches:** By the 4th, 5th, 6th touch, stops and
  pending orders build up below the line. Big players may break the line to take
  that liquidity. So in bounce trades, raise your caution as the number of
  touches grows.
</Callout>

## 5. Channels: the twin of the trendline

After drawing a trendline, place a **parallel copy** of it on the swings on the opposite side. If price moves between the two lines, that is a channel.

<Chart
  id="ascending-channel-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H1 (educational example): ascending channel, buy at the lower edge, take profit at the upper edge"
/>

| Channel                | Trading approach                                                                    |
| :--------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ascending**          | Buy at the lower edge (with the trend), take profit or close part at the upper edge |
| **Descending**         | Sell at the upper edge, take profit at the lower edge                               |
| **Horizontal (range)** | Buy low, sell high; trade both ways                                                 |

**Advantage of a channel:** you can see both the entry and the target in advance, and the risk-reward is clear. **Limitation:** when the channel breaks (a body close outside the edge), you have to change your approach. A break of the upper edge can point to an uptrend speeding up; a break of the lower edge is a sign the trend is ending.

<Callout type="tip">
  The best trade in a channel is at the edge **in the direction of the trend**.
  In an uptrend, buy at the lower edge; a counter-trend sell at the upper edge
  is riskier.
</Callout>

## 6. Breakouts: how to recognise a real break?

Every trendline breaks at some point. The question is: **is this a real break, or a trap (fakeout)?**

| Feature        | Real break                         | Fakeout                                   |
| :------------- | :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| **Close**      | Body close outside the line        | Wick goes outside but the close is inside |
| **Candle**     | Big body, clear momentum           | Small body or long wick                   |
| **Retest**     | The broken line acts as a barrier  | Price goes back inside the line           |
| **Confluence** | Higher-timeframe level, divergence | No support                                |

Some educational sources guess that a large share of breaks in the market are really fakeouts. That number did not come from a reliable backtest, so do not rely on an exact percentage; follow the rules: **wait for the close, wait for the retest.**

### Failed example: when even a valid line is a trap (synthetic/educational example)

<Chart
  id="trendline-fakeout-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H1 (educational example): the wick goes below the line but the close is above, a fakeout; those who sold are trapped"
/>

**What you could see:** a line valid after three touches, then a candle dropped below the line.

**Decision:** many people sold as soon as they saw the wick.

**Result:** the candle closed above, price rose again, and the sell was a loss.

**Lesson:** even if the line is valid, the break is not certain. No break trade without a body close and a retest.

On candle 19, sellers rushed in when they saw the wick break. The candle closed above the line, and the next candles rose again. Following the "wait for the close" rule would have avoided this loss.

### Break-and-retest entry

The method has three steps:

1. Price makes a **body close** outside the line.
2. Price comes back and **tests** the broken line (old support is now resistance. See [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal)).
3. If the line holds (rejection wick), enter in the direction of the trade, with the stop behind the extreme of the retest.

The retest does not always come. If it does not, accept missing the trade; do not chase an entry (FOMO).

## 7. Confluence: do not rely on a trendline alone

Almost all retail traders draw the same line, and big players know about that crowd. So if you trade only on a line break, you risk falling into traps again and again. In a strong setup, at least three things meet:

1. **Horizontal zone:** a higher-timeframe supply or demand zone at the place of the break ([Supply & Demand](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/03-supply-demand)).
2. **Momentum divergence:** price makes a new high but RSI/MACD does not, which means momentum is fading.
3. **Close + retest:** as described in the previous section.

## 8. Real trade case study: winning trades (from the video)

The two examples below are real crypto charts taken from a Generous Traders YouTube video. The entry, stop and target given here are the levels the video's presenter showed. They are educational explanations, not trade signals, and the results do not guarantee the future.

<YouTube id="UfkoV3dEh7I" start={871} />

### Case 1: BEL/USDT (1H) — short on a trendline breakdown

- **Context:** making higher highs and lows in a rising channel, price reached an old supply zone in the 0.8200-0.8500 area.
- **Warning sign:** price made a new high, but RSI showed **bearish divergence**, and the MACD histogram was shrinking.
- **Trigger:** a strong red candle closed below the trendline. Price pulled back and retested inside the supply zone.
- **Trade:** short 0.8099, stop 0.8489 (above the zone), target 0.6897 (the previous major support).
- **Risk-reward calculation:** risk = 0.8489 − 0.8099 = 0.0390; possible gain = 0.8099 − 0.6897 = 0.1202; ratio ≈ 1 : 3.1.

### Case 2: BLZ/USDT (1H) — long on a downtrend line break and CHoCH

- **Context:** lower highs and lows in a downtrend, a descending channel/falling wedge.
- **Warning sign:** price made a lower low, but RSI and MACD showed **bullish divergence**.
- **Trigger:** price broke the downtrend line, went past the previous swing high and confirmed a **CHoCH**.
- **Trade:** long 0.05047 (CHoCH retest), stop 0.04844 (below the swing low), target 0.05663.
- **Risk-reward calculation:** risk = 0.00203; possible gain = 0.00616; ratio ≈ 1 : 3.0.

The pattern is the same in both cases: **a line break alone is not the trigger; zone + divergence + retest came together.**

## 9. Bounce vs breakout: which one when?

|                         | Trendline bounce                              | Trendline breakout                                   |
| :---------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| **Market condition**    | Trend continuing (continuation)               | Trend ending or changing (reversal)                  |
| **When to look for it** | On the 2nd-3rd touch, momentum with the trend | When there is a higher-timeframe zone and divergence |
| **Confirmation**        | Pin bar, rejection wick, engulfing            | Body close + structure break (CHoCH)                 |
| **Entry**               | On the close of the rejection candle          | On the retest after the break                        |
| **Stop loss**           | A little outside the line                     | Behind the extreme of the retest                     |
| **Risk**                | Quick loss if the line breaks                 | Getting trapped in a fakeout                         |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Why it happens                      | What to do                            |
| :------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| A made-up line from 1-2 points         | Rushing                             | Wait for the 3rd touch                |
| Moving the line again and again        | Wanting price to fit the line       | Choose one method and stick to it     |
| Relying on a steep line                | Drawing on a zoomed-in chart        | Check the slope on a higher timeframe |
| Entering as soon as a wick breaks      | Not recognising fakeouts            | Wait for a body close                 |
| Bouncing off a line touched 5-6 times  | Thinking "it has worked many times" | Raise your caution as touches grow    |
| Trading only on a line, without a zone | Not knowing about confluence        | Add supply/demand and divergence      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Draw lines on higher timeframes (D1, H4) and look for entries on lower
  timeframes (H1, M15). More traders watch lines on big timeframes, so the
  reaction is bigger too.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  In a channel, the best trade is with the trend. In an uptrend, buy at the
  lower edge; a counter-trend sell at the upper edge is riskier.
</Callout>

## When trendlines do not work

- In a range or sideways market (no clear trend).
- During big news, when price jumps and ignores the line.
- On very small timeframes (M1-M5), where there is a lot of noise.
- When the line is very steep or has been touched more than 5-6 times.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A trendline is dynamic support/resistance: join the lows in an uptrend, the highs in a downtrend.
- Draw with two points, trust with three.
- Steep and over-touched lines are weak.
- Do not count a break without a body close + retest.
- A line alone is not enough: combine it with zones and divergence.

</KeyTakeaways>
