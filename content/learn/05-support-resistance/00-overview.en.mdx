# Support & Resistance: A Map of Where Price Stops

If you walk through a strange city without a map, every street looks the same. A chart is the same until you know where price has stopped again and again. **Support and resistance are that map.** By the end of this chapter, you will be able to open any chart and say in five minutes where price might stop.

## What support and resistance are

**Support is an area below where buyers have held price up again and again, and resistance is an area above where sellers have held it down.**

The whole chapter looks for the answer to this question: **"Where might price react next time, and what will I do there?"**

## Why learn this now

- **A candle pattern gets its meaning from its location.** In the [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context) lesson you saw that the same pattern says different things in different places. That "place" is in this chapter.
- **You get places to set your stop and target.** When you know the levels, where you put your stop and where you keep your target comes from reasoning, not guessing.
- **It tests the quality of an entry.** Even a good setup becomes a bad one if there is an obstacle right next to it.
- **The later chapters build on this.** Chart patterns, indicators and entry strategies all centre on some level.

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- **Draw** support and resistance as zones, not as one thin line.
- **Recognise** how a broken level takes the opposite role (role reversal).
- **Mark** supply and demand zones.
- **Draw** trendlines and channels **and check whether they are valid**.
- **Use** Fibonacci retracement **together with other levels**.

## How these lessons work together

Say you open a chart and look for a new setup. The questions come in this order:

1. **Level (Lesson 1):** Which are the nearest zones above and below?
2. **History (Lesson 2):** Has this zone broken before and taken the opposite role?
3. **Origin (Lesson 3):** Does the zone carry the mark of big orders?
4. **Slope (Lesson 4):** Does a sloping wall meet the same area?
5. **Confluence (Lesson 5):** Does Fibonacci or something else support the same area?

The more independent reasons meet in the same area, the more important that place is.

## How to use this chapter

- **Follow the lesson order.** Each lesson builds on the one before.
- **Practise on H4 or D1 charts.** Levels are clearer on large timeframes.
- **After each lesson, draw levels on your own chart.** You learn faster by drawing than by reading.
- **Practise on a demo account.**
