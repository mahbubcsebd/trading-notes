# Chart Patterns: How to Read the Shape of Price

Many people guess rain from the look of the clouds. It is not certain, but shapes you see again and again give you an idea of the chances. **A chart pattern is a shape of price that comes back again and again.** By the end of this chapter, you will be able to look at a shape on the chart and say where price might turn, where it might keep going, and where your idea is proven wrong.

## What a chart pattern is

**A chart pattern is a shape made by several swings together, which shows which way the fight between buyers and sellers is leaning.**

The whole chapter looks for the answer to this question: **"What is this shape saying, and at what price will my idea be proven wrong?"**

A pattern is not a prediction. It is a framework for a plan: where you get in, where your stop goes, where your target is.

## Why learn this now

- **You now have the building blocks.** If you know [Swing Highs and Swing Lows](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low), [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) and [Trendlines](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/04-trendlines-channels), patterns sit on top of them.
- **The reason for the stop is built into a pattern.** Every pattern has a "proven wrong" place, and that is your stop.
- **You get a rule for measuring the target.** A reasonable target comes from the height of the pattern.
- **You can recognise breakout traps.** Many patterns break the wrong way at the last moment. The last lesson is about exactly that.

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- **Recognise and draw** a double top, a double bottom and a head and shoulders.
- **Tell apart** triangles, wedges, flags and pennants **as continuation patterns**.
- **Check** whether a range break is **a real breakout**.
- **Recognise** a fake breakout **and keep the loss small**.
- **Set** the **entry, stop and target** for each pattern.

## How these lessons work together

You open a chart and see a shape. The questions come in this order:

1. **What was the trend before?** If there was a trend, this decides whether the pattern is a reversal or a continuation.
2. **Which shape is it (Lessons 1-2)?** If it is a reversal, Lesson 1; if it is a pause, Lesson 2.
3. **If it is sideways (Lesson 3):** Where are the edges of the range, and what are the conditions for a break?
4. **It broke, but is it real (Lesson 4)?** Look at the close, the retest and signs of a return.
5. **Plan:** write down the entry, stop and target, measure the risk, and only then trade.

## How to use this chapter

- **Follow the lesson order.** The last lesson builds on the three before it.
- **Practise on H4 or D1 charts.** Patterns are clear on large timeframes and unstable on small ones.
- **Find patterns on your own chart.** After each lesson, mark at least three examples, both successful and failed.
- **Practise on a demo account.**
