# Reversal Patterns: Double Top, Double Bottom and Head and Shoulders

An uptrend is running. Price goes back near that old high, stops, and turns back. You think, "This time it will break." It does not. It hits the same place a second time and price falls. This scene comes back so often in the market that it has a name. **This is a reversal pattern: the shape of a trend coming to an end.**

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognise** a double top, a double bottom and a head and shoulders.
- **Draw** the **neckline** and check its break.
- **Set** the entry, stop and target **from the size of the pattern**.
- **Tell** when a pattern **has failed** and keep the loss small.

## Why it matters

- **You get an early hint of a trend change.** In the [Trend and Range](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl) lesson you saw that a trend moves in a sequence of highs and lows. A reversal pattern is the shape of that sequence breaking.
- **The place where you are proven wrong is clear.** When the pattern is cancelled, the stop is set too.
- **The target can be measured.** The target comes from the height of the pattern.

Prerequisites: [Swing High and Swing Low](/en/learn/03-market-structure/01-swing-high-low) and [Levels vs Zones](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones).

## What a reversal pattern is

**A reversal pattern is a shape that shows a running trend has run out of strength and a chance of turning the other way has formed.**

Think of a runner. The first time, they hit a wall and come back. The second time they try again, with less force than before. Whether they will try a third time is doubtful. **The repeated failure to get past the wall is the pattern.**

There is one condition: a reversal pattern only means something when there was **a clear trend before it**. In a sideways market you may see this shape, but it is usually just part of the range.

## The three patterns at a glance

| Pattern            | Shape                                          | Neckline                          | Previous trend |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :------------- |
| Double Top         | Two peaks at about the same height             | The low between the two peaks     | Uptrend        |
| Double Bottom      | Two troughs at about the same depth            | The high between the two troughs  | Downtrend      |
| Head and Shoulders | Three peaks, the middle one highest (the head) | The two lows beside the shoulders | Uptrend        |

The inverse head and shoulders is the opposite form: three troughs, the middle one deepest, and it comes at the end of a downtrend. The **neckline** is the line (or zone) whose break confirms the pattern.

## What it looks like on a chart: Double Top

/>

It forms in four steps:

1. **Peak 1 (candle 8):** the uptrend touched its last high at 1.0980 and turned back.
2. **Neckline low (candle 13):** the pullback fell to 1.0880.
3. **Peak 2 (candle 18):** price rose again, but stopped below the first peak.
4. **Break (candle 21):** the candle **closed** below the neckline. This is where the pattern is confirmed.

**The two peaks do not have to be exactly equal.** Close is enough; think of them as two touches of a resistance zone.

## What it looks like on a chart: Head and Shoulders

<Chart
  id="head-shoulders-01"
  height={440}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example): left shoulder (5), head (12), right shoulder (19); neckline about 1.0760; candle 22 closes below"
/>

- **Left shoulder:** a normal high of the trend.
- **Head:** a new, higher high. The uptrend still seems to be in place.
- **Right shoulder:** another attempt to rise, but it ends below the head. This is the first time buyers could not make a new high.
- **Neckline:** a line joining the two lows. When it breaks, the pattern is complete.

A head and shoulders takes more steps than a double top, so it is usually seen less often. But the way you trade the two patterns is the same.

## Step by step: trading a neckline break

<Steps>

### Step 1: Confirm the trend first

Before the pattern there must be a clear uptrend (for a sell pattern) or downtrend (for a buy pattern). If there is not, do not look for the pattern.

### Step 2: Mark the peaks and the neckline

Draw the peaks (or troughs) and the neckline. Think of the neckline as a slightly thick zone, not a thin line.

### Step 3: Wait for a close beyond the neckline

If a wick touches through, it is not a break. Check whether the candle **closed** beyond the neckline, and how strong the body is. On the chart above, candle 21 closed about 14 pips below the neckline.

### Step 4: Look for a retest

After the break, price often comes back to the neckline (candle 23). This is [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal): broken support is now resistance. Not every pattern gets a retest; if it does not come, the rule is to let the trade go.

### Step 5: Enter on the trigger

Sell when the retest candle's low breaks (candle 24, entry 1.0868). For a buy pattern, when the retest candle's high breaks.

### Step 6: Put the stop where you are proven wrong

Above the high of the retest (1.0898). If price closes back above the neckline, the pattern is cancelled. A safer but bigger stop is above the second peak (about 1.0984): the risk grows, so you need to make the position smaller.

### Step 7: Measure the target

**Height of the pattern** = the distance from the highest peak to the neckline. The target is that far from the neckline: 1.0880 - 100 pips = 1.0780. In a head and shoulders, measure the height from the head to the neckline.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The double top chart above:

- **What you could see:** a clear uptrend, two peaks (1.0980 and 1.0976), neckline 1.0880.
- **Break:** candle 21 closed at 1.0866, with a big body.
- **Retest:** candle 23 rose to 1.0892 and closed at 1.0876; it could not take back the neckline.
- **Trigger:** candle 24 broke the low of candle 23 (1.0868). Sell at 1.0868.
- **Stop:** above the retest at 1.0898, a risk of 30 pips.
- **Target:** 1.0780, a distance of 88 pips, which is about 2.9 times the risk. More in the [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) lesson.
- **Result:** Price fell step by step and reached the target.
- **Lesson:** If you had sold as soon as you saw the second peak, the stop would have been far away, and you would not have known whether price would break the neckline.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

The same shape, but the break is weak.

<Chart
  id="double-top-fail-01"
  height={440}
  caption="EURUSD H4 (educational, synthetic example): the same double top, candle 21 closes only 6 pips below the neckline; candle 23 goes past the stop and takes back the neckline"
/>

- **What you could see:** a double top, neckline 1.0880. Candle 21 closed at 1.0874, but the body was small.
- **Decision:** Candle 22 was small; its low (1.0864) broke, the sell trigger. Stop 1.0898, a risk of about 34 pips.
- **What happened:** Candle 23 was strongly bullish and closed at 1.0912. The stop was filled. After that, price went above both peaks too.
- **Why it failed:** There were three weaknesses: a small-bodied break, a trigger with no momentum, and getting in without a retest. Sellers had no strength.
- **Lesson:** Even if the pattern looks nice, the signal cannot be trusted when the quality of the break is poor. The loss was the planned 34 pips. More on this kind of break that quickly comes back in the next lesson, [Fake Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/04-fake-breakout).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                             | Why it happens                               | What to do                                        |
| :-------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| Entering as soon as you see the second peak         | Greed for a better price by getting in early | Wait for the close of the neckline break          |
| Looking for patterns with no trend                  | Seeing shapes everywhere                     | Check the trend first                             |
| Treating a wick break as a real break               | Rushing                                      | Look at the close and the strength of the body    |
| Getting in without measuring the target             | "Let it go as far as it goes"                | Measure the height and check R:R; skip if too low |
| Moving the stop even after the pattern is cancelled | "It will turn a bit later"                   | A close back beyond the neckline means cancelled  |
| Trusting unstable shapes on small timeframes        | A pattern every hour on M5                   | Avoid anything below H1                           |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  In a head and shoulders, the signal is better if the right shoulder is lower
  than or equal to the left shoulder. If the right shoulder rises close to the
  head, the pattern is weak.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  A neckline can slope. But the steeper the slope, the less clear the shape and
  the less certain the measurement of the break. A flat neckline is the
  clearest.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  No pattern gives certainty. Many double tops in the market end up going past
  the peak and continuing the uptrend. Do not enter a reversal pattern without a
  stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **Inside a range:** In a sideways market these shapes form again and again, but without a trend before them they are not reversals.
- **On very small timeframes:** On M1-M5 the shapes are often noise.
- **Around news:** A sudden spike breaks through the neckline and comes back, so you cannot read the break.
- **Against a big higher-timeframe trend:** Even if there is a double top on H1, selling is risky if the D1 uptrend is strong. See the [Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis) lesson.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- There must be a clear trend before a reversal pattern.
- The pattern is confirmed only when price closes beyond the neckline.
- Entry on the retest or trigger, stop where the pattern is cancelled, target measured from the height of the pattern.
- A weak break and a quick return are the most common signs of failure.

</KeyTakeaways>

3. Name three signs of a weak neckline break.
4. After a neckline break, if price closes back above the neckline, what does that tell you?

<summary>Show answers</summary>

**2.** Measure the height from the peak (or head) to the neckline, then place the target that distance from the neckline in the direction of the break.

**3.** A small-bodied break, a lack of momentum after the trigger, and a quick return that takes back the neckline.

**4.** The pattern is cancelled. Get out at your stop.
