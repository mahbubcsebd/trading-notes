# Range Breakout: 3 Conditions for Recognising a Real Breakout

Price has been moving in the same box for two weeks. Suddenly one candle shoots up. You get in. The next candle comes back and takes out your stop. **Not every breakout is real. But there are clear conditions for recognising the real ones.**

## What you will be able to do

- **Draw** a **range** and measure its height.
- **Check** whether a breakout is real **with three conditions**.
- **Set the entry, stop and target** on a breakout and on a retest.
- **Recognise and avoid** weak breakouts.

## Why it matters

- **A big move can come after a range breaks.** Built-up energy finds a way out, and this is where [Support and Resistance](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/01-levels-vs-zones) break.
- **False breakouts take out the most stops.** If you know the conditions, you can avoid half the traps.
- **The target is clear.** The height of the range is the measure of the target.

Prerequisites: [Continuation Patterns](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/02-continuation-patterns) and [Trend and Range](/en/learn/03-market-structure/02-trend-range-hhhl).

## What a range and a breakout are

**A range is a period when price moves between a set upper and lower edge. A breakout is price moving out of those edges in a confirmed way.**

Think of a pet bird in a cage. It keeps pecking at the top bars. If one day it breaks a bar and gets out, it flies far away, but after a weak peck it comes back. **The question is always the same: is the push really strong?**

The word "confirmed" matters. If only a wick touches, or there is a spike for a moment, it is not a breakout.

## The 3 conditions of a real breakout

| #   | Condition             | What to look for                                          | Weak sign                               |
| :-- | :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Close**             | The candle **closed** outside the range                   | Only the wick is outside                |
| 2   | **Body and momentum** | The body is much bigger than the candles inside the range | Small body, long opposite wick          |
| 3   | **Follow-through**    | The next candle or the retest holds the broken level      | Price quickly comes back into the range |

If at least two of the three are not met, wait. For the third, follow-through, a retest is the best proof: broken resistance is now support, and this is [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal).

## What it looks like on a chart: a real breakout

/>

- **Range (0 to 17):** price touched the top at 1.0900 four times and turned back, and touched the bottom at 1.0800 three times. Repeated reactions at both edges mean the range is valid.
- **Breakout (18):** the candle closed at 1.0935 with a body of about 59 pips. Candles inside the range averaged about 25 pips, so this is a big difference.
- **Retest (20):** price came down and touched 1.0899, but did not close below. The old top is now support.
- **Trigger (21):** price broke the high of candle 20 and moved on.

## Step by step: trading a range breakout

<Steps>

### Step 1: Check the range

You need at least two reactions at each edge, and the height of the range has to be big enough. A very narrow range has a lot of noise. In the example, the two edges are 1.0900 and 1.0800.

### Step 2: Measure the height

The distance from the top to the bottom. In the example, 100 pips. This is the measure of your target.

### Step 3: Check the three conditions

Look at the close, the body and the follow-through. Even if the close and body are right, it is safer to wait for the retest.

### Step 4: Choose the entry

- **When the high of the breakout candle breaks:** fast, but the stop is far away.
- **When price holds on the retest and the high of the retest candle breaks:** slower, but the stop is close (in the example, buy 1.0930).

### Step 5: Set the stop

If price closes back inside the broken level, the breakout is cancelled. So the stop goes a little below the low of the retest. In the example, 1.0890, a risk of 40 pips.

### Step 6: Measure the target

Measure the height of the range from the broken edge: 1.0900 + 100 pips = 1.1000. Check the R:R by comparing stop and target; in the example, a 70-pip target against a 40-pip stop, about 1.75 times. More in the [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) lesson.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **What you could see:** a clear range, four reactions at the top, then a big-bodied close.
- **Breakout:** candle 18 closed at 1.0935.
- **Retest:** candle 20 had a low of 1.0899 and a close of 1.0915. The level held.
- **Entry:** candle 21 broke the high of candle 20 (1.0928). Buy at 1.0930.
- **Stop:** 1.0890, a risk of 40 pips.
- **Target:** 1.1000, a distance of 70 pips.
- **Result:** Price rose step by step and reached the target on candle 28.
- **Lesson:** Waiting for the retest made the entry later, but the stop was smaller and it was proven that the breakout was real.

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

The same range, but the breakout is weak.

- **What you could see:** the same range. Candle 18 closed at 1.0908, but the upper wick was long (high 1.0925).
- **Decision:** buy on the break of the high at 1.0927, stop 1.0884, a risk of 43 pips.
- **What happened:** Candle 19 closed at 1.0884 and came back inside the range. The stop was filled. Later, price went toward the bottom of the range.
- **Why it failed:** None of the three conditions was strong: small body, long wick (sellers were defending the top), and getting in without a retest.
- **Lesson:** You cannot get in just because "price went up". The loss was the planned 43 pips. More on this kind of break that comes back in the next lesson, [Fake Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/04-fake-breakout).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | Why it happens                          | What to do                                                                    |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Getting in as soon as you see a wick               | Fear of "missing it"                    | Wait for the close                                                            |
| Trading a breakout of a very narrow range          | Thinking every sideways move is a range | Look for at least two reactions at each edge                                  |
| Putting all your money in right after the breakout | Too much confidence                     | Small position, add the rest on the retest                                    |
| Keeping the stop at the other edge of the range    | It feels "safe"                         | Stop just inside the broken level; measure risk and make the position smaller |
| Trading a range breakout before big news           | Seeing price move                       | Wait before and after the news                                                |
| Getting in without measuring the target            | "Let it go as far as it goes"           | Measure the height and check R:R                                              |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The longer the range, the more likely the breakout is strong. More orders
  build up in a long range. A breakout of a short range comes back easily.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Even if the breakout candle is small, if several candles in a row close above,
  that is a sign of strength slowly building. Decide by looking at the sequence,
  not at one candle.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A breakout and a fake breakout look almost the same at the start. Never trade
  a breakout without a stop.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **In a choppy market:** When the range is shaky, every edge is a trap.
- **Around news:** A spike breaks through the edge and comes back. See this pattern in the [Liquidity and Stop Hunts](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) lesson.
- **Against the higher timeframe:** Even if an H1 range breaks up, the path is limited if D1 resistance is close. See [Multi-Timeframe Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis).
- **At slow times:** Breakouts before a session starts or near its end often do not hold.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- For a breakout to be real, look at the close, the body and the follow-through.
- An entry on the retest makes the stop smaller, but the retest does not always come.
- Target = the height of the range, measured from the broken edge.
- Wicks, small bodies and quick returns are signs of a trap.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. What are the three conditions for checking whether a breakout is real?
2. How do you measure the target of a range?
3. What does a long upper wick on the breakout candle usually mean?
4. If you enter on the retest, where does the stop usually go?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** A close outside the range, a big body, and follow-through or a retest that holds the broken level.

**2.** Measure the height of the range (top to bottom), then place the target that distance from the broken edge.

**3.** Sellers are defending that edge, so the breakout is more likely to be weak.

**4.** A little below the low of the retest candle, which means just inside the broken level.

</details>
