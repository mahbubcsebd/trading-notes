# Fake Breakout: Spot the Trap and Take the Chance the Other Way

You saw a breakout and got in, and the next candle came back and took out your stop. It makes you angry. But think: what did the people waiting on the other side get at that moment? **A fake breakout is not only a cause of loss; read correctly, it is a trade in itself.**

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** what a **fake breakout** is and how it forms.
- **Check** whether a breakout has failed **with one clear condition**.
- **Set the entry, stop and target** for the trade the other way.
- **Avoid the mistake** of treating a pullback as a fake breakout.

## Why it matters

- **Trapped buyers are fuel.** Their stops and sell orders push price the other way. You saw the basis of this in the [Liquidity and Stop Hunts](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) lesson.
- **The stop is very clear.** Everything is settled just above the fake peak.
- **Your own losses get smaller.** If you recognise the trap, you can get out of the first breakout trade quickly.

Prerequisites: [Range Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout) and [Role Reversal](/en/learn/05-support-resistance/02-role-reversal).

## What a fake breakout is

**A fake breakout is a breakout where price passes a level but cannot stay there, and quickly closes back inside the earlier range.**

Think of a shop door. A few people push their way in, then see there is no crowd inside, so they come out again. **Getting in and staying in are different things.**

The only reliable condition for recognising a fake breakout: **price has closed back inside the broken level.** Not on seeing a spike, but on seeing the close back inside.

## What it looks like on a chart: the trap and the trade the other way

It forms in three steps:

1. **Push (18):** the candle rose to 1.0932 but closed at 1.0912, with a big upper wick.
2. **Failure (19):** the next candle closed at 1.0888 and came back inside the range. This is the confirmation.
3. **Pressure (from 20):** buyers who got in above are trapped. Their selling to get out pushes price down.

## Step by step: trading a fake breakout

<Steps>

### Step 1: Mark the range or level first

A fake breakout depends on a valid level. In the example, the top at 1.0900, as in the Range Breakout lesson.

### Step 2: Look at the push

The level has been passed, but how? Usually a long wick, a small body or a weak close. If it is a real breakout with a strong body, do not go this way.

### Step 3: Wait for the close back inside

Check whether the candle closed inside the broken level. If not, nothing is confirmed, and there is no trade.

### Step 4: Enter

Right at the close back inside, or when the next candle's low breaks (in the example, sell 1.0888).

### Step 5: Put the stop above the fake peak

In the example, 1.0932. If price goes back there, the idea of the trap is proven wrong. A risk of 44 pips.

### Step 6: Set the target

The opposite edge of the range, or a reasonable place in the middle. In the example, 1.0808, a distance of 80 pips, about 1.8 times. You can close half the position halfway. More in the [Risk/Reward Ratio](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) lesson.

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

The chart above:

- **What you could see:** the same range (1.0800-1.0900). Candle 18 touched 1.0932 and closed at 1.0912, with a long upper wick.
- **Confirmation:** candle 19 closed at 1.0888, inside the range.
- **Entry:** sell 1.0888.
- **Stop:** 1.0932, above the fake peak. A risk of 44 pips.
- **Target:** 1.0808, a distance of 80 pips.
- **Result:** Price fell step by step and reached the target on candle 28.
- **Lesson:** Selling on candle 18 would have been a mistake, because it was not yet confirmed as a failure. The close back inside is the key.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade, and no result is guaranteed.)

## Example 2: when it is misread (synthetic)

Now the opposite: selling a real breakout, thinking it is fake.

/>

- **What you could see:** candle 18 closed at 1.0936 with a 60-pip body. Big and strong.
- **Decision:** As soon as candle 19 fell, the trader thought "trap". Sell 1.0910, stop 1.0946, a risk of 36 pips.
- **What happened:** Price did not close below 1.0900 (candle 19 closed at 1.0912). Candle 20 rose strongly and hit the stop. Later, price rose further.
- **Why it was wrong:** There was no close back inside, so the condition for failure was never met. This was a retest, not a failure.
- **Lesson:** Do not assume a fake from a wick or a pullback. The condition is a close inside the broken level. The loss was the planned 36 pips.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                          | Why it happens                    | What to do                                           |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the other way as soon as you see a spike | Rushing to catch it early         | Wait for the close back inside                       |
| Treating every pullback as a trap                | Seeing traps everywhere           | If there is no close inside the level, let it go     |
| Keeping the stop below the fake peak             | Trying to reduce risk             | Keep it above the peak and make the position smaller |
| Fighting a big trend                             | Believing your own guess          | Check the direction on the higher timeframe          |
| Getting in again and again on the same failure   | Thinking "this time it will work" | No more than twice on one setup                      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The longer the upper or lower wick of a fake breakout, the higher the chance
  of failure. A long wick means price rejected that place.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  In your own breakout trade, decide in advance at what price a close back
  inside will make you get out. That way, even if it is a trap, the loss stays
  limited.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  A trade the other way means going against strength. Keep the position small
  and always set a stop. No idea is certain.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **During a strong trend:** Breaks in the direction of the trend are often right; fighting the other way is risky.
- **During big news:** Spikes and returns happen fast, and you cannot read the confirmation.
- **At unclear levels:** If the level itself is blurry, the definition of failure is blurry too.
- **On very small timeframes:** There is so much noise that a close back inside means nothing.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- A fake breakout is confirmed when price closes back inside the broken level, not on seeing a spike.
- In the trade the other way, the stop goes above the fake peak.
- If a pullback holds the level, it is a retest, not a trap.
- In your own breakout trade too, set the condition for a return in advance.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. On what condition is a fake breakout confirmed?
2. In the trade the other way, where does the stop go?
3. How do you tell a pullback from a fake breakout?
4. If your own breakout trade turns out to be a trap, when do you get out?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** When price closes back inside the broken level.

**2.** A little above (or below) the fake peak, where the idea of the trap is proven wrong.

**3.** A close inside the level is a trap; a close that holds the level is a pullback or retest.

**4.** At the price you set in advance, which means when price closes back inside the broken level.
