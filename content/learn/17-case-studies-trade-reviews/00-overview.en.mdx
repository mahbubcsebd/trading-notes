# Trade Reviews and Cases: A Method of Learning From Events

A football coach watches the video after the match: where the position was wrong, where the pass was good. He does not learn by looking at the score. For a trader, the video is the journal and the chart; the score is profit and loss. This chapter is the method of watching the video.

## What is a trade review

**A trade review means learning from one trade, one event or one week by looking at the process of the decisions, not the result.**

This chapter answers one question: **"What do I learn from what happened, and how do I separate luck from skill?"**

## Why learn this now

- **You have data but no explanation.** You know the [journal](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/03-journal-template) and the [review](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/04-reviewing-trades); here they are applied to real cases.
- **Learning from the result is the biggest trap.** Winning does not mean being right.
- **Preparing for big events.** If you put the last event into a template, your head stays cool at the next one.
- **The base for making notes and cheat sheets.** The next chapter is a collection.

## What you will be able to do after this chapter

- **Break a trade into five steps** and separate process and result.
- **Place a trade in a four-box template.**
- **Read a big gold move** with four questions.
- **Recognize hindsight bias.**
- **Finish a weekly review in 30 minutes.**

## How the lessons join together

A learning cycle:

1. **Break down one trade.** Five steps, four boxes (lesson 1).
2. **Read a big event.** Cause, structure, a place live, bias (lesson 2).
3. **Wrap up at the end of the week.** A template and one goal (lesson 3).
4. **The goal goes in the plan.** Applied next week, and then again from 1.

## How to read this chapter

- After reading lesson 1, break down your own five trades at once.
- In lesson 2, check the facts of the real event yourself from reliable sources; no real prices are used here.
- At the weekend, fill in the template of lesson 3.
- All charts in this chapter are imaginary (synthetic), to explain the idea. The reconstruction of real events is imaginary too.
