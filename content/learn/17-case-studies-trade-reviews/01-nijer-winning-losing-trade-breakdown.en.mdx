# Breakdown of Your Own Winning and Losing Trade: Judge the Process, Not the Result

When a trade wins we think "I did it right", and when it loses "I made a mistake". But if you roll a die and get a six, the decision does not become good. In this lesson you will learn to break a trade down and judge the **process** (the decisions) and the **outcome** (the result) separately.

## What you will be able to do

- **Break a trade into five steps** and look.
- **Use a four-box template** (good/bad process × win/loss).
- **Separate luck and skill.**

## Why it matters

- **If you learn from the result, bad habits get rewarded.** To keep [Discipline](/en/learn/12-trading-psychology/01-discipline), the process is the thing to measure.
- **The fear of loss falls.** The loss of a good process is sad, not a mistake.
- **The raw material of a [review](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/04-reviewing-trades).** Without broken-down data you cannot split.

Prerequisites: [Journal Template](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/03-journal-template).

## What is a breakdown

**A trade breakdown is writing out each decision step of a trade separately and checking at each step whether the rules were followed.**

| Step          | Question                                                         |
| :------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Context    | What was the big picture? Did the plan's market condition match? |
| 2. Setup      | Were all the conditions of the setup met?                        |
| 3. Entry      | Did I enter at the place and time set by the rules?              |
| 4. Management | Did I change anything while it ran (stop, target, size)?         |
| 5. Exit       | Did I exit at the plan's stop/target, or from emotion?           |

Yes/no at each step. All "yes" means a **good process**.

## The four-box template

| Result \ Process | Good process                 | Bad process                      |
| :--------------- | :--------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Win              | A deserved win, repeat it    | **Luck**, log it as a rule break |
| Loss             | **A normal cost**, it's fine | A lesson, fix the mistake        |

The most dangerous box: **bad process, win**. It turns a bad habit into confidence.

## Step by step: writing a breakdown

<Steps>

### Step 1: Write it after the trade is closed, when the emotion of the result has faded

At least a few hours later. If emotion is there, the split is blurred.

### Step 2: Fill in the five-step template

Looking at the screenshot taken before entry (or the [checklist](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/03-pre-trade-checklist)), yes/no at each step.

### Step 3: Give the process verdict

All "yes" = good. One "no" = bad. No fine shades.

### Step 4: Which of the four boxes?

Match it with the result and pick the box.

### Step 5: One action

If the process was bad, the step that broke is the goal for the next trade. If it was the loss of a good process, there is nothing to do, just log it.

</Steps>

## Example 1: good process, loss (synthetic)

- **Context/Setup:** a close above resistance at 1.0960, a retest.
- **Entry/Management/Exit:** buy 1.0968, stop 1.0938, target 1.1028. Nothing changed. Exited at the stop.
- **Result:** -1R; then price went even past the target.
- **Verdict:** good process, loss. Box: **normal cost**.
- **Lesson:** if the stop had been moved or the entry changed, this loss would not have been a "lesson". Following the rules was the win.

## Example 2: bad process, win (synthetic)

- **What happened:** the wait for the retest was not tolerated; bought on the breakout candle itself.
- **Result:** +2R.
- **Verdict:** "no" at the entry step. Box: **luck**.
- **Why it is dangerous:** if you do the same next time, you will probably hit the stop (as you saw in the earlier lesson). A win makes a bad habit feel "proven".
- **Lesson:** write this win in the journal with the `Rule break` tag. Enjoy it, but do not make a habit of it.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens             | What to do                                 |
| :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| Analyzing only the losing trades             | The pain of losses         | Break down the wins too                    |
| Judging the process by the result            | Hindsight bias             | Look at the earlier screenshot/checklist   |
| Changing the rules after a good-process loss | The rush to ease the pain  | No change before 20 trades                 |
| Many actions at once                         | The urge to fix everything | One action                                 |
| Not writing it down                          | Thinking you'll remember   | Keep the breakdown template in the journal |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Every week, count how many trades are in each of the four boxes. The smaller
  the "bad process, win" box, the stronger your discipline.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  For the process verdict to stay honest, the rules need to be [written in
  advance](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/02-entry-exit-risk-rules-ek-page-e).
  What is not written, you cannot judge whether it was broken.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If the rules are unclear:** the process verdict becomes subjective.
- **If there are very few trades:** the pattern of the four boxes cannot be seen.
- **In a moment of intense emotion:** result and emotion distort the verdict; wait a little.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Five steps: context, setup, entry, management, exit.
- Four boxes: good/bad process × win/loss.
- The most dangerous box: bad process, win.
- The loss of a good process is a normal cost.

</KeyTakeaways>
