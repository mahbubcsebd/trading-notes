# Weekly Review Template: One Week, One Page, One Goal

At the end of the week, many people look only at the account number and close it. Happy if profit, disappointed if loss, then the same again on Monday. In this lesson you get a template that turns the weekly review into a regular 30-minute job.

## What you will be able to do

- **Fill in a one-page review template.**
- **Read the week** by **splitting it by process**.
- **Get one goal** from the review.

## Why it matters

- **It builds a habit.** [Daily and Weekly Routine](/en/learn/14-trading-plan-system/04-daily-weekly-routine) said when to review; here is its template.
- **The pattern is caught.** If the same mistake happens five times in five weeks, it is no longer "bad luck".
- **It gives the plan data.** [Plan v2](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/05-building-a-plan) comes from this review.

Prerequisites: [Your Own Trade Breakdown](/en/learn/17-case-studies-trade-reviews/01-nijer-winning-losing-trade-breakdown).

## The weekly review template

**A weekly review is breaking all the week's trades down in the same template with numbers and questions, and writing one goal for next week.**

| Part                     | What to write                                                       |
| :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Numbers               | total trades, wins/losses, net R, win rate                          |
| 2. Process               | how many trades followed the rules, how many broke them (with tags) |
| 3. Result without breaks | the net R of the rule-following trades, separately                  |
| 4. The biggest mistake   | the most expensive tag                                              |
| 5. Good work             | what went right (one)                                               |
| 6. Next week             | the news list, important levels, one goal                           |

The whole template is on one page. If it is more than that, you will not do it next week.

A net of +3R looks fine, but without the two broken trades it would have been +5R. That difference is this week's lesson.

## Step by step: a 30-minute review

<Steps>

### Step 1 (5 minutes): The numbers

Fill in part 1 from the journal. From the summary page of the spreadsheet.

### Step 2 (10 minutes): Process

Look at the tag of every trade. Which is `Plan` and which is `Rule break`. The reason for the broken trades in one line.

### Step 3 (5 minutes): The calculation without the broken ones

The net R of the process-following trades, separately. This is the real test of your strategy; the loss of the broken trades is not the strategy's.

### Step 4 (5 minutes): One good, one bad

Praise the good work (honestly). The reason for the most expensive mistake.

### Step 5 (5 minutes): Next week

The [news list](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo), levels, and **one** goal, such as "this week no entry without a retest".

</Steps>

## Example 1: when it works (synthetic)

- **Numbers:** 12 trades, 5 wins, 7 losses, net +3R.
- **Process:** 10 followed, 2 `Rule break` (moving the stop and a hurried entry).
- **Without the broken:** net +5R in the 10.
- **Goal:** "The source of my loss is not the setup but breaking the rules. Next week I will never move the stop."
- **Lesson:** the win rate is 42%, yet the goal is not changing the strategy but fixing behavior.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Example 2: when it fails (synthetic)

- **What happened:** the trader saw only the net number (+3R), thought "all fine" and closed the review.
- **Result:** in the next three weeks the same two kinds of breaking happened again. Nobody noticed, because it had not been split.
- **Why it failed:** looking only at the total result keeps the pattern invisible. A good week is dangerous, because then the review gets skipped.
- **Lesson:** fill in the template in a good week too. Especially in a good week.

(Synthetic educational example. This is not a real trade.)

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Why it happens             | What to do                                  |
| :--------------------------------------- | :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| Skipping the review in a good week       | A happy mood               | A fixed time in the calendar                |
| Many goals at once                       | The urge to fix everything | Just one goal                               |
| Reviewing in an open market              | For convenience            | When the market is closed (weekend)         |
| Not writing the reason for broken trades | Shame                      | An honest reason in one line                |
| Only numbers, no questions               | It is easy                 | Parts 4, 5 and 6 of the template are a must |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  At the end of each review, write the goal on a piece of paper and keep it next
  to the monitor. Read it first thing on Monday morning.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
  If you put four weeks of reviews side by side, the monthly pattern shows. That
  is the right time to change the plan.
</Callout>

## When this does not work

- **If there are very few trades:** a pattern cannot be understood from two trades; look at several weeks together.
- **If the journal is incomplete:** the review runs on guesses.
- **If the self-criticism is too much:** the goal is improvement, not punishment.

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- One page, 30 minutes, six parts.
- Split by process and calculate without the broken trades.
- Just one goal.
- Review in a good week too.

</KeyTakeaways>
