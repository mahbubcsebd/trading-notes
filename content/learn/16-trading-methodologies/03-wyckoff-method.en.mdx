# Wyckoff Method: Accumulation and Distribution

After a long decline, price starts moving around in one place. Most traders say, "a range." Wyckoff asks one step further: **who is buying in this range, who is selling, and who is getting tired?** To him a range is not empty time; it is a phase of preparation.

## History and origin

**Richard D. Wyckoff** was a Wall Street trader and writer. In the early 20th century he observed how large operators behaved and built a method that later took his name.

- **The source is usually books and courses.** Today's form is a mix of Wyckoff's own writing and the explanations of later teachers, who arranged his ideas into schematics.
- **Today's names** (SC, AR, spring and so on) are mostly labels organised by later teachers. Not every teacher teaches exactly the same schematic.
- **Forerunner of VSA and SMC.** VSA was built directly from Wyckoff's ideas. Many ideas in SMC/ICT are new names for Wyckoff events.

## Core philosophy

**Wyckoff's question: are big players accumulating or distributing?**

The idea is this: a large institution cannot buy or sell a huge amount in one day. It would push the price. So it builds a position slowly inside a range (accumulation) or unloads slowly (distribution). When that work is done, price leaves in a big move.

An analogy: if a rice wholesaler bought all the rice in the market in one day, the price would jump. So he buys quietly for weeks. Only when his warehouse is full does he start pushing the price up.

**Composite Man:** Wyckoff imagined all the big players as one man who plans his buying and selling. It is a thinking tool, not a real person. The small trader's question: "If I were that man, what would I be doing right now?"

<Callout type="note">
  Wyckoff is a **model**, not a proven truth. "Big players are accumulating" is
  inferred from price and volume. An ordinary trader has no way to see it
  directly.
</Callout>

## Core concepts

### 1. The three laws

Wyckoff's method stands on three laws.

- **Supply and demand:** when demand exceeds supply price rises; when it is lower, price falls.
- **Cause and effect:** a long range (cause) is followed by a big move (effect). The longer the range, the bigger the move can be.
- **Effort and result:** volume is the effort, the price move is the result. If they match, fine. If not, be warned.

An analogy: many people join in pushing a car (effort), yet the car does not move (result). Then something is holding it back.

### 2. The four-phase cycle

In Wyckoff's view a big move runs in a cycle:

| Phase        | What happens                                    |
| :----------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| Accumulation | A range at the bottom; big players build slowly |
| Markup       | The range breaks upward (uptrend)               |
| Distribution | A range at the top; big players unload slowly   |
| Markdown     | The range breaks downward (downtrend)           |

This lesson focuses mainly on **accumulation**. Distribution is its mirror image: the order is reversed.

### 3. The accumulation events

Inside the range certain events occur. They are given labels:

| Short  | Name                  | What happens                                        |
| :----- | :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| SC     | Selling Climax        | Heavy selling, big volume, price falls and stops    |
| AR     | Automatic Rally       | A natural bounce after SC; sets the range's ceiling |
| ST     | Secondary Test        | Returns near SC to test; low volume is a good sign  |
| Spring | Spring                | A brief dip below the range, then a quick return    |
| SOS    | Sign of Strength      | A strong rise above the range ceiling, high volume  |
| LPS    | Last Point of Support | A pullback after SOS; a higher low, the entry area  |

Wyckoff divides these events into five **phases (A to E)**: in A the decline stops (SC, AR, ST), in B price moves up and down inside the range, in C comes a final test such as the spring, in D strength appears (SOS, LPS), and in E price leaves the range.

**Disagreement:** not every range has every event. The spring is optional in many schematics. Names and order differ slightly between teachers. This lesson uses one common version.

### 4. Spring and Test

**A spring is a brief break below the range's lower edge followed by a quick return inside.** The purpose: to touch the stops gathered under the support and shake out weak hands. The next check is called the **Test**: price comes back near that low, but this time on low volume.

An analogy: in a market someone suddenly shouts a lower price, frightened sellers dump their goods, and he quietly buys it all. The next day the price returns to normal.

The condition: **a quick return.** If it does not return, it is not a spring but a real breakdown. A [fake breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/04-fake-breakout) and a [liquidity sweep](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts) are close ideas; Wyckoff calls it a spring.

### 5. Sign of Strength and Last Point of Support

**SOS is a strong rise above the range ceiling, usually with big volume and big bodies.** It says the willing buyers are now aggressive. **LPS is a small pullback after SOS, where price comes back to the old ceiling and stops.**

An analogy: the dam breaks and water rushes out (SOS). Then it returns a little and settles on the new bank (LPS).

This is exactly another name for Price Action's breakout-retest. The difference is that Wyckoff counts the earlier events (from SC to the spring) as part of the story.

### 6. Effort vs result (reading volume)

**Whether volume and price movement agree is Wyckoff's central test.**

- Big volume, small move: much effort, little result. Someone on the other side is absorbing.
- Small volume, small move, during a test: supply is drying up.
- Big volume, big move, at SOS: the strength is real.

The next lesson, VSA, covers this bar-level reading in detail.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The limit of volume in Forex and Gold.** Spot Forex and spot Gold have no
  central exchange. The volume in MT4/MT5 is really **tick volume**: how many
  times price changed on your broker's server. It is not the actual traded
  amount, only a rough hint. An alternative: Gold futures (CME GC) volume. So
  treat Wyckoff's volume-based signals as supporting, not as proof.
</Callout>

<Steps>

### Step 1: The earlier downtrend and the SC

Price falls step by step from 2403. Then one candle drops to **2296** and closes at 2306, with volume **3200**: the average over the whole chart is about 1165, so almost three times as much. Heavy selling, yet price stopped falling. This is the **SC**.

### Step 2: The AR and the range ceiling

A few candles later price rises to **2344** (AR). This bounce fixes the range ceiling. The range is now about 2296–2344.

### Step 3: Low volume on the ST

Price comes back down. The volume on this ST is only **600**: about one fifth of the SC. Selling pressure has dropped a lot.

### Step 4: Spring and Test

One candle drops below the range to **2287** but closes at **2304**, inside. Volume 1500. Two candles later comes the **Test**: volume only **450**, a narrow range. No one down there wants to sell any more.

### Step 5: SOS

Price breaks the range ceiling and closes at **2346**, volume **2400** (more than twice the average). The next candle also closes at 2356. Proof of strength.

### Step 6: LPS

Price falls back and stops at **2336** (the old ceiling), volume only **600**. A higher low on low volume: no supply. This is the LPS.

### Step 7: A warning at the top

At the end price touches 2487 and closes at 2467, with volume **2600** (one of the highest on this chart). Big volume and limited progress hint at distribution. But this chart has no full range at the top. So Wyckoff's honest answer is: "distribution is possible, but the full schematic has not formed."

</Steps>

## Basic trade logic

A Wyckoff trade usually comes **after** the sequence is complete, not on the first dip.

| Part      | Rule (common form)                                            | On the shared chart (synthetic)         |
| :-------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------- |
| Setup     | After a downtrend, the sequence (SC → AR → ST → spring → SOS) | All steps complete                      |
| Entry     | At the LPS, with confirmation of strength at the higher low   | Bullish close after the LPS at 2347     |
| Stop loss | Below the LPS (tight) or below the spring (wide)              | 2333 (below LPS) or 2284 (below spring) |
| Target    | The range height projected upward                             | 2344 + 48 = 2392                        |

**The stop trade-off:** with a stop at 2333 the risk is 2347 − 2333 = 14, the reward 2392 − 2347 = 45, a ratio of about **1:3**. But with a stop at 2284 the risk is 63 and the reward 45, a ratio of 1:0.7: poor. So most Wyckoff traders use the tight stop. But a tight stop can also be hit by an ordinary pullback.

The same logic works in other markets. An EURUSD H1 example (synthetic), not XAUUSD:

## Strengths

- **Gives a range meaning.** A "range" is not just "nothing is happening"; inside it events occur in order.
- **The three laws give a viewpoint.** Supply-demand, cause-effect, effort-result: these three questions help with any method.
- **Stops have logic.** Every event has a "price that proves it wrong".
- **Gives the big picture.** The four-phase cycle tells you where you are now.
- **The base of other methods.** The roots of many VSA, Volume Profile and SMC ideas are here.

## Weaknesses and limits

- **Judgment-based (subjectivity: medium).** Which candle is the SC, which is the spring: two traders can mark differently. There are rules, but not all are mechanical. Unlike Elliott or SMC, many opposing readings of one chart are not usual, so not "high"; but it is not mechanical either, so not "low".
- **Labelling afterwards is easy, doing it live is hard.** Going back over a chart and placing "SC, AR, ST" is simple. Live, you do not know where the range will end. This is the biggest trap of the method (hindsight bias).
- **Not every range is accumulation.** Many ranges break and go down. "There was a downtrend before" is not enough proof.
- **Dependence on volume.** In spot Forex and Gold tick volume is approximate. A method that leans this much on volume gives weaker signals here.
- **It takes patience.** The sequence can take weeks or months to complete. Many lose patience halfway.

**Evidence status:** there is no independent, large-scale research on Wyckoff's schematics; most of the knowledge comes from practitioners' books and courses. General research on technical analysis is mixed (Park and Irwin, 2007). **No method guarantees profit**; verify it in your own backtest.

## Who it suits

| Suits you if you…                                    | Does not suit you if you…                            |
| :--------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Can wait patiently on H4/D1                          | Prefer fast scalping                                 |
| Are curious about the story inside a range           | Want only mechanical, codeable rules                 |
| Already know some Price Action                       | Find volume methods unsatisfying without real volume |
| Want a conceptual framework that helps other methods | Want an immediate, clear entry signal                |

## Combining with other methods

- **Wyckoff + Price Action:** the most natural pair. Range, false break, retest: Price Action names these, Wyckoff tells the story. See the Price Action lesson.
- **Wyckoff + VSA:** VSA reads Wyckoff closely at bar level. A natural fit.
- **Wyckoff + Volume Profile:** a range usually shows up as a high-volume area on the profile. See the Volume Profile lesson.
- **Wyckoff ↔ SMC:** a spring and a sell-side liquidity sweep are almost the same event. Learning both languages together, each explains the other.

**What not to mix:** five oscillators over a Wyckoff sequence. Oscillators signal small moves; Wyckoff is the big picture. The time scales differ, so the signals will conflict.

## How to start

<Steps>

### Step 1: Find long ranges on a D1 chart

On a D1 chart of XAUUSD or your favourite pair, find three long ranges from the last two years. Was there a downtrend before?

### Step 2: Mark the events

Mark the SC, AR and ST in each. Write whether all the events are there. If not, that is also a result.

### Step 3: See what happened after

For each range, write whether price went up or down afterwards. How many of the three were accumulation?

### Step 4: Replay: label first, then the candle

Cover the right side of the chart and advance one candle at a time (replay). Each time, place your label first, then look at the next candle. This will expose hindsight bias.

### Step 5: Write the rules and backtest

Write the LPS-entry rules (entry, stop, target). [Backtest](/en/learn/15-backtesting-journal/01-how-to-backtest) on at least 30 examples.

</Steps>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                             | Why it is a problem               | Fix                                                |
| :---------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| Calling every range accumulation    | Bias toward the model             | Check there was a downtrend and that volume agrees |
| Buying before the spring returns    | No guarantee it will return       | Wait until the quick return is confirmed           |
| Entering without SOS                | Entering because it "looks cheap" | Enter after seeing proof of strength               |
| Treating tick volume as real volume | Differs by broker, approximate    | Look for big differences; consider futures volume  |
| Waiting without a set stop          | A big loss if the range breaks    | Write the "invalidation price" in advance          |

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Wyckoff's question: are big players accumulating or distributing; a range is the working phase.
- Three laws: supply-demand, cause-effect, effort-result.
- The accumulation sequence: SC, AR, ST, spring, SOS, LPS; entry comes after the proof of strength.
- The condition for a spring is a quick return; if it does not return, it is a real breakdown.
- Labelling afterwards is easy, live is hard; volume in Forex is tick-based. No guaranteed profit.

</KeyTakeaways>
