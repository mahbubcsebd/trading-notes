## Where liquidity sits

Stop orders cluster just beyond obvious levels: **equal highs**, **equal lows**, the previous day high or low, and Asian session extremes. Many traders put their stops there, so price is often pushed through these levels before the real move.

## What a sweep looks like

Price spikes past the level, trades through the stops, then closes back inside the range. The wick is the tell.

> **Key idea:** a sweep is not a signal by itself. It is a reason to start paying attention. I still need structure to shift in my favour.

## A simple sequence

1. Mark equal highs or lows and the previous day high/low.
2. Wait for price to take one of them.
3. Look for a **market structure shift** on a lower timeframe in the opposite direction.
4. Enter on the retracement into an order block or fair value gap.
5. Target the opposite side liquidity.

## On gold (XAUUSD)

Gold moves fast around the London open and New York open, and sweeps of the Asian range are common. Wider spreads and sharp spikes mean the stop needs room. Size down rather than tightening the stop too much.

## Risk

A level can be swept and keep going. If price closes beyond it and holds, the idea is wrong. Exit and move on.
