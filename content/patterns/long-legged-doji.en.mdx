In one candle price rose 40 pips, fell 50, and closed right at the open. Stops were hit on both sides, and the chart is left with a giant plus sign. That is a long-legged doji: the market fought hard and nobody won. The question is which way the next candle announces the result.

## At a glance

| Item          | Long-Legged Doji                                                                   |
| :------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type          | Neutral, high-volatility indecision candle, one candle                             |
| Where to look | After a long move, at a marked level, or at the end of a trend pullback            |
| Key features  | Almost no body, very long wicks above and below, range larger than nearby candles  |
| Confirmation  | Next candle closes above the doji high or below its low                            |
| Stop          | Beyond the wick on the side opposite the break, plus spread and a buffer           |
| Invalidation  | After the break, price re-enters the doji range and closes beyond the opposite end |
| Look-alikes   | Doji, [Spinning Top](/en/patterns/spinning-top), news spike candle                 |
| Opposite      | [Marubozu](/en/patterns/marubozu) (large body, no wicks)                           |

## What you will be able to do

- **Measure** the difference between a long-legged doji and a plain doji by range.
- **Separate** a news-release candle from a real long-legged doji.
- **Trade the break** by treating the doji's range as a small range.
- **Handle** the wide stop by cutting size.

## Why it matters

- **Volatility is up, direction is not decided.** Big wicks both ways mean both sides showed strength. A large move often follows, but you cannot know which way.
- **Stops get hit on both sides.** On the wrong side at the wrong place, this one candle can take out traders in both directions.
- **Easily confused with a news candle.** A data release prints nearly the same shape, but that is thin liquidity, not a battle.

Prerequisites: the [Doji](/en/patterns/doji) page and [Range Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout).

## What is a long-legged doji

**A long-legged doji is a doji whose open and close are almost equal, with very long wicks above and below that make its range clearly larger than nearby candles.** When the open-close sits near the middle of the range, Nison calls it a **rickshaw man**.

Think of a wrestling match. Each wrestler throws the other down, and both get back up. A hard fight the whole time, and the result is a draw. If a plain doji is two people shaking hands, a long-legged doji is two exhausted wrestlers.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                             | Required?               | Why                                                          |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open and close almost equal                      | Yes                     | A visible body makes it a high-wave spinning top             |
| Long wicks on both sides                         | Yes                     | One wick only makes it a dragonfly or gravestone             |
| Range clearly larger than nearby candles' ranges | Yes                     | This is the "long-legged" part that separates it from a doji |
| Candle has closed                                | Yes                     | A live candle can still build a body either way              |
| Not on a major news release                      | No (but very important) | A news candle's wicks reflect a thin market, not a battle    |

| Quality check  | Meaningful                                              | Weak                                           |
| :------------- | :------------------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------- |
| When           | Normal market, no news                                  | At a major data release                        |
| Where          | At a marked level or the end of a trend pullback        | Mid-range, no level                            |

<Callout type="note">
  To measure "range clearly larger", many traders use 1.5-2× the average range
  of the previous 10 candles. This is a working measure, not a standard
  definition. Set your own in a backtest.
</Callout>

### Same look, different meaning

| Pattern               | Shape                                     | Difference                                    |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| **Long-Legged Doji**  | Almost no body, very long wicks both ways | Intense fight, high volatility                |
| **Standard Doji**     | Almost no body, medium wicks              | Calm balance; range like its neighbours       |
| **High-wave candle**  | Small but visible body, very long wicks   | Nearly the same meaning; spinning top family  |
| **News spike candle** | Any shape, often huge wicks               | Thin liquidity at a release; treat separately |

## The story inside the candle

One common interpretation of a long-legged doji at a broken resistance (now support) at the end of an uptrend pullback, read through time:

1. **Buyers push up first.** Buy orders at support lift price a good distance.
2. **Sellers drive it down hard.** Profit-taking and new selling push price below support and trigger stops of earlier buyers.
3. **Buyers bring it back.** New buyers arrive lower and price closes right near the open.

The result: both sides made a big effort and weak traders on both sides were shaken out. Whichever way the next candle breaks and closes outside the doji range, that side scored the first clear win.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show
  whose stop was hit. We only see how price behaved.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading a long-legged doji

<Steps>

### Step 1: Check the news first

Look at the [economic calendar](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/02-economic-calendar-nfp-cpi-fomc). If the doji is the candle of a big release, stop here. Look again once the market calms down over the next few candles.

### Step 2: Level and trend

Where is the marked level, and which way is the higher-timeframe trend? A break in the trend direction is usually the cleaner setup.

### Step 3: Mark the doji's range

Draw lines at the doji high and low. It is now a small range. Check the shape: almost no body, range clearly larger than nearby candles.

### Step 4: Wait for the break, then enter

| Entry                      | Where                                                 | Pro                                      | Con                               |
| :------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| **Range break** (standard) | Stop order just beyond the end in the trend direction | You enter only once price shows its hand | Stop beyond the whole range: wide |
| **Confirmation close**     | Once the next candle closes beyond the end            | Fewer wick false breaks                  | Entry further away                |
| **Orders both ways (OCO)** | Buy stop above the high, sell stop below the low      | No need to guess direction               | Whipsaw risk on both sides        |

For beginners, a single order in the trend direction is the simplest.

### Step 5: Place the stop

Beyond the wick on the side opposite the break, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) and a buffer. The whole doji range is the stop distance, so it will be wide.

### Step 6: Size and target

Wide stop means small size: calculate [position size](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) from the entry-to-stop distance, risking no more than 1%. Put the target just before the next obstacle. If the [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) to there is below 1:1.5, skip it.

### Step 7: Manage

Repeated closes back in the middle of the doji range after the break mean the break is weak; consider exiting. A close beyond the opposite end means the idea is wrong.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-long-legged-doji-pretrade-en"

    "Have both the doji and the breaking candle closed?",
    "With a full-range stop, is the risk reward to the next obstacle still at least 1:1.5?",

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="long-legged-doji-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="USDJPY H4 (illustrative, synthetic) — a long-legged doji on the retest of an old ceiling after a range breakout; long on the high break, in the trend direction."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-6 ranged under a ceiling near 149.44. Candle 7 broke out and closed at 149.72. Candle 12 made a swing high at 150.90, and candles 13-16 pulled back to the old ceiling.
- **The long-legged doji:** Candle 17 opened at 149.518 and closed at 149.528. High 149.798, low 149.238: about 28 pips of wick each way, with the low poking below the ceiling and returning.
- **Decision:** Uptrend, so an order on the upside only. Buy stop at 149.818, stop at 149.198 (4 pips below the low): 62 pips of risk. Target 150.865, just under the swing high, about 1.7R.
- **Result:** Candle 18 closed at 149.868 and filled the order. Candle 26 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** In the trend direction, on the retest of a broken level, without news. The stop was wide, so the target went just before the real obstacle instead of the textbook 2R.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="long-legged-doji-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H1 (illustrative, synthetic) — a long-legged doji on a data-release candle; bought on the high break, then stopped out."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-10 drifted quietly between about 1.0834 and 1.0860, with most bodies of 2-6 pips.
- **The doji:** Candle 11 was the candle of a major data release: open 1.0837, close 1.0839, high 1.0885, low 1.0792. About 45 pips of wick each way.
- **The wrong decision:** "A long-legged doji near support; buy if the high breaks." Buy stop 1.0887, stop 1.0788: 99 pips of risk.
- **What happened:** Candle 12 spiked to 1.0893 and filled the order, but closed at 1.0824. Candle 13 broke the stop.
- **Why it failed:** This was not a battle at a level; it was thin liquidity at the release. The first checklist box would have filtered it out. On top of that, a 99-pip stop was abnormally wide compared with the quiet market.

**Lesson:** A news candle's wicks are noise, not a signal. The stop set in advance capped the loss at 1R, but with [slippage](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening) on news it could have been more in practice.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens                      | What to do instead                                |
| :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| Treating a news candle as a long-legged doji | Same shape                          | Check the economic calendar; skip release candles |
| Stop in the middle of the doji               | Fear of a wide stop                 | Stop beyond the opposite wick; cut size           |
| Orders both ways, stopped out both ways      | Not wanting to choose a direction   | Pick one order in the trend direction             |
| Target at 2R by default                      | 2R is far away with a wide stop     | Measure R:R to the next obstacle; skip below 1.5  |
| Trading long-legged dojis mid-range          | Treating every big wick as a signal | Only at levels or at the end of pullbacks         |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Measure the long-legged doji's range with
  [ATR](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/04-atr-stop-size). If the range is
  more than twice ATR, a full-range stop is often too wide. Then consider
  entering on the first pullback after the break on a lower timeframe.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Two or three long-legged dojis or high-wave candles in a row at the top of a
  trend mean momentum is running out. Protecting profit in an open trade then
  matters more than a new trade in the trend direction.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Spreads widen a lot on news and stop orders often fill at worse prices.
  Entering at the moment of a release because of a long-legged doji is one of
  the riskiest things a beginner can do.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Long-legged doji = almost no body, very long wicks both ways, range clearly larger than nearby candles.
- It shows an intense fight, not direction; the direction of the next candle's break is the answer.
- Skip candles of news releases; they picture thin liquidity.
- Enter on a range break in the trend direction; stop beyond the opposite wick, with reduced size, and the target before the real obstacle.

</KeyTakeaways>

|      |              |             |                           |                    |            |

Then compare the average R for news candles and normal candles. Also count how often price fell back into the range after the break. Do not draw conclusions from fewer than 30 samples.

## Quiz

1. What is the main difference between a long-legged doji and a standard doji?
2. A perfect long-legged doji forms on the NFP release candle. What do you do?
3. Why is a long-legged doji's stop wide, and how do you handle it?
4. In Example 1, why was the target placed under the swing high instead of at 2R?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** Both have almost no body. The long-legged doji's wicks and range are clearly larger than nearby candles; a standard doji's range is like its neighbours'.

**2.** No trade. It is a product of thin liquidity at the release. Once the market calms down, look again at the levels and structure.

**3.** The stop goes beyond the opposite wick, so the whole large range is the risk. Cut size to keep risk at 1%, and skip it if R:R to the next obstacle is below 1.5.

**4.** A 2R target would have sat beyond the swing high. Placing it just before the real obstacle gave about 1.7R, still above 1.5.
