Price has been falling for ten days, then come three more big red days. "Three black crows, it is going lower!" You go short exactly where everyone else does. The next day price jumps, because those who shorted much earlier are taking profit. The same three crows mark a beginning when they come from a top, and often an ending after a long decline. This page is the mirror of [Three White Soldiers](/en/patterns/three-white-soldiers); the rules and entry details are there.

## At a glance

| Item          | Three Black Crows                                                                                                             |
| :------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type          | Bearish reversal or breakdown momentum, three candles                                                                         |
| Where to look | At the top of an uptrend or as a top range breaks                                                                             |
| Key features  | Three long bearish candles, each opening within or at the prior body's close, closing near its low, each close below the last |
| Confirmation  | The three candles are the confirmation; afterwards, pullbacks stay below the broken level                                     |
| Stop          | Above the second crow's open (medium) or above the first crow's high (conservative)                                           |
| Invalidation  | A close above the second crow's open                                                                                          |
| Look-alikes   | Identical three crows, a run of [Bearish Engulfing](/en/patterns/bearish-engulfing) candles                                   |
| Opposite      | Three White Soldiers                                                                                                          |

## What you will be able to do

- **Separate** crows from a top from **crows at the end of a long decline**.
- **Sell the pullback** instead of chasing the third close.
- **Measure** the major support below in advance and skip the trade when needed.

## Why it matters

- **A clear picture of sellers taking over from a top.** For three sessions sellers stay in control, closing lower each time.
- **Arriving late, the meaning flips.** Nison warns that crows after a long decline may mean the market is oversold. Late in the move there are few new sellers and many shorts taking profit.
- **The same temptation to chase as soldiers.** After three big red candles price has already fallen a lot; entering there means a huge stop.

Prerequisites: the Three White Soldiers page (linked above) and [Trend Continuation vs Reversal](/en/learn/09-price-action-entries-strategies/02-trend-continuation-vs-reversal).

## What are three black crows

**Three black crows are three consecutive long bearish candles, each opening within or at the prior candle's body, closing near its own low, with each close lower than the last.** Three crows perched on a branch, a bad omen.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                                       | Required?               | Why                                                       |
| :--------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| Three consecutive long bearish candles, each closing lower | Yes                     | A steady fall                                             |
| Each closes near its low (small lower wick)                | Yes                     | A long lower wick means buyers below                      |
| Bodies not shrinking                                       | Yes                     | Shrinking bodies mean sellers are tiring                  |
| An uptrend or top range before it, and a level broken      | Yes                     | At the end of a long decline the same shape is often late |
| Major support below at least 1.5R away                     | No (but very important) | Crows landing right on support face a bounce              |

| Quality check    | Strong                            | Weak                                         |
| :--------------- | :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| What came before | An uptrend or a top range         | Already a long, steep decline                |
| Location         | Breaking a range floor or support | Right at a major support                     |
| Bodies           | All three about equal             | Shrinking, or a long lower wick on the third |
| Room below       | At least 1.5R to the next support | Support within 1R or right below             |

## The story inside the candle

The soldiers story in reverse. One common interpretation: at the top buyers have stalled, then for three sessions sellers close lower each time and buyers cannot push price back once. But at the end of a long decline the same three candles are often last-minute fear selling and late sellers arriving. The profit-taking of those who shorted earlier then creates the next bounce.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show who
  bought or sold.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading three black crows

The full steps are on the Three White Soldiers page, reversed. Only what differs:

<Steps>

### Step 1: Measure the age of the fall

How many candles, and how far, has price fallen before the crows? If it is already a long decline, be careful. Note how far the major support below (D1 or weekly) is.

### Step 2: Entry and stop

Sell limit at 50% of the third body, or on a retest of the broken level. Stop above the second crow's open, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap).

### Step 3: R:R

If the [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) to the nearest support below is under 1:1.5, skip it.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-three-black-crows-pretrade-en"
  items={[
    "Was there an uptrend or top range before (not a long decline), and did the crows break a level?",
    "Does the shape pass the rules (three long red candles, small lower wicks, bodies not shrinking)?",
    "Have all three candles closed, and is the entry on a pullback rather than a chase?",
    "Is the risk reward to the nearest support below at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is there no major higher-timeframe support right below?",
  ]}
/>

If any box stays empty, skip the trade.

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="three-black-crows-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD H1 (illustrative, synthetic) — three black crows out of a flat top range; short on a pullback to 50% of the third body."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-3 rallied, and candles 4-11 formed a flat range above a floor near 1.0861.
- **The crows:** Candle 12 ran 1.0871 to 1.0843, candle 13 1.0843 to 1.0810, candle 14 1.0810 to 1.0779. Bodies of 28, 33 and 31 pips, lower wicks of only 2 pips.
- **Decision:** Sell limit at 1.07945 (50% of the third body). Stop 1.0847 (above the second crow's open): about 52 pips of risk. Target 1.06895 (2R).
- **Result:** Candle 15 paused and candle 16 touched 1.07945, filling the order. Candle 26 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** From a top, breaking a range, equally strong crows, and an entry on the pullback.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="three-black-crows-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="USDJPY D1 (illustrative, synthetic) — three black crows at the end of a long decline, at major support. Sold at the third close, then stopped out on the bounce."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-9 fell long and steeply from 152.00 to 148.87. Below, near 147.74, sat an old weekly support (marked from history beyond the chart).
- **The crows:** Candle 10 ran 148.87 to 148.57, candle 11 148.57 to 148.25, candle 12 148.25 to 147.802, with its low right at the support.
- **The wrong decision:** "Three crows, it will go lower." Sell at the third close at 147.802, stop 148.62: 82 pips of risk, target 146.166, far below the support.
- **What happened:** Candle 13 rose hard off support, and candle 15 broke the stop.
- **Why it failed:** The crows came late (at the end of a long decline), and right at major support. The first, third, fourth and fifth checklist boxes would have filtered it out.

**Lesson:** Crows that arrive late, into support, often mark the end of the fall. Because the stop was set in advance, the loss stayed at 1R.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Why it happens                 | What to do instead                                   |
| :----------------------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Selling crows at the end of a long decline | "Momentum is running"          | Measure how far price fell before the crows          |
| Selling the third close in a hurry         | Fear of missing out            | A limit at 50% of the third body or the broken level |
| Not checking the weekly support below      | Only looking at your timeframe | Check the D1 and weekly charts before trading        |
| Stop at the third candle's high            | Wanting a small stop           | Above the second crow's open                         |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  A long lower wick under the third crow, or a third crow much smaller than the
  first two, is the reverse of the soldiers' advance block: sellers are tiring.
  If you hold a short, think about protecting profit.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Check the higher timeframe: three H1 crows are often one large red D1 candle.
  Where that D1 candle sits, in the middle of nowhere or right on support, is
  the real context. More in [Multi-Timeframe
  Analysis](/en/learn/03-market-structure/05-multi-timeframe-analysis).
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Three black crows = three consecutive long red candles, each closing lower, near its low, with small lower wicks.
- Strong when they come from a top or a top range; often late after a long decline, especially at major support.
- Do not chase the third close: enter at 50% of the third body or the broken level, stop above the second crow's open.
- Measure the D1 and weekly support below before the trade.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice and your own backtest

Open EURUSD H1 or USDJPY D1 in TradingView. Find at least 20 runs of three red candles. For each, log: date, how many candles the decline had run before, R to the major support below, whether a 50% retest came, and result (R). Then split into "from a top" and "at the end of a long decline" and compare the average R. Do not draw conclusions from fewer than 30 samples.

## Quiz

1. When can the same three black crows mark a beginning, and when an ending?
2. The third crow closes right on a weekly support. What do you do?
3. Where does the stop go for crows?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** From a top or breaking a top range, a beginning. At the end of a long, steep decline, especially at major support, often an ending.

**2.** Skip the short. There is no room below and the bounce risk is high.

**3.** Above the second crow's open (medium), or above the first crow's high (conservative), plus spread.
