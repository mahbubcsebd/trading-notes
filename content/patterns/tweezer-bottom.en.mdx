Two candles in a row print their low at exactly the same price, as if someone stepped on the floor twice to check it holds. "Double bottom, tweezer, buyers are here!" Often true. But in the middle of a downtrend, equal lows often mean the opposite: everyone has a stop just below that low, and the market comes back to take those stops. Which tweezer is which is decided by the level.

## At a glance

| Item          | Tweezer Bottom                                                                                                         |
| :------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type          | Bullish reversal, two (or more) candles                                                                                |
| Where to look | At the end of a downtrend or pullback, at a marked support                                                             |
| Key features  | Two consecutive candles with lows at almost the same price; usually the first bearish, the second bullish              |
| Confirmation  | A break of the second candle's high, or the next candle closing above it                                               |
| Stop          | Below the shared low, plus spread and a buffer                                                                         |
| Invalidation  | A close below the shared low                                                                                           |
| Look-alikes   | Double bottom (many candles apart), [Bullish Engulfing](/en/patterns/bullish-engulfing), [Hammer](/en/patterns/hammer) |
| Opposite      | [Tweezer Top](/en/patterns/tweezer-top)                                                                                |

## What you will be able to do

- **Decide** by measurement how equal the lows must be to count as a tweezer.
- **Combine** a tweezer with other patterns (engulfing, hammer, harami) to judge strength.
- **Explain** why equal lows without a real level are a **liquidity pool**.

## Why it matters

- **Rejected twice.** One candle rejecting a price is one thing; two consecutive candles rejecting the same price is stronger evidence.
- **Weak alone, strong in combination.** Nison's observation: tweezers mean more when they coincide with another candle pattern, such as the second candle being a hammer or a bullish engulfing.
- **Equal lows are where stops sit.** Many traders put stops just below equal lows. So a tweezer in the wrong place is exactly the target of a sweep. More in [Liquidity and Stop Hunts](/en/learn/03-market-structure/04-liquidity-stop-hunts).

Prerequisite: [Two Candle Patterns](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/03-two-candle-patterns).

## What is a tweezer bottom

**A tweezer bottom is two (sometimes more) consecutive candles whose lows sit at almost the same price, at support at the end of a downtrend.** The name comes from tweezers: two equal prongs reaching down side by side and stopping at the same point.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                                      | Required?          | Why                                             |
| :-------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| Two consecutive candles with almost equal lows            | Yes                | This is the "tweezer"                           |
| A downtrend or pullback before it                         | Yes                | Equal lows mid-range are ordinary               |
| At a marked support                                       | Yes                | Without a level, equal lows often attract stops |
| First candle bearish, second bullish                      | No (but important) | The colour change shows control shifting        |
| The second candle is itself a pattern (hammer, engulfing) | No                 | Per Nison, stronger in combination              |

<Callout type="note">
  A working rule for "almost equal": the gap between the two lows is under 10%
  of ATR, or about 2-3 pips on EURUSD H1. This is not a standard definition; set
  your own in a backtest.
</Callout>

| Quality check  | Strong                                      | Weak                               |
| :------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------- |
| Location       | At a marked support or demand zone          | Mid-downtrend, no level            |

### Same look, different meaning

| Pattern                         | Shape                                   | Difference                                   |
| :------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| **Tweezer Bottom**              | Two consecutive candles with equal lows | An immediate double rejection by two candles |
| **Double Bottom**               | Two equal lows, but many candles apart  | A chart pattern; a bigger structure          |
| **Bullish Engulfing**           | Second body covers the first body       | Equal lows not required                      |
| **Equal lows (a sweep target)** | Equal lows mid-downtrend, no level      | Where stops collect, not support             |

## The story inside the candle

One common interpretation of a tweezer bottom at support:

1. **Candle A: sellers push down to support.** A low forms, where buyers stop them.
2. **Candle B: sellers test the same low again.** But they cannot take it a single pip lower.
3. **Buyers win.** The second candle closes higher. The same price has been defended twice.

The result: sellers tried twice and failed. But without a level the same picture has the opposite reading: everyone's stops have collected under the two lows, and larger players may come back for exactly those.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show
  where anyone's stop is.
</Callout>

## Gaps in a 24-hour market

<Callout type="note">
  The tweezer definition needs no gap, so the rule does not change in Forex.
  Just remember: lows can differ by a few pipettes between brokers, because each
  broker has its own data feed. So measure "almost equal", not "exactly equal".
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading a tweezer bottom

<Steps>

### Step 1: Level first

Draw support in advance. If the equal lows are not at that support or just below it, stop here.

### Step 2: Wait for both candles to close

A live second candle can still close below the first low. Count it only once both have closed.

### Step 3: Measure

How far apart are the two lows (compared with ATR)? Is the second candle bullish? Does it close above the first one's body? Is the second candle itself a hammer or engulfing?

### Step 4: Entry type

| Entry                     | Where                                        | Pro                              | Con                           |
| :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **High break** (standard) | Buy stop just above the second candle's high | You enter only if buyers push on | Slightly higher entry         |
| **Close entry**           | Buy as the second candle closes              | Closer entry                     | No confirmation               |
| **Retest limit**          | Buy limit at the middle of the second body   | Smaller stop                     | Missed if price never returns |

### Step 5: Place the stop

Below the shared low, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) and a buffer. Do not keep the buffer too small: many stops sit just under equal lows, and wicks there are common.

### Step 6: Size and target

[Position size](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) from the entry-to-stop distance, risking no more than 1%. Target before the prior swing high, with a [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) of at least 1:1.5.

### Step 7: Manage

A close below the shared low means the idea is wrong: get out.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-tweezer-bottom-pretrade-en"

    "Is the risk reward to the prior swing high at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is the higher-timeframe trend or level not against the trade?",
  ]}

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="tweezer-bottom-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="XAUUSD H4 (illustrative, synthetic) — a tweezer bottom just under a once-tested support; long on the high break."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candle 4 marked support at 2373.87. Candles 5-9 rallied to about 2430, and 10-13 fell back.
- **The tweezer:** Candle 14 was bearish with a low of 2372.37 ($1.50 under support). Candle 15's low was 2372.57, only 20 cents apart, and it closed at 2383.37, above candle 14's open: a tweezer and a bullish engulfing in one place.
- **Decision:** Buy stop at 2384.87. Stop at 2369.87 ($2.50 below the shared low): $15 of risk. Target 2414.87 (2R); room to the swing high was about 3R.
- **Result:** Candle 16 broke the high and filled the order. Candle 22 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** A real level, a double rejection, and a second candle that was itself a strong pattern.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="tweezer-bottom-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="GBPUSD H1 (illustrative, synthetic) — equal lows mid-downtrend, no support. Bought on the high break, then swept."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-6 were a steady downtrend from 1.2750 to 1.2670. No marked support below.
- **The tweezer:** Candle 7's low was 1.2656, candle 8's low 1.2657, and candle 8 closed at 1.2672, above candle 7's open. A perfect shape.
- **The wrong decision:** Buy stop 1.2676, stop 1.2651: 25 pips of risk.
- **What happened:** Candle 9 filled the order. Candle 10 turned down, and candle 11 dropped below the equal lows to 1.2644, taking the stop, before the downtrend carried on.
- **Why it failed:** The equal lows had no level behind them, in the middle of a downtrend. Stops had collected there, and the market took them. The first checklist box would have filtered it out.

**Lesson:** Equal lows without a real level are a magnet for stops, not support. Because the stop was set in advance, the loss stayed at 1R.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Why it happens                   | What to do instead                            |
| :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Treating any equal lows as support         | "It held twice"                  | Check first whether a marked level exists     |
| Stop right under the equal lows, no buffer | Wanting a small stop             | Add a buffer, a fraction of ATR or a few pips |
| Looking for exactly equal lows             | Not knowing broker feeds differ  | Measure "almost equal"                        |
| Counting every tweezer on small timeframes | Equal lows are very common there | H1 or above, and only at levels               |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Think from the other side: when you see equal lows with no level in a
  downtrend, do not go long; ask whether price will sweep below them and come
  back. A candle that returns to support after the sweep often gives a cleaner
  setup. More in [Fake
  Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/04-fake-breakout).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  If the tweezer's second candle is itself a hammer, bullish engulfing or
  piercing line, run that pattern's quality checks too. When both patterns pass
  their rules, the signal is stronger.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Tweezer bottom = two consecutive candles with almost equal lows, at support at the end of a downtrend.
- Measure "almost equal" (a small fraction of ATR); lows differ slightly between brokers.
- Strong in combination with another pattern; weak alone.
- Equal lows without a real level are a magnet for stops; no long there.

</KeyTakeaways>
