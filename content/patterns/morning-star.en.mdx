A long red candle, then a tiny candle, then a long green one. A textbook morning star. You buy, price rises a little, then stops exactly at the moving average it has been trading under for twenty days. The three-candle story is lovely, but the rest of the chart tells a story too.

## At a glance

| Item          | Morning Star                                                                                                                                      |
| :------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Type          | Bullish reversal, three candles                                                                                                                   |
| Where to look | At the end of a downtrend, at a marked support                                                                                                    |
| Key features  | 1: long bearish; 2: a small-body "star" (often a doji) near or below 1's close; 3: long bullish, closing above the middle of 1's body             |
| Confirmation  | The third candle is the confirmation; it must close above 50% of the first body                                                                   |
| Stop          | Below the star's low (the lowest of the three), plus spread and a buffer                                                                          |
| Invalidation  | A close below the star's low                                                                                                                      |
| Look-alikes   | [Bullish Engulfing](/en/patterns/bullish-engulfing), [Piercing Line](/en/patterns/piercing-line), [Three Inside Up](/en/patterns/three-inside-up) |
| Opposite      | [Evening Star](/en/patterns/evening-star)                                                                                                         |

## What you will be able to do

- **Explain** the role of each of the morning star's three candles.
- **Know** how to measure the pattern without gaps in Forex.
- **Place** an entry at the third candle's close and a stop below the star.
- **Measure** dynamic obstacles such as moving averages.

## Why it matters

- **The whole reversal story in one place.** Sellers in the first candle, stalling in the second, buyers in the third. More information than a doji or an engulfing.
- **Confirmation is built in.** The third candle is itself the proof, so there is less waiting for separate confirmation.
- **The stop is usually wide.** The stop goes below the star and the entry at the third close, so the distance is large. Judging R:R matters.

Prerequisites: the [Doji](/en/patterns/doji) page and [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context).

## What is a morning star

**A morning star is a three-candle pattern: a long bearish candle in a downtrend, then a small-body candle (the star), then a long bullish candle that closes above the middle of the first candle's body.** The name comes from the morning star: the star you see at the end of the night, just before the sun rises.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                                                   | Required? | Why                                               |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| A downtrend before it; the first candle long and bearish               | Yes       | Sellers' last strong push                         |
| The second candle's body small, near or below the first close          | Yes       | The picture of momentum stalling                  |
| The third candle long and bullish, closing above 50% of the first body | Yes       | Buyers' win; below the middle it is weak          |
| At a marked support                                                    | Yes       | Without a level it is a bounce inside a downtrend |
| The star is a doji (morning doji star)                                 | No        | A clearer indecision                              |

### Same look, different meaning

| Pattern               | Shape                                                  | Difference                                       |
| :-------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Morning Star**      | Long red, small star, long green                       | The full three-candle story                      |
| **Abandoned Baby**    | Gaps on both sides of the star (wicks do not touch)    | Rare, especially in Forex; the extreme form      |
| **Bullish Engulfing** | Two candles; the second body covers the first          | No star in the middle                            |
| **Three Inside Up**   | A harami, then a third candle closing above the mother | The second candle is inside the first, not below |

## The story inside the candle

One common interpretation of a morning star at support:

1. **First candle: sellers push hard.** A long red candle, one more strong push of the downtrend.
2. **The star: stalling.** Price falls a little further (often below support) but closes with a small body. Sellers can no longer push down as before.
3. **Third candle: buyers attack.** A long green candle takes back more than half of the first candle.

The result: across three candles control passes from sellers to buyers. But higher-timeframe obstacles (MAs, lower highs) are outside this story and have to be measured separately.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show who
  bought or sold.
</Callout>

## Gaps in a 24-hour market

<Callout type="note">
  Nison's original definition has the star gapping below the first candle and
  the third candle gapping above the star. In Forex these gaps hardly occur. The
  practical rule: **the star's body sits near or below the first candle's close,
  ideally with the star's low below the first low; the third candle starts from
  the star's close and closes above 50% of the first body.** The abandoned baby
  (full gaps on both sides) is practically never seen in Forex.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading a morning star

<Steps>

### Step 1: Level and obstacles first

Draw support in advance. Look for obstacles above: the prior swing high, lower highs, and the moving average you use. Write down how far away they are.

### Step 2: Wait for all three candles to close

The third candle is the confirmation. While live, it can still fall back below 50%.

### Step 3: Measure

Is the first candle long? Is the star's body small? What percentage of the first body did the third candle take back? (The same calculation as the piercing line.)

### Step 4: Entry type

| Entry                         | Where                                     | Pro                            | Con                           |
| :---------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **Third close** (standard)    | Buy as the third candle closes            | Confirmation is in the pattern | Wide stop                     |
| **Third candle's high break** | Buy stop above the third candle's high    | More certain                   | Higher entry, smaller R:R     |
| **50% retest**                | Buy limit at the middle of the third body | Smaller stop                   | Missed if price never returns |

### Step 5: Place the stop

Below the star's low, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) and a buffer.

### Step 6: Size and target

[Position size](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) from the entry-to-stop distance, risking no more than 1%. Target before the nearest obstacle, the swing high **or the moving average**, whichever comes first. [Risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) at least 1:1.5.

### Step 7: Manage

Repeated closes below the middle of the first candle's body mean the idea is weakening. A close below the star's low means it is wrong.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-morning-star-pretrade-en"
  items={[
    "Was support marked before the morning star, and is the star at that level?",
    "Does the shape pass the rules (long red, small star, third candle above 50% of the first body)?",
    "Have all three candles closed?",
    "Is the risk reward to the nearest obstacle (swing high, lower high or moving average) at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is the higher-timeframe trend or level not against the trade?",
  ]}
/>

If any box stays empty, skip the trade.

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="morning-star-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="GBPUSD H1 (illustrative, synthetic) — a morning doji star at a once-tested support; long at the third candle's close."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candle 4 marked support near 1.2679. Candles 5-9 rallied to about 1.2773, and 10-13 fell back.
- **The morning star:** Candle 14 was a long red candle (1.2707 to 1.2683). Candle 15 was a doji (1.2683 to 1.26835) with a low of 1.2673, below support. Candle 16 was a long green candle closing at 1.27003: about 72% of the first body.
- **Decision:** Buy at the third close at 1.27003. Stop 1.2669 (4 pips below the star's low): about 31 pips of risk. Target 1.27629 (2R); room to the swing high was about 2.3R.
- **Result:** Candle 24 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** A fresh level, a doji star below it, a deep third candle, and open space above.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="morning-star-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="XAUUSD D1 (illustrative, synthetic) — a morning star with no support under a falling SMA 10. The long runs straight into the SMA and is rejected."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-19 fell from 2480 to about 2390, slowing into a grind at the end. Price stayed below its falling 10-day SMA throughout. No marked support below.
- **The morning star:** Candle 20 was a long red candle (2390 to 2370). Candle 21 was a small star (2370 to 2371) with a low of 2364. Candle 22 closed at 2383: 65% of the first body.
- **The wrong decision:** Buy at the third close at 2383, stop 2361: $22 of risk, target 2427 (2R). But the SMA was at about 2388.50, only $5.50 above the entry: 0.25R.
- **What happened:** Candle 23 rose to the SMA (2390), candle 24 turned back from there, and candle 25 broke the stop.
- **Why it failed:** No support, and the nearest obstacle (the falling SMA) was far inside 1.5R. The first and fourth checklist boxes would have filtered it out.

**Lesson:** A falling average right above the entry is an obstacle too. Because the stop was set in advance, the loss stayed at 1R.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Why it happens                    | What to do instead                                   |
| :---------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading on the three candles alone              | Ignoring the rest of the chart    | Measure the level and obstacles above first          |
| Buying when the third candle barely reaches 50% | Measuring by eye                  | Calculate the penetration %; be careful below 60%    |
| Stop at the third candle's low                  | Wanting a small stop              | Below the star's low                                 |
| Not treating a moving average as an obstacle    | Only looking at horizontal levels | Check whether your MA sits before the target         |
| Waiting for an abandoned baby                   | Stock examples from books         | It hardly happens in Forex; a morning star is enough |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  If the morning star's stop feels too wide, consider a limit order at the
  middle of the third candle's body. A small pullback often follows the third
  candle; if it does not, you miss the trade, but the R:R is much better.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  More on moving averages: [Moving Averages and
  Crossovers](/en/learn/07-technical-indicators/01-moving-averages-crossover).
  Which MA you use matters less; what matters is using the same one on every
  chart and checking whether it sits before your target.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Morning star = a long red candle, a small star, then a long green candle closing above 50% of the first body.
- A doji star (morning doji star) is stronger; in Forex, without gaps, the star's body sits near or below the first close.
- Enter at the third close, stop below the star's low; the stop is wide, so R:R matters.
- Measure the nearest obstacle: swing high, lower high, or a falling moving average.

</KeyTakeaways>
