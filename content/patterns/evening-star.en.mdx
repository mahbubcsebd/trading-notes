On Friday night a perfect evening star completes, so you go short and set your stop. On Monday morning you open the chart and price has opened far above your stop. The stop worked, but not at your price. The loss is not 1R; it is more. This page is the mirror of [Morning Star](/en/patterns/morning-star); the three-candle rule, penetration and the gap explanation are detailed there. Here is only what differs, plus one limit of stops.

## What you will be able to do

- **Recognise** an evening star and an evening doji star.
- **Explain** why a star poking above resistance is **the picture of a false breakout**.
- **Decide** on Friday setups knowing the **weekend gap risk**.

## Why it matters

- **Dojis at tops matter more.** Per Nison's observation the evening doji star is a strong signal, because a doji carries more weight at a top.
- **The star often peeks above resistance.** The third candle turns that peek into a false breakout.
- **A stop is not a guarantee.** A stop is an order that fills at the next available price. Over a weekend or on big news that price can be much worse. More in [Slippage and Spread Widening](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/04-slippage-requote-spread-widening).

Prerequisite: the Morning Star page (linked above).

## What is an evening star

**An evening star is a three-candle pattern: a long bullish candle in an uptrend, then a small-body star, then a long bearish candle that closes below the middle of the first body.** If the star is a doji, it is an **evening doji star**.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                                                   | Required? | Why                                           |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| An uptrend before it; the first candle long and bullish                | Yes       | Buyers' last strong push                      |
| The second candle's body small, near or above the first close          | Yes       | The picture of momentum stalling              |
| The third candle long and bearish, closing below 50% of the first body | Yes       | Sellers' win                                  |
| At a marked resistance                                                 | Yes       | Without a level it is a dip inside an uptrend |
| The star is a doji (evening doji star), peeking above resistance       | No        | An even clearer picture                       |

| :------------ | :--------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |

| Star          | A doji or very small                           | A medium body                          |

| Timing        | Midweek, no big news                           | Friday's close, with the weekend ahead |

## The story inside the candle

The morning star story in reverse. One common interpretation: in the first candle buyers lift hard. In the star, price peeks above resistance but closes with a small body: buyers have stalled. In the third candle sellers take back more than half of the first candle. Those who bought the star thinking it was a breakout are now losing.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show who
  bought or sold.
</Callout>

## Gaps in a 24-hour market

<Callout type="note">
  As with the morning star: Forex rarely gaps around the star, so the star's
  body should sit near or above the first candle's close, ideally with the
  star's high above the first high. **But there is one exception: the weekend.**
  The Forex market closes on Friday and reopens on Monday (early morning in
  Asia). If big news arrives in between, Monday's open can be far from Friday's
  close.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading an evening star

The full steps are on the Morning Star page, reversed. Only what differs:

<Steps>

### Step 1: Entry and stop

Sell at the third candle's close, or with a sell stop below its low. Stop above the star's high, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap).

### Step 2: Obstacles below

The prior swing low, a higher low, a rising moving average. Keep the [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) to the nearest one at least 1:1.5.

### Step 3: Think differently on a Friday

If the pattern completes at Friday's close, there are three reasonable paths: (a) enter with a reduced size, so even a gap stays manageable; (b) wait for Monday's open and then decide; (c) skip the trade. Entering at full size thinking "the stop is there" is a fourth path, and the worst.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-evening-star-pretrade-en"
  items={[
    "Was resistance marked before the evening star, and is the star at that level?",
    "Does the shape pass the rules (long green, small star, third candle below 50% of the first body)?",
    "Have all three candles closed, and have you weighed the weekend or big-news gap risk?",
    "Is the risk reward to the nearest obstacle at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is the higher-timeframe trend or level not against the trade?",
  ]}
/>

If any box stays empty, skip the trade.

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="evening-star-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="USDJPY D1 (illustrative, synthetic) — an evening doji star at an old high; short at the third candle's close."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candle 5 left an old high at 149.43. Candles 6-10 fell to about 147.78, and 11-14 rallied again.
- **The evening star:** Candle 15 was a long green candle (148.88 to 149.37). Candle 16 was a doji (149.37 to 149.365) with a high of 149.53, above the old high. Candle 17 closed at 149.027: about 70% of the first body.
- **Decision:** Sell at the third close at 149.027. Stop 149.59 (above the star's high): about 56 pips of risk. Target 147.901 (2R); room to the swing low was about 2.2R. It was midweek.
- **Result:** Candle 24 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** A doji star above the level, a deep third candle, and open space below.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="evening-star-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="EURUSD D1 (illustrative, synthetic) — an evening star completed on Friday; Monday gaps over the stop and the loss is about 1.4R."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-6 rallied from 1.0800 to about 1.0943. There was no marked resistance.
- **The evening star:** Candle 7 (Wednesday) was a long green candle, 1.0943 to 1.0991. Candle 8 (Thursday) was a small star, high 1.1005. Candle 9 (Friday) closed at 1.09612: about 62% of the first body.
- **Decision:** Sell at Friday's close at full size at 1.09612, stop 1.1011: about 50 pips of risk.
- **What happened:** News moved the market over the weekend. Candle 10 (Monday) opened at 1.1029, 18 pips above the stop. The stop filled at the first available price: a loss of about 1.36R.
- **Why it failed:** No marked resistance (first box), and the weekend gap risk was not weighed (third box). The pattern itself was a perfect shape.

**Lesson:** A stop is an order, not a guarantee. On Friday setups, reduce size or wait for Monday.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Why it happens                      | What to do instead                 |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- |
| Treating the stop as a guaranteed maximum loss | Not knowing how orders fill         | Allow for gaps and slippage        |
| Full size at Friday's close                    | Ignoring weekend risk               | Reduce size or watch Monday's open |
| Shorting mid-uptrend without a level           | Impressed by the three-candle shape | Only at marked resistance          |
| Stop at the third candle's high                | Wanting a small stop                | Above the star's high              |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  Know what your broker does at weekends. Many brokers raise margin on Friday
  afternoon, and spreads are very wide in the first minutes after the Monday
  open. More in [Trading
  Sessions](/en/learn/01-foundations/05-trading-sessions).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Even midweek, big news (rate decisions, NFP) can bring gaps or heavy slippage.
  However perfect the pattern, think separately about the risk of a trade left
  open into a major release. More in [Avoiding News
  Trades](/en/learn/11-fundamental-intermarket-analysis/06-news-trade-avoid-korbo).
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Evening star = a long green candle, a small star, then a long red candle closing below 50% of the first body.
- The signal is clearest when the star peeks above resistance and returns, and when it is a doji.
- Enter at the third close, stop above the star's high.
- A stop is not a guarantee: handle weekend and big-news gap risk through size.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice and your own backtest

Open USDJPY D1 or EURUSD D1 in TradingView. Find at least 20 evening stars. For each, log: date and weekday, whether it was at resistance, whether it was a doji star, the third candle's penetration %, and result (R). Separately, check how far Monday's open was for setups that completed on a Friday. Do not draw conclusions from fewer than 30 samples.

## Quiz

1. Why is an evening doji star treated as stronger than a plain evening star?
2. The stop is at 1.1011, and on Monday price opens at 1.1029. Where does the short's stop fill?
3. A perfect evening star at Friday's close. What are the three reasonable paths?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** A doji carries more weight at a top (Nison's observation), and a doji star shows buyers stalling more clearly.

**2.** At about 1.1029, the first available price. A stop order does not guarantee a price.

**3.** Enter with a reduced size, wait for Monday's open before entering, or skip the trade.

</details>
