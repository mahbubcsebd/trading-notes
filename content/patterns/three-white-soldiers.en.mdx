Three big green candles in a row, each closing higher than the last. It feels like a train leaving the station, so you buy at the third candle's close. What you missed: each candle was smaller than the one before and the upper wicks were growing. The very next candle reversed. Three white soldiers picture strength, but a march of tired soldiers looks the same from a distance.

## At a glance

| Item          | Three White Soldiers                                                                                                           |
| :------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type          | Bullish reversal or breakout momentum, three candles                                                                           |
| Where to look | After a downtrend or base, at the moment a range breaks                                                                        |
| Key features  | Three long bullish candles, each opening within or at the prior body's close, closing near its high, each close above the last |
| Confirmation  | The three candles are the confirmation; afterwards, pullbacks hold the broken level                                            |
| Stop          | Below the second candle's open (medium) or below the first candle's low (conservative)                                         |
| Invalidation  | A close below the second candle's open                                                                                         |
| Look-alikes   | Advance block (the tired form), stalled pattern, a run of [Marubozu](/en/patterns/marubozu) candles                            |
| Opposite      | [Three Black Crows](/en/patterns/three-black-crows)                                                                            |

## What you will be able to do

- **Separate** real three white soldiers from an **advance block**.
- **Enter on a pullback** instead of chasing three big candles.
- **Choose** between two stop types knowing the difference.

## Why it matters

- **The clearest three-candle picture of momentum.** For three sessions buyers stay in control, closing higher each time.
- **The biggest temptation to chase.** After three big candles price has already risen a lot; entering there means a huge stop and a poor R:R.
- **The tired version looks the same.** Nison shows two warning forms: the advance block and the stalled pattern. Both look like soldiers but lean the other way.

Prerequisites: the Marubozu page (linked in the table above) and [Range Breakout](/en/learn/06-trend-chart-patterns/03-range-breakout).

## What are three white soldiers

**Three white soldiers are three consecutive long bullish candles, each opening within or at the prior candle's body, closing near its own high, with each close higher than the last.** "White" comes from the white (bullish) candles on Japanese charts.

Picture a column of soldiers advancing in three ranks. Each rank starts just behind the previous one and stops further ahead. Nobody falls back.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                              | Required?          | Why                                                          |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :----------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Three consecutive long bullish candles            | Yes                | These are the "three soldiers"                               |
| Each close above the previous one                 | Yes                | Steady progress                                              |
| Each closes near its high (small upper wick)      | Yes                | A long upper wick means sellers above                        |
| Each opens within or at the prior body's close    | Yes                | In Forex the open is usually at the prior close              |
| Bodies not shrinking                              | Yes                | Shrinking bodies make it an advance block or stalled pattern |
| A downtrend or base before it, and a level broken | No (but important) | At the end of a long rally the same shape can be exhaustion  |

### Same look, different meaning

| Pattern                  | Shape                                                      | Meaning                             |
| :----------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| **Three White Soldiers** | Three equally large green candles, small wicks             | Strong momentum                     |
| **Advance Block**        | Three green candles, bodies shrinking, upper wicks growing | Nison: buyers are tiring; a warning |
| **Stalled Pattern**      | Two large green candles, the third small (often star-like) | Nison: momentum has stalled         |
| **Marubozu**             | One large, wickless candle                                 | One candle of momentum              |

## The story inside the candle

One common interpretation of three white soldiers after a base:

1. **First soldier: buyers try to break the base.** A big green candle closing near its high.
2. **Second soldier: the effort continues.** Starting from the prior close and going higher. Shorts begin to exit.
3. **Third soldier: confirmed.** Another higher close with no long wick. Sellers could not push price back once in three candles.

But when bodies shrink and wicks grow, the story changes: at each new high, sellers push back more.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show who
  bought or sold.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading three white soldiers

<Steps>

### Step 1: Context first

Was there a downtrend or base before? Did the soldiers break a level? How far is the next resistance ahead?

### Step 2: Wait for all three candles to close

The third candle can grow a long upper wick in its last minute. Once closed, measure the bodies and wicks.

### Step 3: Check for an advance block

Write down the size of the three bodies. Shrinking? Upper wicks growing? If yes, this is not soldiers but a warning.

### Step 4: Entry type

| Entry                                       | Where                                     | Pro                      | Con                           |
| :------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **50% retest of the third body** (standard) | Buy limit at the middle of the third body | Smaller stop, better R:R | Missed if price never returns |
| **Retest of the broken level**              | Buy limit at the level the soldiers broke | The best price           | Often never comes             |
| **Third close**                             | Buy as the third candle closes            | Never missed             | Huge stop; this is chasing    |

### Step 5: Place the stop

**Medium:** below the second soldier's open. **Conservative:** below the first soldier's low (much wider). Either way, add the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) and a buffer.

### Step 6: Size and target

[Position size](/en/learn/08-risk-management/02-position-sizing) from the entry-to-stop distance, risking no more than 1%. Target before the next resistance, with a [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) of at least 1:1.5.

### Step 7: Manage

A close below the second soldier's open means momentum has broken: get out. If the trend runs, consider a [trailing stop](/en/learn/10-trade-management-execution/03-trailing-stop).

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-three-white-soldiers-pretrade-en"

    "Is the risk reward to the next resistance at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is there no higher-timeframe level right ahead?",
  ]}

If any box stays empty, skip the trade.

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="three-white-soldiers-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="GBPUSD H4 (illustrative, synthetic) — three white soldiers out of a flat base; entry on a pullback to 50% of the third body."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-3 fell from 1.2700, and candles 4-11 formed a flat base from about 1.2613 to 1.2636.
- **The soldiers:** Candle 12 ran 1.2626 to 1.2654, candle 13 1.2654 to 1.2688, candle 14 1.2688 to 1.2720. Bodies of 28, 34 and 32 pips, each with an upper wick of only 2 pips. The 1.2636 base high was left well behind.
- **Decision:** No chase: buy limit at 1.2704 (50% of the third body). Stop 1.2650 (below the second soldier's open): 54 pips of risk. Target 1.2812 (2R).
- **Result:** Candle 15 paused and candle 16 touched 1.2704, filling the order. Candle 26 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** Equally strong soldiers out of a base, and a patient entry. Buying the third close would have made the stop about one and a half times larger.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="three-white-soldiers-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="XAUUSD H4 (illustrative, synthetic) — an advance block at an old high; bought at the third close, then stopped out."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candle 5 left an old high near 2387. Candles 6-10 fell.
- **The "soldiers":** Candle 11 had a $14 body, candle 12 $9 (with a $4 upper wick), candle 13 $4 (with a $13 upper wick up to the old high). Bodies shrinking, wicks growing.
- **The wrong decision:** "Three green candles!" Buy at the third close at 2375.05, stop 2345.05 (below the first candle): $30 of risk, target 2435.05.
- **What happened:** Candle 14 reversed lower and candle 16 broke the stop.
- **Why it failed:** This was an advance block, not real soldiers, and right at the old high. The second, third and fifth checklist boxes would have filtered it out.

**Lesson:** Shrinking bodies and growing upper wicks turn soldiers into an advance block. Because the stop was set in advance, the loss stayed at 1R.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Why it happens                 | What to do instead                                   |
| :----------------------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Buying the third close in a hurry          | Fear of missing out            | A limit at 50% of the third body or the broken level |
| Calling an advance block soldiers          | Not measuring bodies and wicks | Write down and compare the three bodies and wicks    |
| Buying soldiers at the end of a long rally | Ignoring context               | Check for a base or downtrend before                 |
| Stop at the third candle's low             | Wanting a small stop           | Below the second soldier's open                      |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The first pullback after the soldiers often gives the best entry, because
  momentum is proven and price has come back a little. If the pullback goes
  below 50% of the third body and reaches the second soldier's open, momentum
  has weakened.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Do not accept numbers like "price rises X% after three white soldiers" without
  checking. Figures from stock research do not carry over to Forex directly.
  Your own backtest is the only number you can trust.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Three white soldiers = three consecutive long green candles, each closing higher, near its high, with small wicks.
- Shrinking bodies and growing upper wicks make it an advance block: a warning, not a buy signal.
- Most meaningful after a base or downtrend when a level breaks; at the end of a long rally it can be exhaustion.
- Do not chase the third close: enter at 50% of the third body or the broken level, stop below the second soldier's open.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Quiz

1. What is the difference between three white soldiers and an advance block?
2. Why is entering at the third close after three big green candles risky?
3. Where do the soldiers open in Forex?
4. Which checklist boxes would have filtered out the trade in Example 2?

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

**1.** In soldiers the three bodies stay about equal or grow, with small wicks. In an advance block the bodies shrink and the upper wicks grow.

**2.** Price has already risen a lot; the stop is far away, the R:R is poor, and the first pullback puts pressure on the trade.

**3.** Usually at the previous candle's close, because a 24-hour market rarely gaps. That is how Nison's "opens within the prior body" rule is met in Forex.

**4.** The second box (bodies were shrinking), the third box (chasing the third close) and the fifth box (the old high right ahead).
