A candle at the top of an uptrend: a small body on top and a long lower wick. It looks exactly like a [Hammer](/en/patterns/hammer), so some traders buy it. Others shout "hanging man!" and sell at once, and the trend keeps climbing. Both are wrong. A hanging man's meaning depends entirely on the next candle. This is the hammer's mirror page, so only what differs is detailed here.

## At a glance

| Item          | Hanging Man                                                                                      |
| :------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type          | Bearish reversal warning, one candle                                                             |
| Where to look | At the top of an uptrend, at a marked resistance                                                 |
| Key features  | Small body on top, lower wick at least twice the body, almost no upper wick                      |
| Confirmation  | Next candle closes **below the hanging man's body** (Nison's key condition)                      |
| Stop          | Above the hanging man high, plus spread and a buffer                                             |
| Invalidation  | A close above the hanging man high                                                               |
| Look-alikes   | Hammer (same shape, at the bottom of a downtrend), [Dragonfly Doji](/en/patterns/dragonfly-doji) |
| Opposite      | [Shooting Star](/en/patterns/shooting-star) (long upper wick at a top)                           |

## What you will be able to do

- **Tell** by location why the same shape can be a hammer or a hanging man.
- **Measure** what a bearish confirmation after a hanging man looks like.
- **Place** an entry on the confirming close and a stop above the high.

## Why it matters

- **One shape, two names.** The shape says nothing by itself; where it forms in the trend gives the name. It is the clearest example of [Candle Context](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/05-candle-context).
- **Confirmation is everything.** Nison writes that bearish confirmation after a hanging man, especially a close below its body, matters even more than after a hammer.
- **A trap both ways.** Buying it at the top is a trap, and selling it without confirmation is a trap too.

Prerequisites: the Hammer page (linked above) and [Single Candle Patterns](/en/learn/04-candlesticks/02-single-candle-patterns).

## What is a hanging man

**A hanging man is a candle at the top of an uptrend with a small body in the upper part of the candle and a lower wick at least twice the length of the body.** The name comes from the picture of a hanged man: the head (body) on top, the legs (wick) dangling below.

Picture someone standing on a high wall who suddenly slips and hangs from the edge, then manages to climb back up. They are still on top, but everyone has now seen they can fall.

### Identification rules

| Rule                                                | Required?      | Why                                                  |
| :-------------------------------------------------- | :------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| Small body in the upper part of the candle          | Yes            | A body in the middle makes it a spinning top         |
| Lower wick at least twice the body                  | Yes            | A short wick carries no "it could fall" story        |
| No upper wick, or a very small one                  | Yes            | A long upper wick is a different shape               |
| A clear uptrend before it, near a marked resistance | Yes            | This is what separates it from a hammer              |
| Next candle closes below the body                   | Yes (to trade) | Without confirmation a hanging man is only a warning |

Body colour (green or red) is not in the rules. Some treat a red body as slightly more bearish, but that matters far less than confirmation.

| Quality check | Strong                               | Weak                            |
| :------------ | :----------------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| Where         | At a marked old high or supply zone  | Mid-trend, no level             |
| Lower wick    | 3-5× the body, like nearby ranges    | Just 2× or less                 |
| Next candle   | Closes clearly below the body        | Closes above or inside the body |
| Room below    | At least 1.5R to the nearest support | Nearest higher low very close   |

## The story inside the candle

One common interpretation: buyers are in control of the uptrend. In this candle sellers suddenly manage to push price far down, which they could not do in previous candles. Buyers bring it back to close high, so the shape looks "strong". But the news is: **sellers have woken up.** If the next candle closes below the body, those who bought late are now losing, and their exits can add to fresh selling.

<Callout type="note">
  This is a common interpretation, not a proven fact. A chart does not show who
  bought or sold.
</Callout>

## Step by step: trading a hanging man

The full steps for context, size and target are on the Hammer page; here the direction is reversed. Only what differs:

<Steps>

### Step 1: Measure the confirmation

The next candle has to close **below the hanging man's body**, not just wick below its low. The lower the close, the cleaner.

### Step 2: Entry

| Entry                           | Where                                           | Pro                     | Con                                      |
| :------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| **Confirming close** (standard) | Sell when the next candle closes below the body | Meets Nison's condition | Lower entry, slightly bigger stop        |
| **Low break**                   | Sell stop below the hanging man low             | Momentum confirmed      | Stop beyond the whole candle: wide       |
| **At the hanging man close**    | Sell as the candle closes                       | Small stop              | No confirmation; the trend often resumes |

### Step 3: Stop

Above the hanging man high, plus the [spread](/en/learn/01-foundations/04-spread-commission-swap) and a buffer. The reverse of the hammer: here the stop sits on the short-wick side, so with a confirming-close entry it is usually manageable.

### Step 4: R:R

If the [risk reward](/en/learn/08-risk-management/03-risk-reward) to the nearest support or higher low below is under 1:1.5, skip it.

</Steps>

### Pre-trade checklist

<Checklist
  id="pattern-hanging-man-pretrade-en"
  items={[
    "Was resistance marked before the hanging man, and is it at that level at the top of an uptrend?",
    "Does the shape pass the rules (small body on top, lower wick at least twice the body)?",
    "Has the next candle closed, and did it close below the hanging man's body?",
    "Is the risk reward to the nearest support below at least 1:1.5?",
    "Is there no higher-timeframe level right in front of the trade?",
  ]}
/>

If any box stays empty, skip the trade.

## Example 1: when it works (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="hanging-man-win-01"
  height={420}
  caption="XAUUSD H4 (illustrative, synthetic) — a hanging man under an old high; short when the next candle closes below its body."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candle 5 left an old high at 2449.92. Candles 6-10 fell to about 2398, and 11-15 rallied back under the high.
- **The hanging man:** Candle 16 opened at 2445.92, closed at 2448.52, high 2449.12. The lower wick reached 2429.92: about $16, six times the body.
- **Confirmation:** Candle 17 closed at 2439.42, about $6.50 below the body.
- **Decision:** Sell on that close at 2439.42. Stop 2451.62 (above both the hanging man high and the old high): $12.20 of risk. Target 2415.02 (2R); the earlier pullback low near 2398 left plenty of room.
- **Result:** Candle 19 reached the target.
- **Lesson:** A level, the shape, and a clear close below the body: all three lined up.

## Example 2: when it is a trap (illustrative)

<Chart
  id="hanging-man-fail-01"
  height={420}
  caption="USDJPY H1 (illustrative, synthetic) — a hanging man in a steady uptrend, shorted at its close; no confirmation, stopped out."
/>

- **What was visible:** Candles 0-8 were a steady uptrend from 153.40 to 154.05. No marked resistance above.
- **The hanging man:** Candle 9 opened at 154.050, closed at 154.155, high 154.175, low 153.830. The shape was fine.
- **The wrong decision:** "Hanging man, sell now." Sell at the close at 154.155, stop 154.215: only 6 pips of risk. The small stop looked attractive.
- **What happened:** Candle 10 closed above the body, at 154.215, not below it, and the same candle touched the stop. The trend carried on.
- **Why it failed:** There was no level, and no wait for confirmation. The first and third checklist boxes would have filtered it out.

**Lesson:** No bearish close below the body, no trade. A small stop is not an edge.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Why it happens           | What to do instead                               |
| :-------------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| Buying a hammer shape at the top              | Naming by shape          | Location in the trend first, then the name       |
| Selling at the hanging man close              | Wanting a small stop     | Wait for the next candle to close below the body |
| Counting a wick below the low as confirmation | Mixing up wick and close | Only count a close below the body                |
| Shorting every hanging man mid-trend          | Not checking for a level | Only at marked resistance                        |

## Pro tips

<Callout type="tip">
  The clearest confirmation is a next candle that closes far below the body with
  a large bearish body or a gap. Forex rarely gaps, so the distance of the close
  below the body is the real measure.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  In an open long, a hanging man at the top lets you raise your stop to just
  below the hanging man low without opening a new short. If confirmation comes
  you are out; if not, the trade runs.
</Callout>

## Summary

<KeyTakeaways>

- Hanging man = the hammer shape, but at the top of an uptrend; location gives the name.
- It is a warning, not a signal: no trade unless the next candle closes below the body.
- Enter on the confirming close; stop above the hanging man high.
- Skip it unless there is at least 1.5R to the nearest support below.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Practice and your own backtest

Open XAUUSD H4 or USDJPY H1 in TradingView. Find at least 20 hanging men. For each, log: date, whether it was at resistance, whether the next candle closed below the body, entry type, R to the nearest support, and result (R). Then split into "confirmed" and "not confirmed" and see what shorting without confirmation would have done. Do not draw conclusions from fewer than 30 samples.

**1.** At the bottom of a downtrend it is a hammer; at the top of an uptrend it is a hanging man. Same shape, different place.
