## The idea

An **order block** is the last opposing candle before a strong, impulsive move. In a bullish move it is the last down candle before price rallies and breaks structure. The theory is that large orders were placed there and some may still be unfilled, so price can react when it returns.

> **Key idea:** an order block is a zone you plan to react to, not a guarantee. Without a reason for price to come back, it is just a rectangle on a chart.

## What makes one worth marking

- It led to a **break of structure**, not just a small bounce.
- It sits at a sensible place: a discount for longs, a premium for shorts.
- It took liquidity first (a sweep of a nearby low or high).
- It has not already been traded through.

## How I mark it

1. Find the impulsive move that broke structure.
2. Go back to the last opposing candle before that move.
3. Draw the zone from the candle high to low, or from the open to the low if you prefer the body.
4. Wait for price to return. No return, no trade.

## Common mistakes

- Marking every opposing candle on the chart.
- Entering at the zone with no confirmation on a lower timeframe.
- Ignoring the higher timeframe direction.

## Checklist before entry

- [ ] Higher timeframe bias agrees
- [ ] Liquidity was taken
- [ ] Zone is untouched
- [ ] Stop is beyond the zone, risk is 1% or less
- [ ] Reward is at least 2R
